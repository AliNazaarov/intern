Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a pole fitness performer, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. 
See section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). 
This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens who can 
demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose 
achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers 
(petition), concluding the Petitioner had not established she had received a major, internationally 
recognized award, and that while she had satisfied three of the regulatory criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), she had not demonstrated her extraordinary ability in a final merits 
determination. We dismissed her appeal, highlighting procedural issues and withdrawing SCOPS' 
determination that the Petitioner met the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i), and a subsequent 
combined motions to reopen and to reconsider. The matter is before us again on combined motions 
to reopen and to reconsider. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproofto demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the 
motions. 
A motion to reopen must state new facts and be supported by documentary evidence. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.5(a)(2). A motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision was based on an incorrect 
application of law or policy and that the decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the record of 
proceedings at the time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103 .5(a)(3). Because the scope of a motion is 
limited to the prior decision, we will only review the latest decision in these proceedings. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.5(a)(l)(i), (ii). We may grant motions that satisfy these requirements and demonstrate eligibility 
for the requested benefit. 1 
We dismissed the prior motions to reopen and to reconsider because the new evidence did not address 
the insufficiencies raised in our appeal decision to establish the Petitioner's eligibility and the

In the current motions, the Petitioner highlights previously submitted evidence and submits new 
evidence which she claims demonstrates she satisfies 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i), such as letters of 
support and ._________________. documents, including information on its scoring, 
categories and levels ofcompetition, training, dates ofcompetitions, and social media. She also argues 
that her submitted documents support she is at the top of her endeavor. In our prior decision, we 
concluded that the Petitioner had not provided sufficient evidence that the awards she received have 
national or international recognition. We limit our review of the submitted evidence to this issue. In 
a November 2024 letter, the president of the '---------~recognizes the Petitioner's 
accomplishments but explains that pole and aerial sports are not recognized as national sports in many 
countries, including the United States. Thec=]documents, some of which may be relevant in 
understanding how the organization grants its awards, do not demonstrate national or international 
recognition. In addition, while the documentation on I Isocial media presence evidences the 
organization's tens of thousands of subscribers, it does not demonstrate the international or national 
recognition ofI Iawards. As a result, the Petitioner has not provided new facts to establish she 
satisfies 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) or her eligibility for the visa classification. Because the Petitioner 
has not established new facts that would warrant reopening of the proceeding, we have no basis to 
reopen our prior decision. 
The Petitioner claims we erred in concluding her awards were not nationally or internationally 
recognized by applying a higher standard ofproof. However, she does not provide details or examples 
of how we applied a higher standard. The Petitioner also asse1is that we erred because we did not 
sufficiently weigh the expert letter she submitted and did not conduct a final merits determination in 
our prior decision. However, our prior decision reviewed the letter she submitted, noting it was not 
new to the record and did not provide adequate detail to establish that the awards she received have 
the requisite recognition to qualify under the criterion. Further, as the issue of whether the Petitioner 
satisfied 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) was dispositive of the motion, we did not err in ending our analysis 
there and not re-analyzing the final merits. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) ("courts 
and agencies are not required to make findings on issues the decision of which is unnecessary to the 
results they reach"). Therefore, the Petitioner has not established that our previous decision was based 
on an incorrect application of law or policy at the time we issued our decision. 
For the foregoing reasons, the underlying petition remains denied. 
ORDER: The motion to reopen is dismissed. 
FURTHER ORDER: The motion to reconsider is dismissed.
