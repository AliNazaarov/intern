Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a general manager, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability in business. 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b )(1 )(A), 8 U.S .C. § 1153(b )(1 )(A). This first 
preference (EB-1) classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
SCOPS denied the petition, concluding the Petitioner did not establish that he meets the classification's 
initial evidence requirements by documenting that he satisfies at least three of the ten evidentiary 
criteria set forth in the regulations. The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
An alien is eligible for the extraordinary ability classification if they have extraordinary ability in the 
sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained national or 
international acclaim and their achievements have been recognized in the field through extensive 
documentation; they seek to enter the United States to continue work in the area of extraordinary 
ability; and their entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively the United States. 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate a one­
time achievement (that is, a major, internationally recognized award). If the petitioner does not submit 
this evidence, then they must provide documentation that meets at least three of the ten categories 
listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) - (x) (including items such as awards, published material in certain 
media, and scholarly articles). The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4) allows a petitioner to submit 
comparable material if they are able to demonstrate that the standards at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) 
do not readily apply to their occupation. 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1119-20 (9th Cir. 2010); 
see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 
2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner seeks EB-1 classification based upon his "past and continuing achievements as an 
entrepreneur and CEO in China's building materials industry, especially in the interdisciplinary areas 
of resilient floors and new decorative building materials." He indicates that he intends to start his 
own company in this field in the United States. 
Because the Petitioner has not indicated or established that he has received a major, internationally 
recognized award, he must demonstrate that he satisfies at least three ofthe alternate regulatory criteria 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). The Petitioner has consistently claimed that he meets four of the ten 
criteria. 
SCOPS concluded that the Petitioner provided sufficient evidence to meet two of these four 
evidentiary criteria: (1) Published material about the Petitioner in professional or major trade 
publications or other major media under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii) and (2) performing in a lead or 
critical role for organizations with distinguished reputations under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii). 
Because the evidence did not satisfy at least three of the ten initial criteria, SCOPS denied the petition 
on that basis and did not proceed to a final merits determination. 
The issue on appeal is whether the Petitioner has provided evidence that meets either of the other two 
criteria he has claimed: (1) receipt of a lesser nationally or internationally recognized prize or award 
under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i); or (2) original contributions of major significance to his field under 8 
C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v). Upon review, we conclude that the evidence does not overcome SCOPS' 
denial, for the reasons below. 1

III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner has not overcome SCOPS' stated grounds for denial and established that he meets at 
least three of the initial evidentiary criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). Accordingly, he has not 
demonstrated his eligibility as an individual of extraordinary ability, and we need not provide the type 
of final merits determination referenced in Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20. The petition will remain 
denied. 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
