Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an engineer specializing in software quality assurance and project management, seeks 
classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) 
section 203(b )(1 )(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b )(1 )(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant 
visas available to those who can demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or 
international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive 
documentation. 
SCOPS denied the petition, concluding that while the record showed that the Petitioner met the initial 
evidence requirement for this classification by meeting at least three of the evidentiary criteria under 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), it did not establish that he possessed the requisite sustained national or 
international acclaim to demonstrate his eligibility as an individual ofextraordinary ability. The matter 
is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F .R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 l&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
An individual is eligible for the extraordinary ability classification if they have extraordinary ability 
in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained 
national or international acclaim and their achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation; they seek to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability; and their entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively the 
United States. Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner may demonstrate 
international recognition of their achievements in the field through a one-time achievement (that is, a 
major, internationally recognized award). Absent such an achievement, a petitioner must provide 
sufficient qualifying documentation demonstrating that they meet at least three ofthe ten criteria listed 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination, assessing whether the record shows that the 
individual possesses the acclaim and recognition required for this highly exclusive immigrant visa 
classification. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) (discussing a two-part review 
where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the required number of criteria, 
considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 
126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011). 
TI. ANALYSIS 
A. Evidentiary Criteria 
The Petitioner works as senior quality assurance manager for a company specializing in solar energy 
and battery storage systems. He intends to continue his work in the field of software development in 
the United States. 
SCOPS concluded that the Petitioner met three of the ten evidentiary criteria: 
• 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv), participation, either individually or on a panel, as a judge 
of the work of others in the same or an allied field of specialization for which 
classification is sought; 
• 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi), authorship of scholarly articles in the field, in professional 
or major trade publications or other major media; and 
• 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii), performance in a leading or critical role for organizations 
or establishments that have a distinguished reputation. 
On appeal, the Petitioner asserts that he also meets the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix), which 
pertains to having commanded a high salary or other significantly high remuneration for services 
compared with others in his field. Because SCOPS determined that the Petitioner met at least three of 
the evidentiary criteria required to establish the initial evidentiary requirements for classification as an 
individual of extraordinary ability, we will not address the evidence of his salary separately in our 
decision, but will consider it as part of the totality of the record in the final merits determination. 
B. Final Merits Determination 
As noted above, once an individual has established that they meet the initial evidence requirement, we 
conduct a final merits determination. In a final merits determination, we examine and weigh the 
totality of the evidence to determine whether an individual has demonstrated sustained national or 
international acclaim, that the individual is one of the small percentage at the very top of their field of 
endeavor, and that their achievements have been recognized in the field through extensive 
documentation.

With respect to the Petitioner's experience judging the work of others in his field, as defined at 8 
C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv), the record does not demonstrate that he is among the small percentage at the 
top of his field. The Petitioner submitted evidence of his participation as a judge on several occasions

Regarding the Petitioner's authorship of scholarly articles in the field, in professional or major trade 
publications or other major media, as defined at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi), the record includes 
evidence of his publication of two articles in 2023 on DZone.com, an online forum for a community 
of software professionals and developers. The record contains documentation showing that DZone is 
a free publishing platform that serves readership industrywide as a knowledge resource. A printout 
from the publication's website at https://dzone.com/articles/dzones-article-submission-guidelines 
shows that submissions are subject to review and technical feedback, stating that "all articles submitted 
to DZone must go through our moderation and will be edited by [DZone's] team of editors." We 
observe that although the Petitioner refers to this review as a "rigorous evaluation by experts in the 
field," the record does not include information identifying the qualifications of the editorial team or 
what specific standards are necessary for publication. While publication to a widely read forum in the 
Petitioner's field demonstrates that he has met the plain language definition of the criterion, it does 
not provide persuasive support to demonstrate the extraordinary ability of an acclaimed individual at 
the top of his field. The record, for example, does not contain documentation indicating that the 
requirements for publication extend beyond an article's technical accuracy in the opinion of an 
editorial team that-as the Petitioner states-publishes the work of "thousands of contributors," nor 
does it include evidence to show that accepted submissions regularly consist of those authored by 
experts in the field, as opposed to individuals with basic knowledge of software. The publication of 
two articles on a free publishing platform-even if vetted through an alleged peer-review process-is 
not indicative of the publishing history of an individual whose accomplishments are among those at 
the top of his field, and the fact that his publication history is limited to two articles in single year does 
not indicate sustained acclaim as an individual at the top of his field. 
Concerning the Petitioner's performance in a leading or critical role for organizations or 
establishments that have a distinguished reputation, as defined at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii), he 
submitted letters from his current and previous employers, which include major technology 
companies. A letter from his current employer details his contributions leading a team that 
"revolutionized [the company's] approach to software testing," by developing an "efficiency 
enhancement [that] translates into substantial cost savings, amounting to approximately $576,000 
annually ...." Another letter from his current employer describes his leadership role as a strategic 
influencer in vendor selection for tools and staffing. Letters from previous employers discuss the 
Petitioner's critical roles in the success of projects. For example, one states that "he was the linchpin 
ensuring the software's quality and reliability, elements that formed the bedrock of the client's 
business operations and, by extension, customer satisfaction." A letter from another previous

employer generally characterizes the Petitioner's "contributions" as "transformative," stating that "his 
keen domain knowledge enabled us to streamline complex trade finance activities on a global scale, 
driving down costs and enhancing efficiency." However, while the record demonstrates that the 
Petitioner's work has created positive impacts for his immediate employers and their clients, the record

Finally, with regard to whether the Petitioner commanded a high salary or other significantly high 
remuneration for services compared with others in his field, as defined at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix), 
the record includes documentation of his earnings and information from credible online sources for 
salaries earned in the same year of the Petitioner's employment and in the same geographic location 
where the Petitioner was employed. This information is for positions that most closely resemble the 
Petitioner's position for which he claims high remuneration. The evidence demonstrates that the 
Petitioner was among the highest earners of others in similar positions in his field for the year 2022. 
The evidence of the Petitioner's high earnings in a single year, however-when considered alongside 
his limited participation as a judge, his authorship of two peer-reviewed articles, and the remaining 
evidence of record-does not support his assertion that he is among the small percentage who has 
risen to the top of his field of endeavor. While the Petitioner may have performed at a highly 
satisfactory level for his employers, taken together with his brief history judging the work of others 
and his limited authorship experience on a free online platform, the totality of the evidence does not 
demonstrate that the Petitioner has garnered a level of recognition for expertise in his field that would 
be expected from an individual at the top of his field. And while the Petitioner emphasized his length 
of time working in his field, the record shows that the criteria he highlighted on the filing date of his 
petition concerning his participation as a judge, his authorship of articles, and his high earnings 
occurred within a single year; this limited period is insufficient-as required by the Act and pertinent 
regulations-to demonstrate sustained acclaim in order to qualify for an immigrant visa under this 
classification. The record, as a whole, does not demonstrate eligibility for the benefit sought. 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner seeks a highly restrictive visa classification, intended for individuals already at the top 
of their respective fields, rather than for those progressing toward the top. USCIS has long held that 
even athletes performing at the major league level do not automatically meet the "extraordinary 
ability" standard. Matter ofPrice, 20 T&N Dec. 953,954 (Assoc. Comm'r 1994). Here, the Petitioner 
has not shown that the significance of his work is indicative of the required sustained national or 
international acclaim or that it is consistent with a "career of acclaimed work in the field" as 
contemplated by Congress. H.R. Rep. No. lO1-723, 59 (Sept. 19, 1990); see also section 203(b )( 1 )(A) 
of the Act. The record does not otherwise demonstrate that the Petitioner has garnered sustained 
national or international acclaim in the field and that he is one of the small percentage who have risen 
to the very top of his field. See section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act and 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). We 
therefore conclude that the Petitioner has not established his eligibility as an individual of 
extraordinary ability.
