Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a project management specialist, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary 
ability. Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). 
This fust preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Service Center Operations (SCOPS) 
denied the petition, concluding that the record did not satisfy at least three of the ten initial evidentiary 
criteria as set forth in 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). We dismissed a subsequent appeal. This matter 
is now before us on a motion to reopen. Upon review, we will dismiss the motion. 
A motion to reopen must state the new facts to be provided in the reopened proceeding and be 
supported by affidavits or other documentary evidence. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2). Our review on motion 
is limited to our latest decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(l)(ii). We may grant a motion that meets these 
requirements and establishes eligibility for the benefit sought. Matter ofCoelho, 20 I&N Dec. 464, 
473 (BIA 1992) (holding that a party seeking reopening bears a heavy burden ofproving that the new 
evidence would likely change the outcome). The Petitioner must establish his eligibility by a 
preponderance of the evidence. Matter ofChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act makes visas available to immigrants with extraordinary ability if: 
(i) the alien has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has 
been demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been 
recognized in the field through extensive documentation; (ii) the alien seeks to enter this country to 
continue work in the area of extraordinary ability; and (iii) the alien's entry will substantially benefit 
prospectively the United States. 
"Extraordinary ability" refers only to those in "that small percentage who have risen to the very top of 
the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a 
two-part analysis. First, an alien can show sustained acclaim and the recognition of achievements in 
the field through a one-time achievement (that is, a major, internationally recognized award) or 
qualifying documentation that satisfies at least three of the ten criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) 
(including items such as awards, published material in certain media, and scholarly articles). 1 
1 The Petitioner did not claim, and the record does not show, that he has a one-time achievement. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial regulatory evidence requirements, we then consider the totality 
of the material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record demonstrates 
sustained national or international acclaim and the individual is among the small percentage at the 
very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) (discussing 
a two-part analysis, first determining whether the documentation satisfies the required number of 
regulatory criteria and then conducting a final merits determination). 
The record reflects that the Petitioner claimed eligibility for three regulatory criteria before SCOPS: 
that he has received a lesser nationally or internationally recognized award for excellence in his field; 
made original contributions of major significance in the field; and performed a leading or critical role 
for an organization with a distinguished reputation. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i), (v), and (viii). SCOPS 
concluded that the Petitioner's documents did not satisfy any of the three regulatory criteria. 
In our decision dismissing the appeal, we determined that the record lacked evidence ofthe Petitioner's 
"receipt of lesser nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards for excellence in the field 
of endeavor," and it thus did not satisfy the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i). We considered the 
only documentary evidence of what he claimed was an award, namely a 2022 "Letter of Recognition" 
from a mayor of a city I Iin Kazakhstan for the Petitioner's services overseeing the safety of a 
hospital during unrest and other documents (such as support letters praising his work performance), 
and concluded that the record did not satisfy the criterion because the Petitioner did not provide any 
probative evidence that the receipt ofthe mayoral letter was "nationally or internationally recognized" 
as regulation requires. 2 Id. Because the Petitioner's inability to meet this initial evidentiary threshold 
was dispositive ofthe appeal ( as it meant he could not meet at least three of the ten regulatory criteria), 
we did not reach arguments as to the remaining two criteria he also claimed, and dismissed the appeal. 
On motion, he submits a 2022 sunqarnews.com online article written by A-A-3 praising the Petitioner's 
work ethic, services, and receipt of the mayor's "letter of gratitude" for "conscientious performance 
of his duties and selflessness" during the January 2022 unrest, and stating that the letter shows the 
mayor was "very pleased with [ the Petitioner]. "4 He also submits evidence that this same online article 
was posted on three other online outlets, comprising qazaqscience.kz, jarshy.com, jurtmedia.kz, and 
asserts that the mayoral letter, particularly when considered with the above online coverage of it, now 
satisfies the evidentiary criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i). We disagree. 
The recognition letter "express[es]" the then city mayor's "gratitude to [the Petitioner] for the 
conscientious performance of [his] official duties and selflessness" during the January 2022 events 
and wishes him and his family's wellbeing, health, and future success in his work. As we noted in our 
prior decision dismissing the appeal, the Petitioner did not claim or provide any probative evidence 
(such as evidence that this correspondence was publicly awarded or reported on) that may inform the 
extent of its claimed recognition beyond the two individuals. While we acknowledge that the letter 
was written, signed, and given by an official of a large city for the Petitioner's job performance during

The Petitioner's counsel in the motion brief asserts that the mayoral letter and the Petitioner's related 
accomplishments "have been widely reported in national and independent Kazakhstani media outlet" 
and the above-referenced 2022 article published by four different websites establishes that he received 
a formal award of national recognition for excellence. However, the Petitioner does not identify or 
submit any relevant probative evidence that any of the websites that posted the same article was a 
national media outlet or that his story was in fact "widely reported." Counsel's assertions are not 
evidence. See, e.g., Matter ofObaigbena, 19 I&N Dec. 533, 534 n.2 (BIA 1988) (citation omitted). 
Counsel's statements must be supported with independent evidence, which is deficient here. Even if 
the websites were national outlets, such claimed online media stature does not necessarily prove that 
a mayor's letter of appreciation is inherently nationally recognized such that it may satisfy the 
evidentiary criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i). Further, the 2022 article itself does not address the 
claimed national recognition or reputation of such letter. He does not otherwise provide any relevant 
new assertion or document, and the evidence on motion therefore does not show that the mayoral letter 
constitutes a nationally or internationally recognized award. Thus, the Petitioner has not overcome 
our prior determination that the evidence of record did not satisfy the dispositive regulatory criterion 
as set forth in 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) to warrant reopening. 5 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2). 
ORDER: The motion to reopen is dismissed.
