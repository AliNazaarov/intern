Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an economist, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. 
See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the record did not establish 
that the Petitioner met any of the initial evidentiary criteria. The matter is now before us on appeal 
pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 l&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b )(1 )(A) of the Act makes immigrant visas available to individuals with extraordinary 
ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained 
national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, provided that the individual seeks to enter the United States to continue 
work in the area of extraordinary ability, and the individual's entry into the United States will 
substantially benefit prospectively the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, petitioners can demonstrate 
international recognition of their accomplishments in the field through a one-time achievement (that 
is, a major, internationally recognized award). If petitioners do not submit this evidence, then they 
must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the ten criteria listed at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4) allows petitioners to submit 
comparable evidence if they are able to demonstrate that the standards at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)­
(x) do not readily apply to their occupation. 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) 
( discussing a two-part review where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the 
required number of criteria, considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also 
Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 
(W.D. Wash. 2011). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The record indicates the Petitioner has worked as an economist in the public and private sectors in 
Georgia and the United States for nearly ten years. The Petitioner claimed to meet seven of the initial 
evidentiary criteria relating to membership, published material, judging, original contributions, 
authorship, and leading or critical role at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii), (iii), (iv), (v), (vi), (viii). The 
Petitioner also claimed the criterion relating to artistic display at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii) did not 
apply to his occupation and submitted comparable evidence of the display of his work. SCOPS 
determined the Petitioner did not meet any of the six claimed criteria and did not consider his 
comparable evidence. On appeal, the Petitioner asserts SCOPS misapplied the regulation and 
misstated the facts of his petition. 
A. Evidentiary Criteria 
Because the Petitioner has not shown that he received a major, internationally recognized award, he 
must satisfy at least three of the alternate regulatory criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). SCOPS 
determined the Petitioner did not meet any of the seven criteria he claimed to meet. For the reasons 
discussed below, the record does not support the Director's determinations that the Petitioner did not 
meet the criteria for membership, published material, and judging. 
Membership in Associations 
The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii) requires "documentation of the alien's membership in 
associations in the field for which classification is sought, which require outstanding achievements of 
their members, as judged by recognized national or international experts in their disciplines or fields." 
The level of membership afforded to the person must show that in order to obtain that level of 
membership, recognized national or international experts judged the person as having attained 
outstanding achievements in the field for which classification is sought. See generally 6 USCIS Policy 
Manual F.2(B)(l ), https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
The Petitioner claims to meet this criterion through his honorary membership in the Association of 
Georgian Economists. The Petitioner submitted his membership card issued in 2022 which identifies 
him as an honorary member. The Petitioner also submitted a letter from the President of the 
Association who states that in 2022 the Association's Board unanimously decided to admit the 
Petitioner to honorary membership "due to his outstanding contributions to the field of economics." 
The President explains that in assessing the Petitioner's candidacy, the Board considered the 
Petitioner's extensive work in providing economic advice to major corporations and serving as an

The Petitioner also submitted the minutes of the July 1, 2022 Association's Board meeting which state 
that the Petitioner was one of four candidates for honorary membership and that all seven of the 
members of the Honorary Membership Board voted in favor of his nomination and an invitation to 
honorary membership was extended to the Petitioner. The Petitioner submitted the bylaws of the 
Association which state that honorary membership is awarded only to economists who have "special 
professional achievements by working in both the public and private sectors, who [have] published 
studies of national importance and influence, and who [are] considered to be [] professional 
economist[s] of the highest stature in the field." The Petitioner also submitted the published 
biographies of the Board members which show they are recognized national experts in economics in 
Georgia. 
SCOPS concluded the Association bylaws did not establish that the Association requires outstanding 
achievement of its members as judged by recognized national or international experts in their 
disciplines or fields. SCOPS appears to have disregarded the portion of the bylaws that addresses 
honorary membership. The Association bylaws, the President's letter, and the biographies of the 
Board members show that honorary membership is granted based on outstanding achievements in 
economics as judged by recognized national experts in economics. Accordingly, the Petitioner meets 
this criterion. 
Published Material 
The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii) requires "[p]ublished material about the alien in 
professional or major trade publications or other major media, relating to the alien's work in the field 
for which classification is sought." Professional publications are assessed by their intended audience 
and major media and major trade publications are assessed by their relative circulation, readership, or 
viewership. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
The Petitioner submitted evidence offour articles about him published in two online news publications 
between 2019 and 2024, four interviews with the Petitioner broadcast on three television stations in 
2016 and 2022, and three interviews with the Petitioner broadcast on a business news channel in 2023 
and 2024. The Petitioner submitted evidence that the online news publications are ranked first and 
third in Georgia and the television stations had the first and second highest weekly share audience on 
the weeks the interviews with the Petitioner were broadcast. This evidence demonstrates that the 
articles and interviews were published and broadcast in major media. The Petitioner also submitted 
evidence that the business news channel is a professional publication as it caters to viewers interested 
in current events in the business world. 
SCOPS determined the Petitioner did not meet this criterion because the articles and television 
interviews were not about the Petitioner, but about his work. SCOPS erred because published material 
"relating to the person's work in the field" qualifies as published material about the alien. 
See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. The

Remaining Criteria 
As the Petitioner has established on appeal that he meets three of the initial evidentiary criteria, he has 
satisfied the first part ofthe Kazarian analysis and we need not address and will reserve determination 
of the Petitioner's eligibility under the remaining criteria he claims to meet relating to original 
contributions, authorship, leading or critical role, and comparable evidence ofartistic display. See INS 
v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (per curiam) (holding that agencies are not required to make 
"purely advisory findings" on issues that are unnecessary to the ultimate decision). We have 
considered the evidence relevant to the remaining criteria in the following final merits determination.

III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner seeks a highly restrictive visa classification, intended for individuals already at the top 
of their respective fields, rather than for individuals progressing towards the top. The Petitioner has 
not shown that the significance and recognition of his work are indicative of sustained national or 
international acclaim as one of that small percentage who have risen to the very top of his field, as 
extraordinary ability is described at section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act and 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The 
Petitioner is consequently ineligible for classification as an alien of extraordinary ability.
