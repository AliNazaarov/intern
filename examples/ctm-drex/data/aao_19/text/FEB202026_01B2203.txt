The Petitioner, an executive in the vaccine industry, seeks classification as an individual of 
extraordinary ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. 
§ 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who

I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) ofthe Act makes immigrant visas available to alien with extraordinary ability in 
the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained national 
or international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through extensive 
documentation. These individuals must seek to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability, and their entry into the United States will substantially benefit the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate international 
recognition of their achievements in the field through a one-time achievement in the form of a major, 
internationally recognized award. Alternatively, the petitioner can submit evidence that meets at least

three of the ten criteria listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x), including items such as awards, 
published material in certain media, and scholarly articles. If those standards do not readily apply to

Once a petitioner has met the initial evidence requirements, the next step is a final merits 
determination, in which we assess whether the record shows sustained national or international 
acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage at the very top of the field 
of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) (discussing a two-part review 
where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the required number of criteria, 
considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also Amin v. Mayorkas, 24 F.4th 383, 
391 (5th Cir. 2022) (finding USCIS' two-step analysis of extraordinary ability "consistent with the 
governing statute and regulation").

B. Final Merits 
The Petitioner has provided evidence that he meets at least three of the ten criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) as an executive specializing in business strategy and development in the 
vaccine industry. In a final merits determination, we examine and weigh the totality of the evidence 
to determine whether the Petitioner has sustained national or international acclaim and is one of the 
small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor, and that his achievements have been 
recognized in the field through extensive documentation. See section 203(b)(1 )(A)(i) of the Act; 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2), (3); see also Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20. 
The evidence establishes that the Petitioner has achieved the required recognition in his field for his 
extensive achievements in business strategy and development. Further the record shows that this 
acclaim has been sustained over the course of the Petitioner's long career, during which he has 
successfully identified and steered vaccines, therapeutics and related technologies from early stages 
of research and development through commercialization and widespread availability. The evidence 
indicates he has been particularly effective in establishing ublic-private partnerships andf 
orchestrating major collaborations, such as the establishment of the Ifacility

discussed above, that have had a demonstrable impact on the industry and contributed to pandemic 
preparedness efforts that impact public health. The record also shows that he has commanded a high 
salary that reflects his expertise and recognition in his field, and that he has regularly been invited to 
present in academic, professional and national industry forums based on his status as a leading expert 
in his field. His decades of work as a senior business executive for vaccines for one of the world's 
largest pharmaceutical companies, his significant contributions in this specialized area, and the 
recognition this work has brought him from his industry peers, places him among the small percentage 
at the very top of his field. See section 203(b)(l)(A)(i) of the Act; 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2), (3). 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner has submitted the required initial evidence and demonstrated in the final merits 
determination that he qualifies, by a preponderance of the evidence, as an individual of extraordinary 
ability in business. In addition, he has shown that he will continue to work in his area of extraordinary 
ability and that his entry will substantially benefit prospectively the United States. 
ORDER: The appeal is sustained.
