PETITION: FORM I-129, PETITION FOR A NONIMMIGRANT WORKER 
The Petitioner, a Chinese cultural and language center, seeks to classifY the Beneficiary as a foreign 
national of extraordinary ability in the arts to perform services as a Chinese language and culture 
specialist. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 101(a)(l5)(0)(i), 8 U.S.C. 
§ 1101 ( a)(l5)(0)(i). This 0-1 classification makes nonimmigrant visas available to foreign 
nationals who can demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international 
acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through extensive 
documentation. 
The Director of the Vermont Service Center denied the petition, concluding that the submitted 
documentation did not satisfy the applicable evidentiary requirements. The Director also denied the 
Petitioner's subsequent motion to reopen. 
Upon de novo review, we will dismiss the appeal. 
On March 1 0, 201 7, we sent a notice of intent to ·dismiss the appeal ·based on derogatory 
information, and afforded the Petitioner an opportunity to provide rebuttal evidence. Specifically, 
the Petitioner claims to be a corporation organized under the laws of the Commonwealth of 
Massachusetts. Pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(16)(i), we notified the Petitioner that, according to 
the records of the Massachusetts Department of State, Corporations Division, the Petitioner's 
corporate status is inactive with an "Involuntary Dissolution" dated 2016. 1 
We also informed the Petitioner that, if it is currently dissolved, this fact is material to its eligibility 
for the requested visa.2 Specifically, a company that has been dissolved by the state cannot qualify
