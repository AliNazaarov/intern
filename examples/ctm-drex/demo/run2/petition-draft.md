## I. Employment in a Critical or Essential Capacity for an Organization with a Distinguished Reputation — 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(B)(7)

Kestrelbay Robotics, Inc. ("Kestrelbay" or the "Company") employs the Beneficiary, Mira Okafor-Lund, as Founding Engineer, a position she has held since commencing employment on March 18, 2024 (E1-offer-letter.md). The Beneficiary reports directly to Theo Vance, Co-Founder and Chief Technology Officer (E1-offer-letter.md).

### A. The Organization

Kestrelbay is an autonomous-robotics startup in the Yakima Valley, Washington, whose harvesters "roll down orchard rows on rubber tracks, using soft grippers to pull apples without bruising them" (E2-press-article.md). During the 2025 season, the Company operated eight harvesting machines across three farms (E2-press-article.md). The Company raised a $12 million Series A financing in 2024 (E2-press-article.md). As of September 2025, Kestrelbay has a total company headcount of 31 (E3-org-chart.md). The Company's robotics engineering organization comprises three pods — Motion, Perception, and Hardware — each under the CTO, with a dedicated technical lead for each pod (E3-org-chart.md).

### B. The Beneficiary's Critical and Essential Role

In her capacity as Founding Engineer, the Beneficiary is responsible for the motion-planning software of Kestrelbay's autonomous orchard-harvesting robot (E1-offer-letter.md). She serves as the technical lead of the Motion pod, which comprises six engineers: the Beneficiary and five robotics software engineers (E1-offer-letter.md; E3-org-chart.md). She is one of only three technical leads among the Company's three engineering pods, the others being the leads of the Perception pod and the Hardware pod (E3-org-chart.md). The press has described the Beneficiary as "one of the minds behind the robot's motion planning, which lets the arm reach around branches instead of through them" (E2-press-article.md), and reports that she "helped build a team of dozens since joining in 2024" (E2-press-article.md).

The Beneficiary's work has been decisive to the Company's operational success [client to provide evidence]. The Company's 2025 Harvest Season Field Report states that the new branch-avoidance motion planner, developed by the Motion pod under the Beneficiary's technical leadership, "cut arm collisions with branches by 63% compared with the 2024 season," and that field technicians reported fewer manual resets as a result (E6-field-report.md). That season, the Company's machines harvested approximately 1.9 million apples at a bruise rate of 2.4% — below the hand-picking benchmark of 3.1% at the partner farms — and an average of 410 picks per arm per hour (E6-field-report.md).

The Company's CEO, Ines Calloway, has affirmed the Beneficiary's essential contribution in writing: "You've been essential to getting the harvesters through this season" (E5-email-comp-discussion.md).

For transparency, the Company notes that, as reflected in her offer letter, people-management authority — hiring decisions and performance reviews — remains vested in the CTO, and all of the Company's engineers have the CTO as their people manager (E1-offer-letter.md; E3-org-chart.md). The Beneficiary's role is therefore one of technical leadership rather than personnel supervision; however, her technical leadership over the Motion pod and her ownership of the motion-planning software on which the Company's harvesters depend plainly constitute a critical and essential function within the organization.

Accordingly, the Beneficiary satisfies the first element of the criterion: she is employed in a critical or essential capacity [client to provide evidence].

### C. The Distinguished Reputation of the Organization

The press coverage cited above demonstrates the Company's standing in its field: Kestrelbay's harvesters are the subject of coverage by *Orchard & Machine Weekly*, a trade publication covering the intersection of agriculture and robotics, for their distinctive soft-gripper harvesting approach, their deployment at scale (eight machines on three farms during the 2025 season), and the successful $12 million Series A raised in 2024 (E2-press-article.md). The Beneficiary herself was identified by name in that coverage as one of the Company's earliest hires and one of the minds behind its core motion-planning technology (E2-press-article.md). The Company's reputation is further reflected in the field results it has achieved on its partners' farms, as documented in the 2025 Field Report (E6-field-report.md).

These exhibits establish that Kestrelbay Robotics, Inc. is an organization with a distinguished reputation in autonomous agricultural robotics, and that the Beneficiary occupies a critical or essential position within it [client to provide evidence]. The first criterion is therefore satisfied.

## II. Command of a High Salary or Other Significantly High Remuneration — 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(B)(8)

The Beneficiary's remuneration from Kestrelbay Robotics, Inc. is significantly high, as demonstrated by the base salary established at her hiring (E1-offer-letter.md), the equity component of her compensation (E1-offer-letter.md), and the compensation level at which the Company's CEO has stated the Company's goal of bringing the Beneficiary's package, subject to the contingencies identified in that correspondence (E5-email-comp-discussion.md).

At the time of her hiring, the Company offered the Beneficiary an annual base salary of $182,000, paid semi-monthly, together with an option to purchase 0.8% of the Company's fully diluted shares, vesting over four years with a one-year cliff (E1-offer-letter.md). This base salary has remained in force since her hire: the Company's Employee Earnings Statement for the period January 1, 2025 through October 31, 2025 records an annual base salary rate of $182,000.00, "unchanged since hire" (E4-payroll-2025.md). On that basis, the Beneficiary's gross pay for the period January 1, 2025 through October 31, 2025 was $151,666.70 (E4-payroll-2025.md).

The equity component of her compensation further reflects the Company's valuation of the Beneficiary's contribution [client to provide evidence]. The Beneficiary holds an option to purchase 0.8% of the Company's fully diluted shares, vesting over four years with a one-year cliff (E1-offer-letter.md).

Finally, in an August 21, 2025 email to the Beneficiary — copied to the CTO — the Company's CEO, Ines Calloway, stated that, in recognition of the Beneficiary's essential contribution to the 2025 harvest, the Company's goal is to bring the Beneficiary to "a $250k package next year," to be reconsidered in Q1 2026 (E5-email-comp-discussion.md). The Company submits this correspondence as further evidence of the significantly high remuneration level at which it regards the Beneficiary's compensation, while noting for candor that the 2026 target is contingent on the closing of a Series B financing and Board approval of the 2026 compensation plan, and has therefore been characterized to the Beneficiary as a goal rather than a commitment (E5-email-comp-discussion.md).

In sum, a base salary of $182,000.00, unchanged since hire and verified by the Company's own payroll records (E1-offer-letter.md; E4-payroll-2025.md), an option to purchase 0.8% of the Company's fully diluted shares (E1-offer-letter.md), and a stated compensation goal of "a $250k package next year" articulated by the CEO, subject to the closing of a Series B financing and Board approval of the 2026 compensation plan (E5-email-comp-discussion.md), collectively demonstrate that the Beneficiary commands significantly high remuneration [client to provide evidence]. The second criterion is therefore satisfied.
