Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner intends to work in the field of venture capital investment and management. He seeks 
classification under the employment-based, first-preference (EB-I) immigrant visa category as an 
alien with "extraordinary ability." See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 
203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). Successful petitioners for U.S. permanent residence in this 
category must demonstrate "sustained national or international acclaim" and extensively document 
recognition of their achievements in their fields. Id. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition. SCOPS concluded that the Petitioner met 
one often initial evidentiary criteria- two less than needed for a final merits determination. On appeal, 
the Petitioner contends that he satisfies three additional evidentiary criteria. 
The Petitioner bears the burden of demonstrating eligibility for the requested benefit by a 
preponderance of the evidence. Matter of Chawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). 
Exercising de novo appellate review, see Matter of Christa 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 
2015), we conclude that he has met only one additional evidentiary criterion. He has established: 
commandment of a high salary in relation to others in the field. We will therefore dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
To qualify as an alien with extraordinary ability, a petitioner must demonstrate that they: 
• Have "extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics;" 
• Seek to work in their field of expertise in the United States; and 
• Through their work, would substantially benefit the country. 
Section 203(b)(1 )(A)(i)-(iii) of the Act. The term "extraordinary ability" means expertise 
commensurate with "one of that small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of 
endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
Evidence must demonstrate an alien's receipt of either "a major, international recognized award" or 
satisfaction of at least three of ten lesser evidentiary criteria. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i-x). 1 If a 
petitioner meets either standard and the other statutory requirements, USCIS must then make a final 
merits determination as to whether the record, as a whole, establishes sustained national or 
international acclaim and recognized achievements placing them among the small percentage at their 
field's very top. Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1119-20 (9th Cir. 2010); see generally 6 USCIS 
Policy Manual F.(2)(B), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
II. ANALYSIS 
The record shows that the Petitioner, a German native and citizen, plans to provide expertise and 
leadership for venture capital investment and management in the life sciences industry, with a 
particular focus on growing companies that are developing innovative and emerging therapies and 
treatments for mental illnesses and disorders. This treatment includes, among other things, 
psychedelics - psilocybin (magic mushrooms). Specifically, the Petitioner states that his goal is to 
". . . further support the development of cutting-edge solutions that push the boundaries of what is 
possible in treating mental illness, ultimately improving access to and quality of care for millions of 
individuals." 
The record does not demonstrate - nor does the Petitioner assert - his receipt of a major international 
award. He must therefore meet at least three of the ten lesser evidentiary requirements at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(i-x). 
The record supports SCOPS' s finding that the Petitioner successfully submitted evidence that the alien 
has performed in a leading or critical role for organizations or establishments that have a distinguished 
reputation. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii). 
On appeal, the Petitioner asserts that he also provided documentation of: 
• Evidence of membership in associations that require outstanding achievements of their 
members. 
• Evidence of the alien's participation as a judge of the work of others in the same or an allied 
field; 
• Evidence that the alien has commanded a high salary or other significantly high renumeration 
for services, in relation to others in the field. 
See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii), (iv), (ix) respectively. He therefore contends that he meets these 
requirements.

D. Final Merits Decision 
The Petitioner has met at least two initial evidentiary criteria, falling short of the three initial 
evidentiary criteria required. Congress set a very high benchmark for aliens of extraordinary ability 
by requiring the petitioner demonstrate "sustained national or international acclaim" and present 
"extensive documentation" to show his or her achievements have been recognized in the field. Section 
203(b)(1 )(A)(i) of the Act. Here, because the Petitioner has not satisfied the threshold "initial 
evidence" requirements at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), we cannot conclude that they are an alien of 
extraordinary ability and need not further analyze the merits under the statute. See Kazarian, 596 F.3d 
at 1119-20; see also Nagaiah v. Allen, -- F.Supp.3d ---, 2025 WL 1508028, at* 10 (E.D. Pa. May 27, 
2025) (finding the regulation "establishes a necessary, not sufficient, evidentiary requirement for a 
petitioner who seeks an extraordinary ability visa."). Therefore, we decline to reach and hereby 
reserve any final merits determination in this matter. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) 
(per curiam) (holding that agencies are not required to make "purely advisory findings" on issues that 
are unnecessary to the ultimate decision). 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
