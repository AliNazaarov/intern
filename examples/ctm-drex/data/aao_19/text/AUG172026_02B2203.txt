Appeal of U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) Decision 
Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. See Immigration and 
Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). This first preference 
classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their extraordinary 
ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been 
recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
USCIS denied the petition, concluding that the record did not establish that the Petitioner received a 
major, internationally recognized award or met the requirements of at least three of the ten evidentiary 
criteria. The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will withdraw the decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with the 
following analysis. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(1 )(A) of the Act makes immigrant visas available to individuals with extraordinary 
ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained 
national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, provided that the individual seeks to enter the United States to continue 
work in the area of extraordinary ability, and the individual's entry into the United States will 
substantially benefit prospectively the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate 
international recognition of his or her achievements in the field through a one-time achievement (that 
is, a major, internationally recognized award). If that petitioner does not submit this evidence, then 
he or she must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the ten criteria 
listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) (including items such as awards, published material in certain 
media, and scholarly articles). The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4) allows a petitioner to submit 
comparable evidence ifhe or she is able to demonstrate that the standards at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)­
(x) do not readily apply to the individual's occupation. 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) 
( discussing a two-part review where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the 
required number of criteria, considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also 
Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 
(W.D. Wash. 2011). 
II. ANAL YSrS 
Because the Petitioner has not indicated or established that he has received a major, internationally 
recognized award, he must satisfy at least three of the alternate regulatory criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). users concluded that the Petitioner satisfied two criteria relating to 
judging and authorship of scholarly articles at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv) and (vi). However, USCrS 
further determined that the Petitioner did not meet the remaining two claimed evidentiary criteria 
associated with awards and original contributions of major significance at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) 
and (v). On appeal, the Petitioner asserts that he meets these additional two criteria. We will not 
disturb USCrS' determinations regarding the Petitioner's judging and authorship of scholarly articles. 
Upon de novo review, we conclude that the Petitioner has met the additional criterion of original 
contributions under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v) and has therefore satisfied the initial evidence 
requirements. 
A. Original Contributions of Major Significance 
The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v) calls for "evidence of the alien's original scientific, 
scholarly, artistic, athletic, or business-related contributions of major significance in the field." A 
petitioner may show that his or her contributions have been widely implemented throughout the field, 
have remarkably impacted or influenced the field, or have otherwise risen to a level of major 
significance. See Visinscaia, 4 F. Supp. 3d at 134-35. Relevant evidence of the significance of an 
original contribution may include, but is not limited to: 
• Published materials about the significance of the alien's original work; 
• Letters providing details about how the alien's original work has been significant in the field; 
• Citation ofthe alien's published scholarly work at a level indicative ofmajor significance; and 
• Patents or licenses deriving from the alien's work or evidence of its commercialization. 
See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l ), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
Detailed letters from experts in the field explaining the nature and significance of the person's 
contribution may also provide valuable context for evaluating the claimed original contributions of

major significance, particularly when the record includes documentation corroborating the claimed 
significance. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, supra, at F.2(B)(l). Expert letters should 
specifically describe a petitioner's contribution and its significance to the field and state the basis of 
the writer's knowledge and expertise. Id. 
The Petitioner contends that USCIS erred in determining the "record lack[s] objective documentary 
evidence to indicate the significance of the [P]etitioner's contributions to the field" and we agree. The 
Petitioner is a researcher in the field of radiotherapy, or radiation therapy, whose work involves 
understanding and overcoming radiation resistance in cancer. The record contains evidence of the 
Petitioner's published research in which contends he has made "groundbreaking discoveries," his 
Google Scholar Profile, and five letters of support from experts in his field. On appeal, the Petitioner 
additionally submits a joint support letter signed by five physicians and scientists in his field. 
The Petitioner states that his work "significantly advanced our understanding of how cancer cells 
survive radiation therapy and has identified novel therapeutic targets to improve cancer treatment 
outcomes." He asserts that the importance of his work is reflected in his research accumulating more 
than 4,000 citations. This number is confirmed by his Google Scholar Profile. The profile also shows 
his highest cited publication has garnered 314 citations. The Petitioner submits a letter from M-S-, 
Director of Radiobiology atl l who highlights the Petitioner's research and states 
that "200-300 citations associated with a single manuscript in this field is very high and demonstrates 
the significant impact of that work. A researcher with 4,000 total journal citations, especially if no 
single publication has more than 300 citations, demonstrates a significant and sustained impact within 
their field." 
Furthermore[ in the support letter written by G-T-, Director in the Division of Experimental Radiation 
Oncology at !University, the author states that the Petitioner's "discoveries have not 
remained confined to the laboratory. They have directly informed the development of rational 
combination therapies ... many of which are now being tested by oncologists on their patients in 
preclinical and clinical settings in the United States and around the world." In the letter written by B­
W-, Executive Vice President of Science and Research at the~---------~the author 
discusses the Petitioner's discoveries and how they have "significantly advanced the field and 
proposed actionable strategies to overcome resistance and enhance radiotherapy outcomes." Further, 
in the letter the Petitioner submits on appeal, five physicians and scientists in his field jointly state that 
they "place his contributions in the top 1-5% of global researchers in this field." Here, the Petitioner 
provides evidence ofa high citation record along with letters of support which confirm the significance 
of his discoveries in his field. In addition, the letters attest that his work has not only contributed to 
research in his field, but also to the ongoing treatment of cancer patients. We conclude that the 
Petitioner has shown, by a preponderance of the evidence, that he has made original scientific 
contributions ofmajor significance in his field. Therefore, the Petitioner has established that he meets 
this criterion, and we withdraw USCTS' conclusion to the contrary.

B. Final Merits Determination 
Based on the foregoing discussion, the Petitioner has satisfied at least three of the ten evidentiary 
requirements at 8 e.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). 1 We will therefore withdraw USeTS' decision, because 
that decision was based on a determination that the Petitioner had satisfied only two criteria and that 
he did not satisfy the initial evidence requirements for this classification. 
However, granting three criteria does not suffice to establish eligibility for the classification the 
Petitioner seeks. users must now make a final merits determination on the Petitioner's filing and 
consider whether the record establishes the sustained national or international acclaim and recognized 
achievements sufficient to place the Petitioner among the small percentage at the very top of his field. 
See section 203(b)(l)(A)(i) ofthe Act; 8 e.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2), (3); see also Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-
20. users did not make such a finding. Rather than make this determination in the first instance, we 
will remand the matter. 
The final merits determination should weigh the evidence submitted in support of all claimed initial 
evidentiary criteria at 8 e.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x), any other potentially relevant evidence in the 
record (including evidence that does not fit one of the evidentiary criteria), and the Petitioner's claims 
and evidence on appeal. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, supra, at F.2(8)(2). 
ORDER: The decision is withdrawn. The matter is remanded for the entry of a new decision 
consistent with the foregoing analysis.
