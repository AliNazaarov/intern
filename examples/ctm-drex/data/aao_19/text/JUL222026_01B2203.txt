Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an integration architect, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) denied the petition, concluding the Petitioner did 
not establish his receipt ofa major internationally recognized award or demonstrate, in the alternative, 
that he satisfied at least three ofthe ten initial evidentiary criteria for this classification. We dismissed 
the Petitioner's appeal of USCIS' decision and have dismissed four subsequent combined motions to 
reopen and reconsider. The matter is now before us on a combined motion to reopen and reconsider. 
The Petitioner bears the burden of proof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the motion. 
A motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision was based on an incorrect application of 
law or policy and that the decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the record of proceedings 
at the time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). A motion to reopen must state new facts and be 
supported by documentary evidence. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2). We may grant motions that satisfy these 
requirements and demonstrate eligibility for the requested benefit. See Matter ofCoelho, 20 I&N Dec. 
464, 473 (BIA 1992) (requiring that new evidence have the potential to change the outcome). Motions 
that do not satisfy these requirements must be dismissed. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
Further, the scope of a motion is limited to "the prior decision" and "the latest decision in the 
proceeding." 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(l)(i), (ii). Therefore, we will only consider new evidence and stated 
reasons for reconsideration to the extent that they pertain to our latest decision. 
On April 16, 2026, we dismissed the Petitioner's fourth combined motion to reopen and reconsider, 
concluding that it was untimely filed. An affected party is required to file a motion on an unfavorable 
decision within 33 calendar days of the date the decision was mailed. 8 C.F.R. §§ 103.5(a)(l), 
103.8(b). We observed that our prior decision was mailed to the Petitioner on December 10, 2025, 
and that his Form I-290B, Notice of Appeal or Motion, was received at the designated filing location 
on February 13, 2026, 65 days after we mailed the decision. 
On motion to reconsider, the Petitioner claims that his prior motion included a cover letter explaining 
the reason for the untimely filing, accompanied by supporting evidence sufficient to demonstrate that 
the delayed filing was attributable to users error. He maintains that we erred by summarily 
dismissing his motion as untimely without acknowledging his claims and evidence, contending that 
our "[f]ailure to address a properly raised legal argument is itself a basis for reconsideration." 
Failure to file a motion to reopen within the 33-day period allowed by regulation may be excused in 
users' discretion where it is demonstrated that the delay was reasonable and was beyond the control 
of the applicant or petitioner. See 8 e.F.R. § 103.5(a)(l)(i). However, contrary to the Petitioner's 
claims, the record shows that his fourth motion did not include a brief or cover letter with an 
explanation for the untimely filing, nor was it accompanied by supporting evidence. The motion 
included a Form G-28, a Form I-290B, and a Form G-1650, Authorization for AeH Transactions. The 
Petitioner has not established that we failed to address a legal argument in our prior decision, or that 
we incorrectly applied the law or users policy by dismissing the fourth motion as untimely filed, 
based on the evidence in the record at the time of our April 2026 decision. Accordingly, we will 
dismiss the motion to reconsider. 
In support of his motion to reopen, the Petitioner submits new evidence showing that his prior Form 
I-290B was timely submitted to the designated users filing location on January 7, 2026, but rejected 
based on a determination that the submission did not include all required filing fees. 1 The Petitioner 
claims that this original submission included a new Form I-290B, signed by him on January 5, 2026, 
and an attached exhibit that included a copy of a previously filed Form I-290B. He emphasizes that 
users issued two rejection notices, with two separate receipt numbers, on January 25, 2026, thus 
suggesting that during intake, it erroneously deemed the older Form I-290B to be a separate, new filing 
requiring a filing fee. The Petitioner requests that we exercise our discretion to excuse the untimely 
filing ofthe fourth motion and consider that motion on its merits. He claims that the delay in the filing 
was due to users error and was outside his control. See 8 e.F.R. § 103.S(a)(l)(i). 
While the Petitioner has now provided an explanation for the untimely filing of his fourth motion, the 
newly submitted evidence does not establish that users erred by rejecting the Form I-290B it received 
on January 7, 2026. Notably, the Petitioner has not submitted a complete copy of that original 
submission or other evidence to corroborate his claim that his Form I-290B, when filed, was 
accompanied by the correct payment. Without such evidence, the Petitioner has not established that 
users erroneously rejected the motion based on his failure to submit the correct fee. The Petitioner 
has therefore not shown proper cause for reopening and the motion to reopen will be dismissed. 2

Finally, we acknowledge the Petitioner's submission of new evidence and legal arguments relating to 
his underlying Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers, and his eligibility for classification 
for an alien of extraordinary ability under section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act. He states that the new 
evidence addresses evidentiary deficiencies identified in USCIS' denial dated July 22, 2024, and in 
our subsequent dismissals of his appeal and earlier motions, noting that the new evidence "cures those 
concerns." 
However, the scope of a motion is limited to "the prior decision" and "the latest decision in the 
proceeding." 8 C.F.R. § 103.S(a)(l)(i), (ii). Therefore, we will only consider new evidence to the 
extent that it pertains to our latest decision dismissing the Petitioner's fourth combined motion. Here, 
the Petitioner has not provided new facts to establish that we erred in dismissing the prior motion and 
we have no basis to reopen our prior decision. We will not grant the Petitioner's request for sua sponte 
reopening or otherwise re-adjudicate the petition anew. Therefore, the underlying petition remains 
denied. 
Although the Petitioner has submitted additional evidence in support of the motion to reopen, he has 
not established eligibility. On motion to reconsider, the Petitioner has not established that our previous 
decision was based on an incorrect application of law or policy at the time we issued our decision. 
Therefore, the motion will be dismissed. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
ORDER: The motion to reopen is dismissed. 
FURTHER ORDER: The motion to reconsider is dismissed.
