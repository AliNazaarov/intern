Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-129, Petition for a Nonimmigrant Worker (Extraordinary Ability- 0) 
The Petitioner, a pastry bakery restaurant, seeks to classify the Beneficiary as a confectionary bakery 
chef of extraordinary ability. To do so, the Petitioner pursues 0-1 nonimmigrant classification, 
available to individuals who can demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or 
international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through extensive 
documentation. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 10l(a)(15)(O)(i), 8 U.S.C. 
§ 1101(a)(15)(O)(i). 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding the Petitioner did not establish 
the Beneficiary's satisfaction of the initial evidentiary criteria applicable to individuals of 
extraordinary ability in the arts: nomination for or receipt of a significant national or international 
award or at least three of six possible forms of documentation. The matter is now before us on appeal. 
8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance ofthe evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 l&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
At initial filing, the Petitioner submitted a cover letter claiming that the "[ e ]nclosed set of 
documentation ... serves to verify the fact that Chef [ the Beneficiary] is one of the toR recognized 
chefs in Re ublic of Korea in the field of culina arts as officiall certified by the I Iast 
.....____________________________. awarded in 2016." However, 
the Petitioner did not explain which categories of evidence, if any, related to the documentation. A 
petitioner may submit evidence either ofnomination for or receipt of significant national or international 
awards or prizes such as an Academy Award, an Emmy, a Grammy, or a Director's Guild Award, or at 
least three of six listed categories ofdocuments. See 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iv)(A)-(B). A petitioner may 
submit comparable evidence if the criteria do not readily apply to the beneficiary's occupation. See 8 
C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iv)(C). 
SCOPS issued a request for evidence (RFE), informing the Petitioner of the evidentiary requirements, 
including a discussion of the submitted documentation. In response, the Petitioner again provided 
documentation but did not specify which evidence, if any, related to 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iv)(A) or 
(B)(l)-(6). 
In denying the petition, SCOPS stated: 
Upon review of the record, USCIS acknowledges that the pet1t10ner submitted 
numerous documents in support ofthe Form 1-129 petition seeking classification ofthe 
beneficiary as an alien of extraordinary ability in the arts (O-lB). However, the 
petitioner did not provide sufficient explanation or analysis demonstrating how the 
submitted materials meet the evidentiary criteria set forth at 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iv). 
While the record contains items such as testimonials, contract, certificates, and media 
articles, these documents were presented without adequate discussion linking them to the 
specific regulatory criteria. Merely providing documentation, without identifying which 
criterion each piece of evidence is intended to satisfy or explaining its significance, is 
insufficient to establish eligibility. 
USCIS cannot make the necessary determinations based solely on the submission of raw 
documents absent accompanying explanation or analysis. 
Nevertheless, SCOPS conducted an analysis of the evidence that it determined was most likely 
appropriate under 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iv)(A)-(B). SCOPS concluded that the Petitioner did not show 
the Beneficiary met 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iv)(A) or any of the criteria under 8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2(o)(3)(iv)((B)(l)-(6). 
On appeal, the Petitioner again does not identify which regulatory requirements, if any, relate to the 
Beneficiary's documentation. Instead, the Petitioner maintains that the Beneficiary's receipt of the
I I' and "No objection letter issued by thel t' "has 
demonstrated the extraordinary abilities and professional accomplishments ofbeneficiary for the position 
of pastry chef' and "verif[ies] [the Beneficiary's] extraordinary abilities as a Pastry Chef." 
The Petitioner bears the burden of proof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance ofthe evidence. 
Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. at 375-76. Here, providing evidence without explaining or indicating how 
that evidence qualifies the Beneficiary for the specific regulatory requirements does not meet that 
burden. Even on appeal, the Petitioner does not argue that the Beneficiary fulfills 8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2(o)(3)(iv)(A), or which of the six categories, if any, the Beneficiary satisfies under 8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2(o)(3)(iv)(B)(l)-(6). The burden remains with the Petitioner to identify the regulatory categories 
and explain how the evidence establishes eligibility rather than simply offering evidence and generally 
asserting that it demonstrates the Beneficiary's extraordinary abilities. 
Because the Petitioner has not shown how the Beneficiary satisfies the regulatory requirements under 
8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iv)(A) or at least three of the six categories under 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iv)(B), 
we need not provide a totality determination to establish whether the Beneficiary has sustained national 
or international acclaim, has received a high level of achievement, and has been recognized as being

alternate basis for the decision. 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
