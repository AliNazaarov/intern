Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a competitive swimmer, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding the record did not establish that 
the Petitioner had a major, internationally recognized award, nor did it demonstrate that he met at least 
three of the ten regulatory criteria listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) - (x). The matter is now before 
us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance ofthe evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(1 )(A) of the Act makes immigrant visas available to individuals with extraordinary 
ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained 
national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, provided that the individual seeks to enter the United States to continue 
work in the area of extraordinary ability, and the individual's entry into the United States will 
substantially benefit prospectively the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate 
international recognition of his or her achievements in the field through a one-time achievement (that 
is, a major, internationally recognized award). If that petitioner does not submit this evidence, then 
he or she must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the ten criteria 
listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) (including items such as awards, published material in certain 
media, and scholarly articles). The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4) allows a petitioner to submit 
comparable evidence if they are able to demonstrate that the standards at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)­
(x) do not readily apply to the individual's occupation. 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) 
( discussing a two-part review where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the 
required number of criteria, considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also 
Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 
(W.D. Wash. 2011).

A. Awards 
The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3 )(i) calls for "[ d]ocumentation of the alien's receipt oflesser 
nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards for excellence in the field of endeavor." We 
first determine whether the alien is the recipient of prizes or awards, and then whether the award is 
nationally or internationally recognized and was granted based upon excellence in the alien's field of 
endeavor. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
SCOPS determined that the Petitioner did not satisfy this criterion. However, upon review, we 
conclude the record contains sufficient documentary evidence to establish that the Petitioner received 
nationally and internationally recognized awards from competitive swimming events, and SCOPS' 
determination on this issue will be withdrawn. For example, the Petitioner provided documentation 
showing that he earned first, second, and third place medals in multiple events at Polish National 
Championships. On appeal, the Petitioner contends that the "hierarchy of competitive swimming 
places National Championship medals at the apex of domestic recognition." Further, the record 
contains evidence that the Polish National Swimming Championship is a designated World Aquatics

B. Memberships 
The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii) calls for "documentation of the alien's membership in 
associations in the field for which classification is sought." In addition, the association must require 
"outstanding achievements oftheir members, as judged by recognized national or international experts 
in their disciplines or fields." Once membership in a qualifying association has been established, we 
determine whether the association requires those at the alien's level of membership to have attained 
outstanding achievements in the field. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), 
www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
The Petitioner asserts that he meets this criterion as a member ofthe Polish National Swimming Team. 
Being a member of a national team may demonstrate eligibility for this criterion, as such teams 
typically limit their number of members and have a rigorous selection process. However, it is the 
Petitioner's burden to demonstrate that he meets every element of a given criterion. Specifically, the 
record does not contain sufficient information to establish his outstanding achievements were judged 
by recognized national or international experts in his field. The Petitioner submitted a letter from
I L The author states that she is "entrusted with 
oversight, selection, and governance of Poland's national swimming teams." In describing the 
selection process, the author states that "[ a ]fter a thorough evaluation, our Executive Bureau... 
determined that [the Petitioner] exceeded all national qualification requirements and merited selection 
to represent Poland at major international competitions ...." Although the record includes information 
about the author and she indicates she is a member of the "Executive Bureau," the record does not 
include sufficient supporting evidence addressing her specific role in the selection process and who 
the other members of the "Executive Bureau" are in order for us to determine if they are recognized 
national or international experts in the field. We therefore conclude that the Petitioner has not satisfied 
this criterion. 
C. Published Materials 
The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii) requires "[p]ublished material about the alien in 
professional or major trade publications or other major media, relating to the alien's work in the field 
for which classification is sought." These materials must include "the title, date, and author of the 
material, and any necessary translation." Id. The published material should be about the alien and 
their work, rather than the alien's employer and its work (or about an organization and its work). 
Professional or major trade publications may be identified by their intended audience, while other 
major media and major trade publications should have elevated circulation or readership relative to 
other media in the field. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l). 
The Petitioner submitted four articles in support of meeting this criterion. The first is a brief 
introduction to an interview with the Petitioner on a sports show on I I The brief summary

Further, the other three articles show competition results where the Petitioner is briefly mentioned in 
a sentence or two or his name appears in the results of the competition. For example, in one article, 
the only mention ofthe Petitioner is in the sentence, 'I I I I .. also swam in the final." While the two other articles cover competitions he 
participated in while at I Iand list him as a member of the relay team, report the 
competition results, and state that he was a sophomore at the time of publication. Here, articles with

E, 2023 WL 8037730, at *6 (N.D. Tex. Nov. 20, 2023) (upholding USCIS' determination that the

passing as it related to competition results). We conclude that the publications in the record do not 
provide sufficient information about the Petitioner and his work in order to meet this criterion. 
Therefore, we withdraw SCOPS' finding that the Petitioner satisfied this criterion. 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner has not submitted the required initial evidence of either a one-time achievement or 
documents that meet at least three of the ten lesser criteria. As a result, we need not provide the type 
of final merits determination referenced in Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20. Nevertheless, we advise 
that we have reviewed the record in the aggregate, concluding that it does not support a conclusion 
that the Petitioner has established the acclaim and recognition required for the classification sought. 
Congress set a very high benchmark for aliens of extraordinary ability by requiring the petitioner 
demonstrate "sustained national or international acclaim" and present "extensive documentation" to 
show his or her achievements have been recognized in the field. Section 203(b )(1 )(A)(i) of the Act. 
Here, because the Petitioner has not satisfied the threshold "initial evidence" requirements at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3), we cannot conclude that he is an alien of extraordinary ability and need not further 
analyze the merits under the statute. See Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20; see also Nagaiah v. Allen, -
- F.Supp.3d---, 2025 WL 1508028, at *10 (E.D. Pa. May 27, 2025) (finding the regulation "establishes 
a necessary, not sufficient, evidentiary requirement for a petitioner who seeks an extraordinary ability 
visa."). Therefore, we decline to reach and hereby reserve reaching a determination on any additional 
criterion and any final merits determination in this matter. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 
(1976) (per curiam) (holding that agencies are not required to make "purely advisory findings" on 
issues that are unnecessary to the ultimate decision).
