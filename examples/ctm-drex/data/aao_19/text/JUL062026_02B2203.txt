Appeal of U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) Decision 
Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a tattoo artist, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See Immigration 
and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S .C. § 1153(b0(l)(A). This first preference 
classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their extraordinary 
ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been 
recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
USCIS denied the petition, concluding the record did not establish that the Petitioner qualifies as an 
alien of extraordinary ability either as the recipient of a major, internationally recognized award, or 
that the Petitioner meets at least three of the ten initial regulatory criteria listed at 8 C.F.R. § 
204.5(h)(3)(i)- (x). The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa 's, Inc. , 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act makes visas available to immigrants with extraordinary ability if: 
1. the alien has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which 
has been demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements 
have been recognized in the field through extensive documentation, 
11. the alien seeks to enter the United States to continue work in the area of extraordinary ability, 
and 
m. the alien's entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively the United 
States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those aliens in "that small percentage who have risen 
to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate sustained 
acclaim and the recognition of his or her achievements in the field through a one-time achievement 
(that is, a major, internationally recognized award). If that petitioner does not submit this evidence, 
then he or she must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the ten 
categories listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) (including items such as awards, published material 
in certain media, and scholarly articles). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the alien is among the small percentage at the 
very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) (discussing 
a two-part review where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the required number 
of criteria, considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. 
Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner is a tattoo artist with approximately 10 years of professional experience in Venezuela 
and the United States. He states that he currently serves as a resident artist at I 
LLC inl lCalifornia and intends to continue working there as a tattoo artist. 1 
A. Evidentiary Criteria 
Because the Petitioner has not indicated or demonstrated that he has received a major, internationally 
recognized award, he must instead satisfy at least three of the alternate regulatory criteria at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(ii)(i)-(x). In his initial petition, the Petitioner claimed to meet four criteria. USCIS 
evaluated the claims the Petitioner made in the initial petition and in response to the request for 
evidence (RFE) and concluded the Petitioner only satisfied two criteria, participation as a judge of 
others' work at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv) and display of work at artistic exhibitions or showcases at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii). On appeal, the Petitioner reasserts the same four criteria presented in the 
initial filing, published materials about the alien in professional or major media (iii); judge of the 
works of others (iv); display of work at artistic exhibitions or showcases (vii); and high salary or 
remuneration for services (ix), and adds one additional criterion that he claimed in response to USCIS' 
RFE, leading or critical role for distinguished organizations or establishments (viii). He contends that 
he satisfies "five of the ten regulatory criteria." After reviewing all the evidence in the record, we 
conclude the Petitioner has not demonstrated eligibility for this highly restrictive immigrant 
classification for the reasons we will discuss below.

The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii) requires "[p]ublished material about the alien in 
professional or major trade publications or other major media, relating to the alien's work in the field 
for which classification is sought." These materials must include "the title, date, and author of the 
material, and any necessary translation." Id. The published material should be about the alien and 
their work, rather than the alien's employer and its work ( or about an organization and its work). 
Professional or major trade publications may be identified by their intended audience, while other 
major media and major trade publications should have elevated circulation or readership relative to 
other media in the field. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), www.uscis.gov/policy­
manual. 
In support ofthis criterion, the Petitioner submitted copies ofonline articles published by media outlets 
such as, El Farandi, La Patilla, Univision, El Universal, and The Wynwood Times, as well as evidence 
of appearances on radio and television (TV) programs. He asserts that these publications and 
broadcasts feature his work and professional achievements in the field of tattoo art. The Petitioner 
also submitted website traffic data, media fact sheets, and transcripts of the TV and radio interviews. 
USCIS concluded that the Petitioner did not satisfy this criterion because he failed to submit sufficient 
evidence establishing the circulation and reach of the publications and demonstrating that the 
submitted evidence qualified as professional or major trade publications or other major media. Upon 
de novo review, we conclude that the Petitioner has submitted sufficient evidence to establish that 
some of the published materials appeared in media outlets with significant circulation or audience 
reach. The record also demonstrates that the articles focus primarily on the Petitioner and his artistic 
work and professional achievements. Accordingly, the Petitioner has submitted sufficient evidence to 
satisfy this criterion. 
2. Comparable Evidence 
This regulatory provision provides petitioners the opportunity to submit comparable evidence to 
establish the person's eligibility, if it is determined that the evidentiary criteria described in the 
regulations do not readily apply to the person's occupation. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual 
F.2(B)(l), https://www.uscis.gov/policymanual. When evaluating such comparable evidence, officers 
must consider whether the regulatory criteria are readily applicable to the person's occupation and, if 
not, whether the evidence provided is truly comparable to the criteria listed in that regulation. Id. 
The Petitioner asserts that the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii), relating to membership in 
associations, is not readily applicable to his occupation because "the tattoo industry does not have 
formal, peer-reviewed associations that admit members based on documented outstanding 
achievement." Instead, he requests that we consider his selection as a representative of the tattoo 
supply company I ILLC as comparable evidence, arguing that the competitive, merit­
based selection process is equivalent to membership in a qualifying association. 
The Petitioner also asserts that the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(x), relating to commercial 
successes in the performing arts in the form of box office receipts or record, cassette, compact disk, or 
video sales, is not "readily applicable" to him. Instead, he contends that his teaching role at the I II Iand his celebrity clientele constitute comparable evidence demonstrating that he has 
risen to the top of his field.

B. Final Merits Determination 
As the Petitioner has established that he meets the requisite initial evidence, we will evaluate whether 
he has demonstrated, by a preponderance of the evidence, that he has sustained national or 
international acclaim and is one of the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor, and 
that his achievements have been recognized in the field through extensive documentation. In a final 
merits determination, we analyze the Petitioner's accomplishments and weigh the totality of the 
evidence to determine if his successes are sufficient to demonstrate that he has extraordinary ability in 
the field of endeavor. See section 203(b)(l)(A)(i) of the Act; 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2)-(3); see also 
Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20. 
The Petitioner contends on appeal that USCIS failed to conduct a proper final merits analysis. 
Nonetheless, we exercise de novo review of all issues of fact, law, policy, and discretion. See Matter 
o_f Christo 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537. This means that we look at the record anew and are not required 
to defer to findings made in the initial decision. Furthermore, our decision may address new issues 
that were not raised or resolved in the prior decision. 
While we may not discuss every document in the record, we have reviewed and considered each one. 
Based on our de novo review, we conclude the Petitioner has not demonstrated eligibility for this 
highly restrictive immigrant classification for the reasons we will discuss below. 
The Petitioner maintains that the totality of the evidence establishes that he is among the small 
percentage of individuals who have risen to the very top of the tattoo art field. He contends that his 
sustained acclaim, widespread recognition, and commercial success demonstrate that he has achieved

top of his field. However, the record, in the aggregate, does not support the conclusion that he has 
shown the required acclaim and recognition, or otherwise shown that he is one of the small percentage 
of individuals who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor. See Section 203(b )(l)(A) of the 
Act; 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2).

III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner has submitted the required initial evidence that meets at least three of the ten categories 
of evidence listed under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). As a result, we have provided the type of final 
merits determination referenced in Kazarian, 596 F. 3d at 1119-20. Based on our review of the record 
as a whole, we conclude that the record, in the aggregate, does not support a conclusion that the 
Petitioner has established the acclaim and recognition required to be classified as an alien of 
extraordinary ability in his field. 
The Petitioner seeks a highly restrictive visa classification, intended for individuals at the top of their 
respective fields. Here, he has not shown that his accomplishments rise to the required level of 
sustained national or international acclaim, or that it is consistent with a "career of acclaimed work in 
the field" as contemplated by Congress. H.R. Rep. No. 101-723, 59 (Sept. 19, 1990); see also Section 
203(b)(1 )(A) of the Act. Moreover, the record does not otherwise demonstrate that the Petitioner is 
one of the small percentage of individuals who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor. See 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act; 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
For the reasons discussed above, the Petitioner has not demonstrated eligibility to be classified as an 
alien of extraordinary ability in his field. The appeal will be dismissed for the above stated reasons.
