Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-129, Petition for a Nonimmigrant Worker (Extraordinary Ability- 0) 
The Petitioner, an archaeology business, seeks to classify the Beneficiary as an archaeologist of 
extraordinary ability. This 0-1 nonimmigrant visa classification is available to individuals with 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in the field through extensive documentation. See Immigration and Nationality Act 
(the Act) section 10l(a)(15)(O)(i), 8 U.S .C. § l 10l(a)(15)(O)(i). 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition because the Petitioner did not satisfy the 
consultation requirement, and the Petitioner did not demonstrate the Beneficiary met the initial 
evidentiary criteria applicable to individuals of extraordinary ability: either receipt of a major, 
internationally recognized award or at least three ofeight possible forms of documentation. The matter 
is now before us on appeal. 8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christa 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
As relevant here, the regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(2)(ii)(D) requires written advisory opinions 
from the appropriate consulting entities. Peer group means a group or organization which is comprised 
ofpractitioners ofthe alien's occupation. 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(ii). Consultation with an appropriate 
U.S . peer group (which could include a person or persons with expertise in the field), labor, and/or 
management organization regarding the nature of the work to be done and the alien's qualifications is 
mandatory before a petition for an 0-1 or 0-2 classification can be approved. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2( o )(5)(i)(A). Evidence of a consultation shall be in the form of a written advisory opinion from 
a peer group (which could include a person or persons with expertise in the field), labor, and/or 
management organization with expertise in the specific field involved. 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(5)(i)(B). 
The petitioner shall obtain a written advisory opinion from a peer group (which could include a person 
or persons with expertise in the field), labor, and/or management organization with expertise in the 
specific field involved, and the advisory opinion shall be submitted when the petition is filed. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2( o )(5)(i)(C). Written evidence of consultation shall be included in the record in every approved 
0 petition. 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(5)(i)(D). Consultation with a peer group in the area of the alien's 
ability (which may include a labor organization), or a person or persons with expertise in the area of 
the alien's ability, is required in an 0-1 petition for an alien of extraordinary ability, and a consulting 
organization may also submit a letter of no objection in lieu of the above if it has no objection to the 
approval of the petition. 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(5)(ii)(A). 
At initial filing, the Petitioner did not provide the required consultation or written advisory opinion for 
the Beneficiary. SCOPS advised the Petitioner of this deficiency in a request for evidence. 
Specifically, SCOPS stated: 
You indicated that no appropriate peer group/labor organization exists for the field of 
archaeology. Please note, a U.S. peer group may include a person or persons with 
expertise in archaeology in U.S. In support of this requirement, you provided multiple 
letters from individuals in Turkey. As an initial matter, only advisory opinions from 
experts in the U.S. may qualify as a U.S. peer group. The letters appear to indicate that 
the writers are not U.S. experts, thus they do not qualify as U.S. peer group. This 
requirement has not been met. 
In response, the Petitioner indicated: 
... [W]e have procured a no objection letter from Federation of Turkish American 
Associations [FT AA]. 
[FTAA] is the largest Turkish-American umbrella non-profit organization in the United 
States, founded in 1956. [FTAA] possesses extensive experience and insight in fields 
integrating both business practices and cultural heritage preservation, and it maintains 
a robust professional network. This aligns with the provision that permits consulting 
organizations to issue a letter of no objection when there is no objection to the approval 
of the petition. 
In denying the petition, SCOPS determined that "[y Jou have not established that [FTAA] is in the field 
of archaeology," and "[y Jou have not established that [FTAA] is a person with expertise in the field 
of archaeology." 
On appeal, the Petitioner argues: 
[FTAA], despite its primary mission of unifying Turkish-American communities, has 
demonstrated considerable involvement in initiatives that bridge cultural and 
commercial spheres. Their extensive experience in these areas, coupled with a 
longstanding reputation as the largest Turkish-American umbrellas non-profit 
organization since 1956, substantiates their capacity to evaluate and affirm [the 
Beneficiary's] qualifications in her field. It is both impractical and unjust to limit the 
definition of "expertise in archaeology" solely to academic institutions or narrowly 
defined labor organizations, especially when the Beneficiary's professional activities 
and achievements clearly straddle the realms of both archaeology and business. The 
regulatory framework under 8 CFR 214.2( o )( 5) does not preclude consideration ofpeer 
group input from organizations that evaluate the Beneficiary's overall contributions in 
2 
her field. In light of her demonstratable business success and leadership-evidenced 
by the establishment of her U.S.-based company-the consultation letter should have 
been given full weight in the evaluation process. 
The regulation at 8 C.F .R. § 214.2( o) consistently requires "appropriate consulting entities" (8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2( o )(2)(ii)(D) ), "comprised of practitioners of the alien's occupation" (8 C.F .R. 
§ 214.2(o)(3)(ii)), "appropriate U.S. peer group" and "a person or persons with expertise in the field" 
(8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(5)(i)(A)), "a person or persons with expertise in the field" and "with expertise in 
the specific field involved" (8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(5)(i)(B)), "a person or persons with expertise in the 
field" and "with expertise in the specific field involved" (8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(5)(i)(C)), and "area of 
the alien's ability" and "a person or persons with expertise in the area of the alien's ability" (8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2(o)(5)(ii)(A)). 1 
Thus, the Petitioner must submit an appropriate consultation from a person or persons with expertise 
in the Beneficiary's field; in this case, expertise in archaeology or even archaeology business. Here, 
the Petitioner did not establish that the letter from G-A-, president ofFTAA, qualifies as a person with 
expertise in the field of archaeology or archaeology business. As pointed out by SCOPS, FTAA "is 
the largest Turkish-American umbrella non-profit organization in the United States, since 1956" and 
"[o]ur Federation represents nearly all of Turkish communities in the Northeastern United States Tri­
State area, since its formation have been a strong advocate in bringing Turkish-American communities 
together and have always played a role for security, prosperity and freedom." Although the letter 
claims that FT AA "possesses extensive experience and a deep understanding ofinterdisciplinary fields 
that encompass both business sector and cultural heritage," the letter does not show G-A- or FTAA 
possesses expertise in the Beneficiary's ability- archaeology or archaeology business. 
Because the Petitioner did not provide an appropriate consultation from a person with expertise in the 
Beneficiary's field of archaeology or archaeology business, the Petitioner did not demonstrate that 
SCOPS erred as a matter of law or policy. Accordingly, the Petitioner has not shown eligibility for 
the consulting requirements under 8 C.F.R. § 214.2( o ). Therefore, we need not determine whether the 
Beneficiary satisfies the requirements as an alien ofextraordinary ability, and we reserve those issues. 2 
The appeal will be dismissed for the above-stated reasons, with each considered as an independent 
and alternate basis for the decision. 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
