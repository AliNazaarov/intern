Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a chief architect ofdigital transformations, seeks classification as an alien ofextraordinary 
ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S .C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). 
This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner met only two 
initial evidentiary criteria, in which three must be satisfied. Specifically, SCOPS found the Petitioner 
fulfilled judging under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv) and roles under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii). On 
appeal, we determined the Petitioner met a third criterion relating to scholarly articles under 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(vi). Therefore, we conducted a final merits determination but concluded the Petitioner 
did not demonstrate that he is among the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. 
Accordingly, we dismissed the appeal. The matter is now before us on combined motions to reopen 
and reconsider. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the 
motions. 
I. Analysis 
A motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision was based on an incorrect application of 
law or policy and that the decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the record of proceedings 
at the time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103 .5(a)(3). A motion to reopen must state new facts and be 
supported by documentary evidence. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2). Because the scope ofa motion is limited 
to the prior decision, we will only review the latest decision in these proceedings. 8 C.F .R. 
§ 103 .5(a)(l)(i), (ii). We may grant motions that satisfy these requirements and demonstrate eligibility 
for the requested benefit. 
The Petitioner argues that our final merits determination "discounted [his] apex Salesforce architecture 
credential (CT A/ Architect Review Board)" and provides additional evidence regarding "the enormous 
size and economic footprint of the Salesforce ecosystem" and "the rigorous, board-style nature of the 
CTA/Architecture Review Board process." 
As indicated in our prior decision, the Petitioner presented evidence claiming that he is among 400 
people who possess Salesforce CTA. The record, for instance, contains screenshots from 
salesforceen.com stating that "[l]aunched in 2011, the CTA certification is, according to Salesforce, 
the 'pinnacle' of certifications," and screenshots from freepressjournal.in stating that CTA 
"designation is considered one of the most challenging and prestigious certifications within the 
Salesforce ecosystem as it is designed for professionals with extensive experience in Salesforce 
architecture." On motion, the Petitioner references various figures from his evidence, such as 
"Salesforce and its ecosystem would create 9.3 million jobs and $1.6 trillion in new business 
revenues," and projects that "[i]f CTA holders are approximately 400-500 globally and the ecosystem 
jobs figures is 9,300,000, then the CTA cohort is approximately 0.004%-0.005% of that workforce 
base" and "[i]f CTA holders are approximately 400-500 and Trailhead learners are 7,400,000, then 
CT As represent approximately 0.005%-0.007% of that learner base." 
However, the Petitioner did not demonstrate the significance of this credential in establishing him 
among that small percentage at the very top of his field. Although he may be among 400 - 500 people 
who possess Salesforce CTA, the Petitioner did not offer relevant context, such as how many people 
even pursue the credential or how many people never make it through the certification process. 
Moreover, the fact that, according to the Petitioner's own figures, his Salesforce CTA represents 
0.004% - 0.007% of the workforce does not necessarily indicate his standing in the field ifrecognition 
is limited within Salesforce, only applies to people who utilize Salesforce, or only a few apply for it, 
regardless of the review board process. 
Furthermore, the record includes screenshots from cio.com regarding 'Top 15 certifications for 
enterprise architects," in which Salesforce CT A is limited among them. However, the article does not 
indicate the prestigious nature of these certifications rather than "certifications are a great way to 
validate your enterprise architecture" and "one or more of these 15 certifications will help strengthen 
your resume." The Petitioner did not demonstrate that his Salesforce CTA places him among that very 
top ofhis field as opposed to being better qualified as an enterprise architect. In addition, the Petitioner 
did not show how his Salesforce CTA differentiates from the other 14 certifications or how he 
compares himself to other individuals in his field who are viewed among the upper echelon that have 
any of these certifications, including the Salesforce CT A. 
Without additional information and further context, considering his Salesforce CTA both individually

petitioner to present more extensive documentation than that required for lesser classifications. 56 FR 
30703-01, 30704. Here, the majority of the Petitioner's personal and professional accomplishments

A petition for an alien of extraordinary ability must be accompanied by evidence that the alien has 
sustained national or international acclaim and that his or her achievements have been recognized in 
the field of expertise. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). In determining whether an individual has enjoyed 
"sustained" national or international acclaim, USCIS should consider that such acclaim must be 
maintained. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(A)(l), https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
According to Black's Law Dictionary (11th ed. 2019), the definition of sustain is "to support or 
maintain, especially over a long period of time ... To persist in making ( an effort) over a long oeriod 
of time." Id. Although he received Salesforce CTA in 2020 and has worked atl ]from 
2013 - 2021 and atl Ifrom 2024 - present, the Petitioner did not demonstrate that his relative 
short personal and professional achievements immediately preceding the filing of the petition 
represent a career of acclaimed work. An alien can qualify on the basis of a career of acclaimed work 
in the field. 1990 U.S.C.C.A.N. 6710, 6739.

The record as a whole, including the evidence discussed above, does not establish the Petitioner's 
eligibility for the benefit sought. 1 Here, the Petitioner seeks a highly restrictive visa classification, 
intended for individuals already at the top of their respective fields, rather than those progressing 
toward the top. Matter ofPrice, 20 I&N Dec. 953,954 (Assoc. Comm'r 1994) (concluding that even 
major league level athletes do not automatically meet the statutory standards for classification as an 
individual of "extraordinary ability,"); Amin v. Mayorkas, 24 F.4th at 394 (stating that extraordinary 
ability is such an elite level of accomplishment); Lapteva v. Schuurmans, No. 8:24CV83, 2025 WL 
815087, at *1 (D. Neb. Mar. 10, 2025) (stating that EB-1 visa are intended to be available to only an 
exceedingly small amount of individuals with extraordinary ability). In this case, the Petitioner has 
not shown the significance of his work is indicative of the required sustained national or international 
acclaim or it is consistent with a career of acclaimed work in the field. See Section 203(b)(l)(A) of 
the Act. While the Petitioner need not establish that there is no one more accomplished to qualify for 
the classification sought, the record is insufficient to demonstrate that he is among the small percentage 
at the top of his field. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
IL Conclusion 
On motion, the Petitioner has not demonstrated that our previous decision was based on an incorrect 
application of law or policy at the time we issued our decision. Further, although he has submitted 
additional evidence in support of the motion, the Petitioner has not established eligibility. Therefore, 
the motion will be dismissed. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
ORDER: The motion to reopen is dismissed. 
FURTHER ORDER: The motion to reconsider is dismissed.
