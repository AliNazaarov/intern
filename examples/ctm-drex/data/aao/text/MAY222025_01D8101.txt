Form 1-129, Petition for a Nonimmigrant Worker (Extraordinary Ability- 0) 
The Petitioner, a restaurant and bar, seeks to classify the Beneficiary as a mixologist of extraordinary 
ability. To do so, the Petitioner pursues 0-1 nonimmigrant classification, available to individuals who 
can demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and 
whose achievements have been recognized in the field through extensive documentation. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 101(a)(l5)(O)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(O)(i). 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that in a totality determination the 
Petitioner did not establish the Beneficiary's sustained national or international acclaim, he is one of 
the small percentage who has risen to the very top of the field of endeavor, and his achievements have 
been recognized through extensive documentation. The matter is now before us on appeal. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter ofChristo 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 101 ( a)(l 5)( o )(i) of the Act establishes 0-1 classification for an individual who has 
extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics, which has been 
demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim, whose achievements have been 
recognized in the field through extensive documentation, and who seeks to enter the United States to 
continue work in the area of extraordinary ability. See also 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(l)(i) and (ii)(A)(l). 
As relevant here, the regulations provide separate and distinct 0-1 categories: 1) O-lA - extraordinary 
ability in the field of science, education, business, or athletics, and 2) O-lB - extraordinary ability in 
the field of arts. Extraordinary in the field of science, education, business, or athletics means a level 
of expertise indicating that the person is one of that small percentage who has arisen to the very top of 
the field of endeavor. 8 C.F.R. § 214.2( o )(3)(ii). Extraordinary ability in the field of arts means 
distinction, which means a high level of achievement in the field of arts evidenced by a degree of skill 
and recognition substantially above that ordinarily encountered to the extent that a person described 
as prominent is renowned, leading, or well-known in the field of arts. Id. Moreover, arts include any 
field of creative activity or endeavor such as, but not limited to, fine arts, visual arts, culinary arts, and 
performing arts. Id. 
In order to qualify for O-lA, an alien of extraordinary ability in the fields of science, education, 
business, or athletics must demonstrate sustained national or international acclaim and recognition for 
achievements in the field of expertise by providing evidence of either a receipt of a major, 
internationally award, such as the Nobel prize; or at least three of eight forms of documentation, such 
as awards, memberships, published material, judging, contributions, authorships, capacities, and 
salary. 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(A) and (B)(l)-(8). 
In order to qualify for O-lB, an alien of extraordinary ability in the field of arts must be recognized as 
being prominent in his or her field of endeavor as demonstrated by either evidence that the alien has 
been nominated for, or has been the recipient of: significant national or international awards or prizes 
in the particular field such as an Academy Award, an Emmy, or a Director's Guild; or at least three of 
six forms of documentation, such as past and prospective services in productions, national or 
international recognition, past and prospective roles for organizations, commercial or critically 
acclaimed successes, significant recognition, and salary. 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iv)(A) and (B)(l)­
(6). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner filed Form I-140, Petition for a Nonimmigrant Worker, indicating on the O and P 
Classifications Supplement to Form 1-129 that it sought to classify the Beneficiary as an "O-lA 
Beneficiary ofextraordinary ability in sciences, education, business or athletics (not including the arts, 
motion picture or television industry)." 1 Moreover, the Petitioner's accompanying letter described the 
Beneficiary's eligibility, referencing documentation, for four of the criteria under 8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2( o )(3)(iii)(B)(l )-(8) as an alien of extraordinary in the fields of science, education, business, or 
athletics (O-lA). 
SCOPS issued a request for evidence stating: 
According to the evidence, you intend to hire the beneficiary as a Mixolofst. You also 
provided evidence on the beneficiary's profession as a business owner of IThe 
evidence in the record is insufficient to establish that the beneficiary's career as a 
business owner ofi Isufficiently shares skillsets, knowledge, or expertise with 
that of a Mixologist. Therefore, the record does not establish how evidence of the 
beneficiary's business ownership demonstrates sustained national or international 
acclaim as a Mixologist.

In response, the Petitioner indicated: 
To be absolutely clear, the Beneficiary's field of endeavor is MIXOLOGY, that is, he 
is an expert MIXOLOGIST considered to be, among the best in his country, Armenia. 
In this regard, the Petition is filed under the O-lB (Arts) Classification. Without a

At the crux of the definition for Arts are the words "field of creative activity," which 
means that any endeavor, pursuit or profession that are preeminently involves creation 
or creative thinking could qualify as an art. By this metric, a mixologist is, 
undoubtedly, an artist considering that the practice involves the creation of mixing 
drinks, be it alcoholic or nonalcoholic or both with other ingredients. True enough, 
mixology is defined as "the art or skill of preparing mixed drinks." 
Although the Petitioner attempted to change the Beneficiary's eligibility classification from an O-lA 
to an O-lB, SCOPS adjudicated the petition based on the Petitioner's original classification claim as 
an O-lA. Specifically, SCOPS determined that the Beneficiary satisfied three of the eight categories 
of evidence under 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(B)(l)-(8). However, SCOPS concluded that the 
Petitioner did not show the Beneficiary's sustained national or international acclaim through an 
analysis of the totality of the evidence. 2 
We agree with the Petitioner that the Beneficiary's field of endeavor is mixology and falls within "the 
arts" of the O-lB classification. However, as indicated above, the Petitioner filed Form 1-140 seeking 
to classify the Beneficiary as an O-lA, specifically claiming his eligibility for the categories of 
evidence under 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(B)(l)-(8). Although the Petitioner attempted to change the 
Beneficiary's classification from O-lA to O-lB and claimed the Beneficiary's eligibility for criteria 
under 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iv)(B)(l)-(6), USCIS will only consider the visa classification that the 
petitioner annotates on the petition at initial filing. The affected party has the burden of proof to 
establish eligibility for the requested benefit at the time of filing the benefit request and continuing 
until the final adjudication. 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l); see also Matter ofKatigbak, 14 I&N Dec. 45, 49 
(Comm'r 1971) (providing that "Congress did not intend that a petition that was properly denied 
because the beneficiary was not at that time qualified be subsequently approved at a future date when 
the beneficiary may become qualified under a new set of facts"); Brazil Quality Stones, Inc., v. 
Chertoff, 286 Fed. Appx. 963, 2008 WL 2743927 (9th Cir. July 10, 2008) (determining that once 
USCIS concludes that an alien is not eligible for the specifically requested classification, the agency 
is not required to consider, sua sponte, whether the alien is eligible for an alternate classification). 
Furthermore, even though SCOPS adjudicated the categories of evidence and conducted a totality 
analysis under O-lA classification, the Petitioner did not demonstrate that the Beneficiary's field of

III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner did not demonstrate the Beneficiary's eligibility for O-lA classification as an alien of 
extraordinary ability in the sciences, education, business, or athletics. The appeal will be dismissed for 
the above-stated reasons, with each considered as an independent and alternate basis for the decision.
