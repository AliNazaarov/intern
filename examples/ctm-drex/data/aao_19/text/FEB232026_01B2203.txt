Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a business executive in the pomegranate industry, seeks classification as an individual 
of extraordinary ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. 
§ 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can 
demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose 
achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not 
establish that he meets the initial evidence requirements for this classification, either through his 
receipt of a major internationally recognized award or by satisfying at least three of the ten evidentiary 
criteria set forth in the regulations. SCOPS further determined the Petitioner did not establish that his 
entry to the United States would offer substantial prospective benefits to the country. We dismissed a 
subsequent appeal. The matter is now before us on a combined motion to reopen and reconsider. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the 
motions.

A motion to reopen must state new facts and be supported by documentary evidence. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.5(a)(2). A motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision was based on an incorrect 
application of law or policy and that the decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the record of 
proceedings at the time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). Because the scope of a motion is 
limited to the prior decision, we will only review the latest decision in these proceedings. 8 C.F.R. § 
103.5(a)(l)(i), (ii). We may grant motions that satisfy these requirements and demonstrate eligibility 
for the requested benefit. See Matter of Coelho, 20 I&N Dec. 464, 473 (BIA 1992) (requiring that 
new evidence have the potential to change the outcome). 
On motion to reopen, the Petitioner submits documentation previously submitted. He does not state 
new facts related to the evidence of record. Because the Petitioner has not submitted new facts that 
that would warrant reopening of the proceedings, he has not met the requirements for a motion to 
reopen. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2). The motion to reopen will be dismissed. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
Regarding the motion to reconsider, the Petitioner asserts that our dismissal of his motion to reopen 
"contained several legal and factual misunderstandings that, once clarified, will show that [he] meets 
the requirements of the classification sought." He presents several descriptions of what he refers to as 
"legal and factual errors" that form the basis of his motion. Upon review, we conclude that the 
Petitioner has not met the requirements for a motion to reconsider. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). 
It is not clear how some of his descriptions of purported error refer to the latest decision in these 
proceedings. For example, the Petitioner cites the two-step process outlined in Matter ofKazarian, 3 
which involves a final merits determination if a petitioner has met initial evidence requirements, 
contending that "AAO merged these two stages" and "heightened the evidentiary burden ...." 
However, because the Petitioner did not establish that he had met at least three evidentiary criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x), our dismissal of his motion to reopen did not include a final merits 
determination. The Petitioner also contends that the decision did not fully evaluate evidence 
concerning whether he met the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v), which concerns contributions of 
major significance to the field. Our latest decision, however, did not discuss whether he met this 
criterion, as it was reserved in our earlier dismissal of the Petitioner's appeal. The Petitioner also 
asserts that we gave "limited consideration" to letters from individuals with knowledge of his work, 
although our latest decision did not include discussion of eligibility criteria related to any letters in the 
record. Further, the Petitioner references the comparable-evidence provision at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.S(h)( 4), stating that "AAO' s failure to apply this flexibility constitutes a legal error that warrants

The Petitioner makes an additional contention concerning whether he met the criterion at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(iii), which does appear to relate to our latest decision. He asserts that "AAO discounted 
several materials that were clearly relevant to the regulatory criterion for published material," but he 
does not identify this material or discuss how we erred in evaluating the record. Instead, he cites a 
U.S. district court decision5 that he states "confirmed that publications targeted to the professional 
audience in the field are valid under this criterion" and concludes, "The decision's restrictive reading 
of what constitutes qualifying material was inconsistent with that authority." U.S. district court 
decisions, however, do not bind USCIS in other matters. 6 Regardless, the Petitioner does not explain 
how we erred in evaluating material in the record related to the criterion for published material. 
Because the Petitioner does not explain how our most recent dismissal of his motion to reopen was 
based on an incorrect application oflaw or policy, nor does he explain how the decision was incorrect 
based on the evidence in the record of proceedings at the time of the decision, he has not met the 
requirements for a motion to reconsider. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). Accordingly, the motion to 
reconsider will be dismissed. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
ORDER: The motion to reopen is dismissed. 
FURTHER ORDER: The motion to reconsider is dismissed.
