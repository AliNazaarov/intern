Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner - a biophysical chemist specializing in nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, 
membrane proteins, and polymer nanodiscs - requests classification under the employment-based, 
first-preference (EB-1) immigrant visa category as an alien with "extraordinary ability." See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b )(1 )(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b )(1 )(A). Successful 
petitioners for U.S. permanent residence in this category must demonstrate "sustained national or 
international acclaim" and extensively document recognition of their achievements in their fields. See 
section 203(b )( 1 )(A)(i) of the Act. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition. SCOPS concluded that the Petitioner met 
two of ten initial evidentiary requirements for the requested category- one less than needed for a final 
merits determination. On appeal, be contends that SCOPS erred by finding that he did not demonstrate 
that his original contributions in his field bad "major significance." 
The Petitioner bears the burden of demonstrating eligibility for the requested benefit by a 
preponderance of the evidence. Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). 
Exercising de novo appellate review, see Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 l&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 
2015), we find that be satisfied the requisite three evidentiary criteria. We will therefore withdraw 
SCOPS' decision and remand the matter for a final merits determination and entry of a new decision 
consistent with the following analysis. 
I. LAW 
To qualify for this immigrant visa category, a petitioner must demonstrate that they: 
• Have "extraordinary ability" in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics; 
• Seek to continue work in their field of expertise in the United States; and 
• Would substantially benefit the country as a permanent resident. 
Section 203(b )(1 )(A)(i-iii) of the Act. 
The term "extraordinary ability" means expertise commensurate with "one of that small percentage 
who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
Unless a petitioner received "a major, international recognized award," they must satisfy at least three 
of ten alternate evidentiary criteria. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). 1 If a petitioner meets either evidentiary 
standard and the requirements at sections 203(b)(1 )(A)(ii), (iii) of the Act, U.S. Citizenship and 
Immigration Services (USCIS) must then make a final merits determination. To merit final approval, 
the record - as a whole - must establish a petitioner's "sustained national or international acclaim" 
and recognized achievements placing them among the small percentage at their field's very top. See 
Amin v. Mayorkas, 24 F.4th 383, 391 (5th Cir. 2022) (finding USCIS' two-step review process to be 
"consistent with the governing statute and regulation"); see generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B), 
www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
II. ANALYSIS 
A. The Petitioner and His Field 
Indian universities awarded the Petitioner multiple academic degrees. He has a bachelor of science in 
mathematics, physics, and chemistry and a master of science and doctorate in chemistry. He now 
works as a scientist for a U.S. university, developing polymer-based nanodiscs for use in nuclear 
magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy to benefit drug development. At the time of the petition's 
filing, he had authored 45 scholarly articles in scientific journals and peer-reviewed at least 32 articles 
by other researchers in his field. He claims to have "revolutionized" NMR spectroscopy by developing 
polymer-based nanodiscs that can stabilize and isolate membrane proteins for study. 
The record does not indicate - nor does the Petitioner claim - his receipt of a major internationally 
recognized award. He therefore must meet at least three of the ten initial evidentiary criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i-x). 
The record supports SCOPS' findings that the Petitioner successfully submitted evidence of his: 
• Participation as a judge of others' work in his field; and 
• Authorship of scholarly articles in the field. 
See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv), (vi). 
On appeal, the Petitioner contends that SCOPS erred in finding that he did not meet the evidentiary 
criterion for original contributions of major significance in his field. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v). 
To satisfy this requirement, he must objectively meet the parameters of the applicable regulatory 
description. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B). "[N]either USCIS nor [the] AAO 
[ Administrative Appeals Office] may unilaterally impose novel substantive or evidentiary 
requirements beyond those set forth at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5." Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1121 
(9th Cir. 20 l0).

B. Original Contributions of Major Significance 
This criterion requires "[e]vidence of the alien's original scientific, scholarly, artistic, athletic, or 
business-related contributions of major significance in the field." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v). 
When adjudicating this requirement, USCIS first determines whether a petitioner made original 
contributions in their field. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.(2)(B)(l ), Criterion Five. If so, 
USCIS then considers whether the contributions are of major significance to the field. Id. 
[P]ublished research that has provoked widespread commentary on its importance from 
others working in the field, and documentation that it has been highly cited relative to 
others' work in that field, may be probative of the significance of the person's 
contributions to the field of endeavor. 
Id. Also, "evidence that the person developed a patented technology that has attracted significant 
attention or commercialization may establish the significance of the person's original contribution to 
the field." Id. 
The Petitioner submitted evidence of original contributions to his field, including: 45 peer-reviewed 
research articles, which other researchers cited 1,454 times; three patents; and one patent application 
publication. SCOPS, however, found insufficient evidence that these original contributions had major 
significance in his field. 
The record does not support SCOPS' determination. SCOPS primarily erred by discounting the 
support letters the Petitioner submitted from other researchers in his field. 
Under USCIS policy: 
Detailed letters from experts in the field explaining the nature and significance of the 
person's contribution may ... provide valuable context for evaluating the claimed 
original contributions of major significance, particularly when the record includes 
documentation corroborating the claimed significance. 
6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)( 1 ), Criterion Five. Documentary evidence should corroborate expert 
letters, and they should specifically explain why witnesses believe a petitioner has extraordinary 
ability. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(3). "Letters that merely reiterate USCIS' 
definitions relating to this classification or make general and expansive statements regarding the 
[petitioner] and the[ir] accomplishments are generally not persuasive." Id. 
Here, the support letters that the Petitioner submitted do not merely reiterate USCIS definitions or 
make general or expansive statements about him. Rather, the letters specify why the researchers 
believe the Petitioner has extraordinary ability in their field. The letters describe the Petitioner's 
development of novel polymer-based nanodiscs allowing for the study of membrane protein 
dysfunctions, which are leading causes of disorders such as Alzheimer's disease, cystic fibrosis, and 
type-2 diabetes. Research articles and patents in the record corroborate the researchers' statements. 
The letters explain that the Petitioner developed novel non-ionic charge-free nanodiscs able to extract 
3 
membrane proteins. A U.S. university professor of chemical and biochemical engineering who 
worked with the Petitioner during his post-doctoral tenure stated: 
[The Petitioner]'s research provides a stable medium for membrane proteins to be 
isolated for experimentation, and his work in expanding the environmental tolerances 
of polymer-based nanodiscs facilitates an enhanced range of proteins that are able to 
be extracted for study. In this way, he has extended the research community's horizons 
on the possible applications ofNMR imaging techniques in molecular investigations. 
An independent researcher, a U.S. university professor of chemistry and biochemistry, stated: 
[The Petitioner]' s research advancements provided researchers with novel tools for the 
study of this little understood class of proteins and revolutionized biophysical 
chemistry in the process.... Through [his] research, substantial progress is made in 
both the basic molecular understanding of these proteins and in their application for 
providing biomarkers for drng discovery research and translational clinical medicine. 
Another independent researcher, a Belgian university professor of chemical biology, stated that the 
Petitioner's "novel development of polymer-based nanodiscs has revolutionized the study of 
membrane proteins." 
These detailed, corroborated letters demonstrate that the Petitioner's research had major significance 
in his field. See 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), Criterion Five. ("Detailed letters from experts in 
the field explaining the nature and significance of the person's contribution may ... provide valuable 
context for evaluating the claimed original contributions of major significance, particularly when the 
record includes documentation corroborating the claimed significance.") Evidence of the Petitioner's 
high citation rate and the commercialization of his patented polymers provide additional support for 
this conclusion. 
The Petitioner submitted evidence of original contributions of major significance in his field. We will 
therefore withdraw SCOPS' contrary finding. 
C. The Final Merits Determination 
The Petitioner has met the requisite three of ten initial evidentiary requirements. Thus, USCIS must 
now make a final merits determination on his petition. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). SCOPS did not 
make a final merits determination. Rather than do so in the first instance, we will remand the matter. 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner submitted evidence of original contributions of major significance in his field. USCIS 
must now make a final merits determination.
