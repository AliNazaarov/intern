Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an airline pilot and aviation consultant, seeks classification under the employment­
based, first-preference (EB-1) immigrant visa category as an alien with "extraordinary ability." See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b )(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b )(l)(A). Successful 
petitioners for U.S. permanent residence in this category must demonstrate "sustained national or 
international acclaim" and extensively document recognition of their achievements in their fields. 
Section 203(b )(1 )(A)(i) of the Act. 
U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner 
did not meet the requested category's initial evidentiary requirements. On appeal, we affirmed the 
decision of the Director of the Texas Service Center. See In Re: 37346480 (AAO Apr. 8, 2025). The 
matter returns to us on the Petitioner's combined motions to reopen and reconsider. He contends that 
we misapplied law and disregarded evidence. 
The Petitioner bears the burden of demonstrating eligibility for the requested benefit by a 
preponderance of the evidence. Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon 
review, we conclude that he has not satisfied the category's initial evidentiary requirements. We will 
therefore dismiss the motions. 
I. LAW 
A motion to reopen must state new facts , supported by documentary evidence. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2). 
In contrast, a motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision misapplied law or policy 
based on the record at the time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). On motion, regulations limit 
our scope of review to the prior decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103 .5(a)(l)(i), (ii) (referring to "the prior 
decision" and "the latest decision in the proceeding"). We may grant motions that satisfy these 
requirements and demonstrate eligibility for the requested benefit. 
TI. ANALYSIS 
A. The Evidentiary Criteria 
Unless a petitioner documents their receipt of "a major, international recognized award," see 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3), they must submit documentation meeting at least three of the ten evidentiary 
requirements at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i-x). 1 Our prior decision found that the Petitioner did not 
satisfy any of the requirements that he claimed. On motion, he submits additional evidence and 
continues to maintain that he meets the following three evidentiary criteria: 
• Membership in associations in his field that require outstanding achievements of their 
members, as judged by recognized national or international experts; 
• Performance in a leading or critical role for organizations with distinguished reputations; and 
• Commandment of a high salary or significantly high remuneration in relation to others in his 
field. 
See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii), (viii), (ix). 
To meet a requirement, the Petitioner's evidence must objectively satisfy the parameters of the 
applicable regulatory description. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B), www.uscis.gov/ 
policy-manual. "[N]either USCIS nor [the] AAO [Administrative Appeals Office] may unilaterally 
impose novel substantive or evidentiary requirements beyond those set forth at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5." 
Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1121 (9th Cir. 2010) (citation omitted). 
l. Membership in Associations 
This criterion requires "[d]ocumentation of the alien's membership in associations in the field for 
which classification is sought, which require outstanding achievements of their members, as judged 
by recognized national or international experts in their disciplines or fields." 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(ii). 
The Petitioner previously submitted a copy of his airline transport pilot certificate from the U.S. 
Federal Aviation Administration (FAA). Our prior decision found that he did not demonstrate that the 
certificate reflects membership in the FAA or required outstanding achievements as judged by experts 
to obtain. 
On motion, the Petitioner acknowledges that the FAA is a government regulatory agency, not a 
professional organization. But he asks us to accept his materials as "comparable evidence" meeting 
this criterion. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4). He argues that the membership-in-associations criterion 
does not readily apply to his occupation "due to the unique strncture of the commercial aviation 
industry - where elite professional standing is conferred almost exclusively through rigorous federal 
licensure rather than traditional association membership."

evidence" to establish their eligibility. 8 C.F.R. § 204.S(h)( 4).

The Petitioner, however, raises his comparable-evidence argument in these proceedings for the first 
time. "A motion to reconsider based on a legal argument that could have been raised earlier in the 
proceedings will be denied." Matter ofO-S-G-, 24 I&N Dec. 56, 58 (BIA 2006). Also, the Petitioner's 
comparable-evidence argument exceeds the scope ofour review, which is limited to the issues decided 
in our prior decision. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(l)(i), (ii) (referring to "the prior decision" and "the 
latest decision in the proceeding"). 
Further, even if we could consider the Petitioner's comparable-evidence argument, he has not 
submitted evidence of the purported inapplicability of the membership-in-associations criterion to his 
occupation. See, e.g., Krasniqi v. Dibbins, 558 F. Supp. 3d 168, 190 (D.N.J. 2021) ("To avail herself 
of§ 204.5(h)(4), [the petitioner] needed to first show that one of the (h)(3) criteria was not readily 
applicable to her field.") Counsel asserts that the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii) does not readily 
apply to the Petitioner's occupation. But counsel's assertion is not evidence. See Matter of M-K-, 
29 I&N Dec. 556, 569 (BIA 2026) ("[S]tatements of counsel are not evidence.") The Petitioner also 
submits additional evidence regarding the purported "elite status" that his pilot certificate confers. But 
that evidence does not demonstrate, or even discuss, the purported inapplicability of the membership­
in-associations criterion to his occupation. Thus, the Petitioner has not established that he meets this 
evidentiary criterion through comparable evidence. 
2. The Remaining Claimed Evidentiary Criteria 
Even if the Petitioner satisfied the remaining two evidentiary requirements that he claims to meet, he 
would not qualify for the requested immigrant visa category. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) (requiring a 
petitioner to meet at least three evidentiary criteria). We therefore need not reach and hereby reserve 
consideration of his additional evidence and arguments regarding his purported performance in a 
leading or critical role for distinguished organizations and claimed commandment of a high salary or 
significantly high remuneration in relation to others in his field. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 
24, 25 (1976) (per curiam) (holding that agencies need not make "purely advisory findings" on issues 
unnecessary to their ultimate decisions). 
B. Final Merits Determination 
Because the Petitioner has met less than three initial evidentiary requirements, we also need not make 
a final merits determination on the petition. See Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. at 25. 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner's motions do not demonstrate his satisfaction of the requisite number of evidentiary 
requirements for the requested immigrant visa category. We will therefore affirm our appellate 
decision.
