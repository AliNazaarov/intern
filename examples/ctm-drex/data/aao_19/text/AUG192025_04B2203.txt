Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a scientific researcher, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not 
establish that she meets the initial evidence requirements for this classification, either through her 
receipt of a major internationally recognized award or by satisfying at least three of the ten evidentiary 
criteria set forth in the regulations. We dismissed an appeal, a subsequent motion to reopen and a 
motion to reopen and reconsider. The matter is now before us on a motion to reopen and reconsider. 
In our dismissal of the appeal, we determined that the Petitioner did not demonstrate that she had a 
one-time achievement under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), nor did she provide evidence sufficient to 
demonstrate that she met at least three of the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x); we therefore did 
not conduct a final merits analysis under Kazarian, reserving the issue. 1 
Because the scope of a motion is limited to the prior decision, we will only review the latest decision 
in these proceedings-our dismissal of the motion to reopen and reconsider. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.5(a)(l)(ii). 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter of Chawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the 
combined motion.

In her prior motion, the Petitioner reasserted her eligibility by c1tmg previous arguments and

authored which was published several months after the denial of her petition. We informed the 
Petitioner that we could not consider this evidence because evidence submitted on motion to reopen

a motion to reopen. 8 C.F.R. § l 03.5(a)(2). The Petitioner did not establish that our previous decision 
was based on an incorrect application of law or policy at the time we issued our decision. Therefore, 
she did not meet the requirements for a motion to reconsider. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). We therefore 
dismissed the combined motion. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
The Petitioner's current motion is predicated on her alleged non-receipt of a notice informing her that 
her appeal had been transferred to the Administrative Appeals Office (AAO). The Petitioner correctly 
states that after she filed her appeal, SCOPS had forty-five days to conduct an initial field review, and 
if the decision remained unfavorable, then SCOPS would forward the appeal to the AAO; and she 
would receive a Notice ofTransfer to the AAO. The Petitioner asserts that she never received a Notice 
ofTransfer. However, the Petitioner has not explained how this perceived procedural anomaly harmed 
her case, especially where the Notice of Transfer would only serve to inform the Petitioner that her 
case with sent to the AAO. Moreover, the Petitioner's recourse if she did not receive a Notice of 
Transfer to the AAO within seventy-five days of filing an appeal was to contact the United States 
Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) Contact Center by phone or through e-mail. See 
generally AAO Practice Manual, Ch. 6.2, https://www.uscis.gov/administrative-appeals/aao-practice­
manual; see also, USCIS policy memorandum PM-6-2-0124, Initial Field Review ofAppeals to the 
Administrative Appeals Office 2 (Nov. 4, 2015), https://www.uscis.gov/legal-resources/policy­
memoranda. Regardless however, because the Petitioner did not raise this issue on appeal, we consider 
this argument waived.

Here, the Petitioner has not provided new facts to establish that we erred in dismissing the prior 
motion. Because the Petitioner has not established new facts that would warrant reopening of the 
proceedings, we have no basis to reopen our prior decision. We will not re-adjudicate the petition 
anew and, therefore, the underlying petition remains denied. 
The Petitioner requests that we contradict our prior decision despite our explanation ofwhy the record 
does not establish eligibility. The Petitioner has submitted a nearly identical brief making the same 
arguments as she did in her prior motion. The Petitioner's repeated claims do not show that we 
committed legal error, but rather only indicate that she disagrees with our decision. Because the 
Petitioner has not established that our previous decision was based on an incorrect application of law 
or policy at the time we issued our decision, the motion to reconsider will be dismissed. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.5(a)(3)-(4).
