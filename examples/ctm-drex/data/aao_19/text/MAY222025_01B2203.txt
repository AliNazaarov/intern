Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a business executive in the pomegranate industry, seeks classification as an individual 
of extraordinary ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. 
§ 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can 
demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose 
achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not 
establish that he meets the initial evidence requirements for this classification, either through his 
receipt of a major internationally recognized award or by satisfying at least three of the ten evidentiary 
criteria set forth in the regulations. SCOPS further determined the Petitioner did not establish that his 
entry to the United States would offer substantial prospective benefits to the country. We dismissed a 
subsequent appeal. The matter is now before us on combined motions to reopen and reconsider. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the 
motion.

A motion to reopen must state new facts and be supported by documentary evidence. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.5(a)(2). Because the scope of a motion is limited to the prior decision, we will only review the 
latest decision in these proceedings. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(l)(i), (ii). We may grant motions that satisfy 
these requirements and demonstrate eligibility for the requested benefit. See Matter of Coelho, 20 
I&N Dec. 464, 473 (BIA 1992) (requiring that new evidence have the potential to change the 
outcome). 
On motion to reopen, the Petitioner submits documentation previously submitted, asserting that the 
evidence ofrecord establishes his eligibility for the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii) and (ix). The 
Petitioner's motion to reopen, however, focuses solely on his argument that our decision was 
erroneous based on the evidence of record; he does not identify new facts supported by probative 
evidence. Because the Petitioner has not submitted new facts that that would warrant reopening ofthe 
proceedings, he has not met the requirements for a motion to reopen. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2). The 
motion will be dismissed. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
A motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision was based on an incorrect application of 
law or policy and that the decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the record of proceedings 
at the time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). Because the scope of a motion is limited to the 
prior decision, we will only review the latest decision in these proceedings. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(l )(i), 
(ii). We may grant motions that satisfy these requirements and demonstrate eligibility for the requested 
benefit.

does not establish the Petitioner's eligibility under either 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii) or (ix).

On motion to reconsider, concerning whether the record contains evidence to establish that the 
Petitioner has commanded a high salary or other significantly high remuneration for services in 
relation to others in his field, the Petitioner reiterates arguments previously made on appeal and points 
to the evidence of record to establish eligibility. As explained in the dismissal of the Petitioner's 
appeal, the evidence does not demonstrate that the Petitioner has received high compensation or 
remuneration for services relative to others in comparable positions within his field for eligibility 
purposes under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix). 
In response to a request for evidence, the Petitioner explained that he earned a higher salary than other 
individuals within the agrarian industry in Azerbaijan for a period from 2019 to 2021. He submitted 
documentation indicating that his monthly salary for services rendered to the entity I I 
I las its "director, enterprise" was 6,133.90 manat from 
September 2019 to at least July 2021. The Petitioner also submitted data from the State Statistical 
Committee of the Republic of Azerbaijan for the average monthly salary of salaried employees in the 
field of agriculture, forestry, and fishing, showing average monthly salaries of 371.40 manat and 
433.50 manat for 2020 and 2021, respectively. As discussed in our appellate decision, it was not clear 
how the Petitioner's salary as a director-also described in the record as "chairman"-ofan enterprise 
was comparable to the average monthly salaries of all salaried employees in the field. The Petitioner 
did not offer any comparative data for managerial or executive positions in his field or industry. 
Without that evidence, he did not support his claim that he commanded a high salary in relation to 
others in the field. 
On motion, the Petitioner asserts that the dismissal of his appeal "does not specifically analyze why 
the official Azeri document related to the salaries of employees in Agriculture, Forestry, and Fishing 
is not probative enough and is insufficient to demonstrate the Petitioner's clearly very high salary as 
compared to his peers in the industry." However, as reiterated above, our appellate decision explained 
why the average salaries of workers in the Petitioner's field are not comparable to the Petitioner's 
earnings in a specific managerial role; all employees in the Petitioner's field cannot be considered 
collectively as the Petitioner's peers, as their roles and earnings are necessarily varied, and the 
Petitioner did not submit evidence of the salaries of other executives in roles comparable to his own. 
Indeed, on motion, the Petitioner acknowledges the inclusionary nature of the data that he submitted, 
stating the following: 
Moreover, the Azeri government's official statistical [data] encompassed all 
positions-including the executive ones-in its report. Hence, when comparing the 
Petitioner's income to the average income [in] Agriculture, Forestry, and Fishing, his 
remuneration is certainly high. 
The report does not differentiate between executive positions and others represented in the data. And 
although the Petitioner, throughout the record, has emphasized his leadership role, he has not 
submitted specific data for "his peers in the industry"-such as chairpersons, directors, or other 
executives-but for an average of individual salaries reflective of a wide swath of roles within his 
industry. This broad range of occupational data for an average of salaries for an entire industry is not

The Petitioner, on motion, also reiterates an argument based on guidance in the USCTS Policy Manual. 
The Petitioner references, in part, the following USCIS Policy Manual section: 
For entrepreneurs or founders of a startup business, officers should consider evidence
