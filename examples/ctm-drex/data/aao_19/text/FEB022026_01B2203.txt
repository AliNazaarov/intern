Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a chief executive officer, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S .C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding the record did not establish that 
the Petitioner had satisfied at least three of the initial evidentiary criteria for this classification, as 
required. The Petitioner subsequently filed combined motions to reopen and reconsider, which 
SCOPS dismissed, determining that he had not overcome the previously stated grounds for denial. 
The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3 . 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christa 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will withdraw the decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with the 
following analysis. 
I. LAW 
Immigrant visas are available to individuals with extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, 
business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim and 
whose achievements have been recognized in the field through extensive documentation. Section 
203(b)( 1 )(A) of the Act. These individuals must seek to enter the United States to continue work in 
the area of extraordinary ability and show that their entry will substantially benefit the United States. 
Id. The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who 
have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
The implementing regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a 
petitioner can demonstrate international recognition of the individual's achievements in the field 
through a one-time achievement in the form of a major, internationally recognized award. Or the 
pet1t10ner can submit evidence that meets at least three of the ten criteria listed at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x), including items such as awards, published material in certain media, and 
scholarly articles. If those standards do not readily apply to the individual's occupation, then the 
regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4) allows the submission of comparable evidence. 
Once a petitioner has met the initial evidence requirements, the next step is a final merits 
determination, in which we assess whether the record shows sustained national or international 
acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage at the very top of the field 
of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) (discussing a two-part review 
where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the required number of criteria, 
considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 
126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011).

Because the Petitioner has not indicated or shown that he has received a major, internationally 
recognized award, he must satisfy at least three of the alternate regulatory criteria at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). The Petitioner initially claimed to have satisfied eight of these criteria. In its 
decisions denying the petition and dismissing the Petitioner's motion, SCOPS determined that he 
satisfied two of the eight claimed criteria, relating to judging the work of others in the same or allied 
field and performing in leading or critical roles for organizations with a distinguished reputation. See 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv) and (viii). 
On appeal, the Petitioner maintains that he submitted evidence satisfying the other six claimed criteria 
relating to awards, memberships, published materials about him, original contributions, scholarly 
articles, and high salary or other significantly high remuneration. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(iii), 
(v), (vi) and (ix). He contends that SCOPS erred in its evaluation of the evidence, applied standards 
beyond what the regulations and U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) policy guidance 
require, and relied on "undisclosed research" in reaching its determinations regarding his eligibility 
under certain criteria. 
We agree with the Petitioner that SCOPS' latest decision is based, in part, on evidence reviewed by 
USCIS that was neither disclosed to the Petitioner prior to the issuance of the decision, nor 
incorporated into the record. If a decision will be unfavorable to the petitioner and is based on 
potentially derogatory information considered by USCIS and of which the petitioner is unaware, the 
petitioner must be advised of this fact and offered an opportunity to rebut the information before a 
decision is rendered. 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l6)(i). A determination ofstatutory eligibility shall be based

For example, the Petitioner claimed eligibility under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii) based on his 
membership in the Forbes Business Council. In evaluating his eligibility under this criterion, SCOPS 
advised that "USCIS reviewed data from the North American Industry Classification System" and 
then relied on that information to conclude that "millions of 'senior level executives' would be eligible 
for membership consideration for the Forbes Business Council," noting that "such inclusion does not 
denote outstanding achievement when millions of people are eligible for consideration." 
In determining whether the Petitioner had provided published material about him in professional or 
major trade publications under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii), SCOPS' decision introduced a definition 
of "professional magazine or journal" obtained from the website o~ land relied on 
that source to dismiss the Petitioner's claim that he satisfied this criterion. Further, SCOPS' discussion 
of the original contributions criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v) states that USCIS consulted

None of the evidence addressed above, or other independently researched information referenced in 
SCOPS' latest decision, was incorporated into the record or nor was the Petitioner put on notice that 
SCOPS would be considering such information. To ensure fair and complete consideration of the 
proceedings before us, it is necessary that the record of proceedings contains copies of all evidence 
considered. SCOPS bears the responsibility of ensuring that the record is complete and contains all 
evidence submitted by the Petitioner and any other evidence considered by SCOPS in reaching their 
decision. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l); cf Matter o_fGibson, 16 I&N Dec. 58, 59 (BIA 1976). 
In addition, review ofthe record and SCOPS' prior decisions indicates that SCOPS did not consider the 
Petitioner's request that USCIS consider comparable evidence with respect to certain evidentiary 
criteria. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4). Specifically, SCOPS did not evaluate whether the Petitioner's 
evidence of the presentation of his work at trade shows is of comparable significance to publication of 
scholarly articles and whether he presented evidence of highly valued equity holdings in his start-up 
company that are of comparable significance to the high salary criterion. See generally 6 USCIS Policy 
Manual F.2(B)(l), https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual (providing examples of potentially qualifying 
comparable evidence under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4)). 
Therefore, for the reasons discussed, we will withdraw SCOPS' decision and remand this matter. On 
remand, SCOPS should identify and incorporate any documents that may have been omitted from the 
record of proceeding, before reviewing the entire record, including the Petitioner's appeal, re­
evaluating the Petitioner's eligibility under the claimed evidentiary criteria at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) and 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4), and issuing a new decision. If the new decision will 
be based in part, on materials not submitted by the Petitioner, SCOPS should issue an RFE or NOID, 
granting the Petitioner a reasonable opportunity to respond, before issuing a new decision. 
ORDER: The decision is withdrawn. The matter is remanded for the entry of a new decision 
consistent with the foregoing analysis.
