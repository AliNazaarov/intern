Appeal of U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) Decision 
Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a chief executive, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability in 
business. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. 
§ l 153(b)(l)(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens 
who can demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim 
and whose achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
USCIS denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not establish either that he received a 
major, internationally recognized award, or that the record satisfied at least three of the 10 initial 
evidentiary criteria. The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 
203(b )(I) of the Act makes visas available to immigrants with extraordinary ability if: 
(i) the alien has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or 
athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained national or international 
acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, 
(ii) the alien seeks to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability, and 
(iii) the alien's entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively 
the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate sustained 
acclaim and the recognition of his or her achievements in the field through a one-time achievement 
(that is, a major, internationally recognized award). If that petitioner does not submit this evidence, 
then he or she must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the 10 
categories listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) (including items such as awards, published material 
in certain media, and scholarly articles). Ifa criterion at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) does not readily 
apply to a petitioner, he or she may submit comparable evidence to establish eligibility. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(4). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010).

A. Initial Evidentiary Criteria 
USCIS concluded that the record satisfies the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi) but not the criteria 
at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(ii)-(v), (vii)-(viii), noting that the Petitioner did not assert eligibility under 
the remaining criteria. On appeal, the Petitioner reasserts that the record satisfies the criteria at 
8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(ii)-(vi) and (viii), separately addressing the criterion at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(vii). For the reasons discussed below, the record does not satisfy at least three of the 
criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). 
1. Membership in Associations in the Field 
The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii) requires "[d]ocumentation of the alien's membership in 
associations in the field for which classification is sought, which require outstanding achievements of 
their members, as judged by recognized national or international experts in their disciplines or fields." 
In a request for evidence (RFE), USCIS acknowledged the record contained information regarding the 
Petitioner's membership in the Bensalem Business Association (BBA); however, USCIS informed the 
Petitioner that the record did not establish whether the BBA requires outstanding achievements of its 
members, and whether prospective members' achievements are judged by recognized national or 
international experts in their disciplines or fields. Although the Petitioner responded to the RFE, 
USCIS concluded that the Petitioner did not address the evidentiary deficiencies in his response and, 
therefore, that he abandoned his claim that the record satisfies this criterion, citing Sepulveda v. US. 
Att'y Gen., 401 F.3d 1226, 1228 n.2 (11th Cir. 2005); Hristov v. Roark, No. 09-CV-2731, 2011 WL 
4711885 at *9 (E.D.N.Y. Sept. 30, 2011).

On appeal, the Petitioner asserts that USCIS erred by not addressing evidence regarding this criterion 
submitted at the time he filed the Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers, regardless of 
whether he addressed the criterion in his RFE response. 
The record contains a letter, dated June 12, 2025, from the president of the BBA, stating that the 
Petitioner has been a member of the BBA "since 2025." The Petitioner also submitted a printed copy 
of the BBA website, which in relevant part explains: 
Participation is simple - with your paid dues, we will place your business information

your place ofbusiness, and a [BBA] decal to put in your window so that the community

Neither the BBA website excerpt nor the remainder of the record establishes that the BBA requires 
anything of prospective members other than that they pay dues. Likewise, neither the BBA website 
excerpt nor the remainder of the record indicates that nationally or internationally recognized experts 
in their disciplines or fields judge prospective members. Therefore, the record does not support the 
conclusion that the BBA qualifies as an association in the field for which classification is sought, 
which requires outstanding achievements of its members, as judged by recognized national or 
international experts in their disciplines or fields. The Petitioner does not assert that he is a member 
of any other association that may satisfy this criterion. 
The Petitioner has not established that he satisfies this criterion. 
2. Published Material About the Alien 
The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii) requires "[p]ublished material about the alien in 
professional or major trade publications or other major media, relating to the alien's work in the field 
for which classification is sought. Such evidence shall include the title, date, and author of the 
material, and any necessary translation." 
USCIS acknowledged that the record contains material published by Asia-Plus; however, USCIS 
observed that information in the record about Asia-Plus does not establish whether it qualifies among 
professional or major trade publications or other major media. Considering USCIS' analysis of this 
criterion in its entirety, although USCIS stated, "Based on the evidence submitted, this criterion has 
been met," this sentence appears to contain a typographical or scrivener's error in the form of an 
omitted word and, rather, USCIS appears to have intended it to read, "Based on the evidence 
submitted, this criterion has not been met." 
On appeal, the extent of the Petitioner's discussion of this criterion is, "Criteria [sic] met." For the 
following reasons, we withdraw USCIS' statement that this criterion has been met. 
The Petitioner stated that Asia-Plus published two articles about him, dated I 2013, and 
2025. Although the 2013 material bears a title, a date, and the identity of an individual 
claiming authorship of it, the 2025 material omits the identity of an individual author, as required by 
the plain language of the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii). Because the 2025 material does not

4. Authorship of Scholarly Articles in the Field 
The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi) requires "[e]vidence of the alien's authorship of scholarly 
articles in the field, in professional or major trade publications or other major media." Relevant factors 
for determining whether a publication qualifies among professional or major trade publications or 
other major media include the intended audience, for professional publications, and the circulation or 
readership relative to other media in the field, for major media. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, 
supra, at F.2(B)(l). A scholarly article should be written for learned persons in the field, meaning 
persons with profound knowledge of a field. See generally id. Examples of scholarly article 
authorship include, but are not limited to, publications in professionally relevant, peer-reviewed 
journals and published conference presentations at nationally or internationally recognized 
conferences. See generally id. 
The extent of USCIS' analysis of this criterion is a conclusory sentence that it has been met. We 
withdraw USCIS' determination that the record satisfies this criterion for the following reasons. 
The record establishes that the Petitioner authored six articles between 2017-21, published in the 
Faculty of International Economics' collected papers; the International Scientific and Practical 
Conference on Scientific Research and Modem Education proceedings; Journal ofCorporate Finance 
Research; Finance, Money, Investments; and Financial Economics. However, he did not clearly state 
whether he believes the respective publications qualify as professional publications, major trade 
publications, or other major media. Even if the Petitioner clarified whether he believes the respective 
publications are professional publications, major trade publications, or other major media, the record 
does not support such a conclusion. For example, the record does not establish what the respective 
publications' intended audiences are, nor does the record indicate whether the publications require 
peer review of articles prior to publication; therefore, the record does not establish whether any of the 
publications qualify as professional publications. 3 See generally id. In tum, the record does not

5. Artistic Displays 
The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii) requires "[e]vidence of the display of the alien's work in 
the field at artistic exhibitions or showcases." 
USCIS acknowledged that the record contains information regarding invitations for him to speak or 
present at events. However, USCIS observed that the record does not establish that the events in 
question were "artistic in nature" and that the Petitioner created or developed work being displayed at 
such events. Because the record does not establish that the events were artistic, USCIS concluded that 
the record does not satisfy the plain language requirements of this criterion. 
On appeal, the Petitioner "expressly acknowledges that the evidence submitted under this criterion 
does not satisfy the plain language requirements of 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii)" and that USCIS 
"correctly concluded that this criterion was not met." We agree. 
Because the record would not satisfy at least three of the 10 criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) 
even if the Petitioner met the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(v) and (viii), we reserve our opinion 
regarding those criteria. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (stating that agencies are not 
required to make "purely advisory findings" on issues that are unnecessary to the ultimate decision); 
Matter of L-A-C-, 26 I&N Dec. 516, 526 n.7 (BIA 2015) (declining to reach alternative issues on 
appeal where an applicant is otherwise ineligible). 
B. Final Merits Determination 
Because the record does not satisfy at least three of the 10 criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x), 
we need not conduct a final merits determination of whether the record shows sustained national or 
international acclaim and demonstrates that the Petitioner is among the small percentage at the very 
top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20 ( citing section 203(b )(1 )(A) of the 
Act; 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(2)-(3)). Nevertheless, we note that, for the following reasons, the record

does not establish the Petitioner has sustained4 national or international acclaim and that he is among 
the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. See id. 
The totality ofthe evidence in this case does not show that the Petitioner is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor, working as a CEO of a carpooling mobile application. For 
example, the record does not demonstrate how the Petitioner has accrued national or international 
acclaim in the field of his claimed extraordinary ability as a chief executive by virtue of becoming a 
member ofone local business association that requires only that members pay dues, nor does the record 
indicate that the Petitioner's one local business association membership places him among the small 
percentage at the very top As noted above, the I material cannot establishof the field. 2025 
eligibility and the 2013 material is not about the Petitioner but, even if the Petitioner had 
submitted two articles about him, the record does not contextualize them relative to the typical number 
and nature of articles written about those in the small percentage at the very top of the field. Although 
the Petitioner claims he judged the work of others, evidence he submitted indicates that he performed 
ordinary business administration duties and that he interned under the supervision of another 
individual; the record does not show that the Petitioner has garnered national or international acclaim 
by virtue of these activities, nor does it show how performing ordinary business administration duties 
or interning places him among the small percentage at the very top of the field of chief executive 
officers. 
We acknowledge that the Petitioner authored six articles in a five-year period; however, again, the 
record does not establish that he received national or international acclaim by virtue of publishing 
those articles, nor does he contextualize his articles with information regarding the typical number and 
nature of articles published by those at the very top of the field of endeavor. We also acknowledge 
that the Petitioner claims his role in distributing hygienic products throughout Tajikistan in 2020-21 
were leading or critical, and of major significance. However, hygienic product distribution, like the 
Petitioner's policy research work he discusses, appears to be attenuated from the work he proposes to 
undertake in the United States as a chief executive officer of a carpooling mobile application; 
therefore, his references to hygienic product distribution and policy research work do not show how 
he may be among the small percentage at the very top of a relevant field. 
For the foregoing reasons, the record does not establish that the Petitioner has sustained national or 
international acclaim, that his achievements have been recognized in the field of expertise, and that he 
is among the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 
1119-20 (citing section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act; 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(2)-(3)). 
III. CONCLUSION 
The evidence does not meet at least three ofthe 10 criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). Moreover, 
even ifthe evidence satisfied at least three ofthe 10 initial evidentiary criteria, the totality ofthe record 
does not support a conclusion that the Petitioner has established the acclaim and recognition required 
for the classification sought. See Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20. USCIS has long held that even 
athletes performing at the major league level do not automatically meet the "extraordinary ability"
