Appeal of U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) Decision 
Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. See Immigration and 
Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference 
classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens who can demonstrate their extraordinary 
ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been 
recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
The Director revoked the approval of the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not establish that 
he either received a major, internationally recognized award or satisfied at least three of the 10 initial 
evidentiary criteria. The Director further noted that, even if the Petitioner satisfied the initial 
evidentiary criteria, the record did not show that the Petitioner has sustained national or international 
acclaim and that he is among the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. The matter 
is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F .R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance ofthe evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will withdraw the decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with the 
following analysis. 
I. LAW 
Section 205 of the Act, 8 U.S.C. § 1155, states that USCIS may revoke the approval of a 
previously approved visa petition for "good and sufficient cause." This is defined as "where the 
evidence of record at the time the notice [ of intent to revoke] is issued, if unexplained and unrebutted, 
would warrant a denial of the visa petition based upon the petitioner's failure to meet his burden of 
proof." Matter ofHo, 19 I&N Dec. 582, 590 (BIA 1988); see also Matter ofEstime, 19 I&N Dec. 
19 I&N Dec. 450 (BIA 1987). 
Section 203(b )(1) of the Act makes visas available to immigrants with extraordinary ability if: 
(i) the alien has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or 
athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained national or international 
acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, 
(ii) the alien seeks to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability, and 
(iii) the alien's entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively 
the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate sustained 
acclaim and the recognition of their achievements in the field through a one-time achievement (that 
is, a major, internationally recognized award). If that petitioner does not submit this evidence, then 
they must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the 10 categories 
listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). If a criterion at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) does not readily 
apply to a petitioner, they may submit comparable evidence to establish eligibility. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(4). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010). 
II. ANALYSIS 
At the time the Petitioner filed the Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers, he claimed to 
meet the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i), (ii), (v), and (vi). The Director issued a request for 
evidence, and the Petitioner's response reasserted eligibility under the same four criteria. After 
initially approving the petition, the Director issued a notice of intent to revoke (NOIR), informing the 
Petitioner of deficiencies regarding the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i), (ii), (v), and (vi). In 
response, the Petitioner again asserted eligibility under the four initial criteria and he claimed for the 
first time that he also met the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(viii) and (ix). The Director concluded 
that the Petitioner satisfied only two criteria (the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi) and the newly 
claimed criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii)), and the Director revoked the Form 1-140 approval 
under section 205 of the Act. The Petitioner then appealed the revocation and he now asserts that he 
meets a total of seven criteria (including the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv), in addition to the 
six claimed in response to the NOIR). 
As explained above, good and sufficient cause to revoke an approval exists if "the evidence of record 
at the time the [NOIR] is issued, if unexplained and unrebutted, would warrant a denial of the visa

petition based upon the petitioner's failure to meet his burden of proof" Matter ofHo, 19 I&N Dec. 
at 590. That same decision limited its scope to the "aspects of the record that related to the stated 
ground for revocation." Id. at 584. Moreover, a revocation can be grounded upon, and a petitioner is 
obliged to respond to, only the allegations in the NOIR. Matter ofArias, 19 I&N Dec. 568 (BIA 
1988). And the regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 205.2(b) provides a petitioner the opportunity to offer 
evidence "in opposition to the grounds alleged for revocation of the approval" in the NOIR. 
Therefore, although the NOIR was properly issued, we conclude that the Director erred by addressing 
newly claimed criteria and a final merits determination in its revocation notice because those grounds 
were not included in the NOIR. 1 Accordingly, while we agree that the Petitioner has not established 
eligibility for the requested benefit, we will remand the matter to the Director to enter a new decision 
based on the grounds for revocation addressed in the NOIR and the Petitioner's response to those 
stated grounds. If the Director intends to revoke the approval under additional grounds not addressed 
in the NOIR, the Director should issue a new NOIR to provide the Petitioner an opportunity to explain 
or rebut them. See 8 C.F.R. §§ 205.2(b)-(c); Matter ofHo, 19 I&N Dec. at 590.
