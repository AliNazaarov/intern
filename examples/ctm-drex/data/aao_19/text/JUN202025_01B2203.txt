Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a visual artist, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference 
classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their extraordinary ability 
through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in 
their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not satisfy 
at least three initial evidentiary criteria. We dismissed a subsequent appeal, similarly concluding that 
the record does not satisfy at least three initial evidentiary criteria. We then dismissed a combined 
motion to reopen and motion to reconsider. The matter is now before us on a second combined motion 
to reopen and motion to reconsider. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the 
combined motion. 
I. ANALYSIS 
We incorporate by reference our analysis in our decisions on the appeal and on the prior combined 
motion to reopen and motion to reconsider. By way of summation, SCOPS determined that the record 
satisfies the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii), which requires "[e]vidence of the display of the 
alien's work in the field at artistic exhibitions or showcases." However, SCOPS determined that the 
record does not satisfy the other criteria under which the Petitioner claimed eligibility, specifically the 
criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i), (iii), (v), (viii)-(ix). In our decision on the Petitioner's appeal, 
we concluded that, in addition to satisfying the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii), the record also 
satisfies the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii), which requires "[p ]ublished material about the 
alien in professional or major trade publications or other major media, relating to the alien's work in 
the field for which classification is sought." However, similar to SCOPS, we determined that the 
record does not satisfy the other criteria under which the Petitioner had claimed eligibility with 
SCOPS, specifically the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i), (v), (viii)-(ix). 
The Petitioner then filed a combined motion to reopen and motion to reconsider, which we dismissed. 
We determined that, because the Petitioner did not state new facts and submit documentary evidence, 
the motion did not satisfy the requirements of a motion to reopen. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2). We 
also determined that, because the Petitioner did not establish that we misapplied a law or policy, the 
motion did not satisfy the requirements of a motion to reconsider. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). 
A. Motion to Reopen 
A motion to reopen "seeks a new hearing based on new or previously unavailable evidence." Matter 
of 0-S-G-, 24 I&N Dec. 56, 57-58 (BIA 2006) (citing Matter ofCerna, 20 I&N Dec. 399,403 (BIA 
1991)). A motion to reopen must state new facts and be supported by documentary evidence. 8 C.F .R. 
§ 103.5(a)(2). We may grant motions that satisfy these requirements and demonstrate eligibility for 
the requested benefit. See Matter ofCoelho, 20 I&N Dec. 464, 4 73 (BIA 1992) ( requiring that new 
evidence have the potential to change the outcome). 
Now, on a second motion to reopen, the Petitioner reasserts eligibility under the criterion at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(vi), "[ e ]vidence ofthe alien's authorship of scholarly articles in the field, in professional 
or major trade publications or other major media," as he asserted in the prior combined motion. 
However, as with the prior motion to reopen, the Petitioner does not submit documentary evidence in 
support of this motion. Rather, he states that "the evidence that [he] authored scholarly articles in the 
field existed at the time of the initial filing, and thus, should be considered new facts." More 
specifically, he indicates that "evidence was provided for this category with the petition as well as the 
[r]esponse to the [request for evidence (RFE)]" that SCOPS issued, although he concedes that he "did 
not argue [his] authorship of scholarly articles" satisfies the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi) until 
after SCOPS denied his Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers. 
As we indicated in our decision on the prior combined motion, the Petitioner's reference to information 
already in the record-which he submitted in support of his Form I-140 and in response to SCOPS' 
RFE-does not state new facts. Rather, it requests a new decision based on the record at the time of 
a prior decision, which is the purpose of a motion to reconsider. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3) (requiring 
a movant to establish that a prior decision was incorrect based on the evidence ofrecord at the time of 
the underlying decision). Because the Petitioner does not state new facts to be provided in the 
reopened proceeding, the motion does not satisfy the plain language requirements of a motion to 
reopen, there is no basis for reopening the proceeding, and the motion to reopen will be dismissed. 
See 8 C.F.R. §§ 103.5(a)(2), (4). 
B. Motion to Reconsider 
A motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision was based on an incorrect application of 
law or policy and that the decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the record of proceedings 
at the time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). Because the scope of a motion is limited to the 
prior decision, we will review only the latest decision in these proceedings. 8 C.F.R. §§ 103.5(a)(l)(i)­
(ii). 
On motion to reconsider, the Petitioner asserts that we imposed novel requirements "not supported by 
the Policy Manual guidelines or plain meaning of the regulations." Specifically, the Petitioner

references our "claim[] that the evidence 'does not discuss or indicate the recognition ofthe fellowship 
in the field,"' and he states that "nowhere in the Policy Manual or regulations does it require the 
Petitioner to submit such evidence to meet [the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)]." The Petitioner 
further states that "the fact that [thel ~ has significant press coverage in major 
media outlets such as Advocate" and Artnet establishes the fellowship's "national or international 
recognition," as required in part by the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i). The Petitioner also 
asserts that "federal case law" requires us to conclude that his "conferences and lecture seminars at 
the prominent universities and institutions, constitute 'authorship of scholarly articles in the field,'" 
contemplated by the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi). 
The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) requires "[d]ocumentation of the alien's receipt of lesser 
nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards for excellence in the field of endeavor." 
Stated another way, a petitioner must document receipt of prizes or awards, the prizes or awards must 
be nationally or internationally recognized, and the prizes or awards must have been for excellence in 
the field of endeavor. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i). 
The full sentence in our prior decision, to which the Petitioner objects in the motion to reconsider 
before us now, is as follows: "The evidence, for instance, does not discuss or indicate the recognition 
of the fellowship in the field or otherwise reflect the field's view of the fellowship as a nationally or 
internationally recognized prize or award." Thus, our reference to whether the record "discuss[es] or 
indicate[s] the recognition of the fellowship in the field" was an example of how the record may 
establish whether the prize or award in question is nationally or internationally recognized. Contrary 
to the Petitioner's assertions on motion to reconsider, the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) 
specifically requires petitioners to establish that prizes or awards in question are nationally or 
internationally recognized. Therefore, the Petitioner has not established how the selected fragment of 
one sentence in our decision misapplied the plain language ofthe criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i), 
when considered in its full context.

Next, regarding the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi), the Petitioner cites Gulen v. Chertoff, No. 
07-2148, 2008 U.S. Dist. Lexis 54607, 9-10 (E.D. Pa. July 16, 2008), which he summarizes as having 
concluded, "where the beneficiary's work was the 'subject of international conferences of scholars' 
the authorship of scholarly articles criterion was met." He also cites the USCTS Policy Manual, which 
similarly states that examples of scholarly article authorship include published conference 
3 
presentations at nationally or internationally recognized conferences. See generally 6 USCJS Policy 
Manual F.2(B)(l ), https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
Gulen v. Chertoff is an unpublished district court decision arising in a circuit other than the Second 
Circuit's jurisdiction, in which the Petitioner resides. In contrast to the broad precedential authority 
of the case law of a United States circuit court, we are not bound to follow the published decision of a 
United States district court, even in cases arising within the same district. See Matter ofK-S-, 20 I&N 
Dec. 715, 719-20 (BIA 1993). The reasoning underlying a district judge's decision will be given due 
consideration when it is properly before us; however, the analysis need not be followed as a matter of 
law. Id. In addition, as the published decisions of the district courts are not binding on us outside of 
that particular proceeding, the unpublished decision of a district court has even less persuasive value. 
Given that the Petitioner does not reside within the circuit with jurisdiction over the district court that 
issued the unpublished decision he references on motion to reconsider, it bears minimal persuasive 
value in this matter. 
Even if we were to consider Gulen as persuasive in this matter, the Petitioner overstates its holding. 
The full context of the district court's analysis is that the petitioner in that case authored theological, 
political science, and Islamic studies articles in a body of scholarly work that "is prominent on the 
syllabi ofgraduate and undergraduate courses at major American colleges and universities ... and has 
been the subject of international conferences of scholars," supporting the conclusion that he met the 
criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi). Gulen, 2008 U.S. Dist. Lexis 54607 at 9-10, supra. The 
Petitioner in this case does not establish that he has authored scholarly articles that are prominent on 
the syllabi ofgraduate and undergraduate courses at major American colleges and universities, in order 
to compare the facts of his case to those in Gulen. 
We acknowledge that the Policy Manual provides relevant information regarding the criterion at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi), when a claim of eligibility under that criterion is properly made. However, 
as we explained in our decision on the Petitioner's prior combined motion, we do not recognize the 
Petitioner's claim of eligibility under this criterion first raised on appeal because he had adequate 
notice and reasonable opportunity to raise this claim before SCOPS both at the time of filing the Form 
1-140 and in response to SCOPS' RFE, which he declined to do. The Petitioner conceded that he did 
not raise this claim of eligibility prior to SCOPS' decision, stating on appeal, "The Original Petition 
did not argue [the Petitioner satisfies the criterion of] authorship of scholarly articles." Although the 
Petitioner identifies a policy in the Policy Manual relevant to determining whether the record satisfies 
the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi), he does not identify a relevant law or policy we may have 
misapplied regarding our decision not to recognize his claim first raised on appeal. Because the 
Petitioner does not establish that we misapplied a law or policy regarding our decision not to recognize 
his claim of eligibility under the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi), the issue ofwhether the record 
satisfies that criterion is moot. 
Because the Petitioner does not establish that our prior decision was based on an incorrect application 
of law or policy, the motion does not satisfy the requirements of a motion to reconsider; therefore, it 
will be dismissed. See 8 C.F.R. §§ 103.5(a)(3)-(4).
