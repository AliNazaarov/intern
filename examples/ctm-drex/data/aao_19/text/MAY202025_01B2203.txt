Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a scientific researcher, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not 
establish that she meets the initial evidence requirements for this classification, either through her 
receipt ofa major internationally recognized award or by satisfying at least three of the ten evidentiary 
criteria set forth in the regulations. We dismissed an appeal and a subsequent motion to reopen. The 
matter is now before us on a combined motion to reopen and reconsider. 
In our dismissal of the Petitioner's appeal, we determined that she did not demonstrate that she had a 
one-time achievement under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), nor did she provide evidence sufficient to 
demonstrate that she met at least three of the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x); we therefore did 
not conduct a final merits analysis under Kazarian, reserving the issue. 1 
Because the scope of a motion is limited to the prior decision, we will only review the latest decision 
in these proceedings-our dismissal of the motion to reopen. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(l)(ii). 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproofto demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter of Chawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the 
combined motion. 
A motion to reopen must state new facts and be supported by documentary evidence.

incorrect application of law or policy and that the decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the

In our dismissal of the Petitioner's motion to reopen, we explained that while the Petitioner presented 
assertions concerning her eligibility, she did not identify evidence to support her claims. We also 
referenced our decision to dismiss her appeal, which explained that new evidence that she submitted 
concerning awards could not be considered on appeal. 2 Our motion decision further explained that 
this evidence would also not be considered on motion, and that a motion following an adverse decision 
on an appeal should not act as a vehicle to introduce new eligibility claims for the first time. Our 
dismissal also explained that the requirement for "new facts" on motion to reopen pertains to new 
information associated with the eligibility claims a filing party presented to us in their most recent 
filing. The Petitioner's initial motion to reopen did not provide new facts to establish that we erred in 
dismissing the appeal, nor did it establish that our dismissal of the appeal was an incorrect application 
of law or policy at the time it was issued. Accordingly, the motion to reopen was dismissed. 
The Petitioner's present combined motion to reopen and reconsider likewise does not provide new 
facts or evidence, nor does it identify any deficiency concerning the dismissal of her motion to reopen. 
The Petitioner reasserts her eligibility by citing previous arguments and resubmitting evidence 
previously included in the record. 3 The Petitioner also submits a paper that she co-authored which 
was published several months after the denial of her petition, which we cannot consider because 
evidence submitted on motion to reopen must establish eligibility at the time a petition is filed. See 
8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l); see also Matter o_fKatigbak, 14 I&N Dec. 45, 49 (Reg'l Comm'r 1971). 
The Petitioner has not submitted new facts that that would warrant reopening of the proceedings and, 
therefore, has not met the requirements for a motion to reopen. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2). The Petitioner 
has not established that our previous decision was based on an incorrect application of law or policy 
at the time we issued our decision. Therefore, she has not met the requirements for a motion to 
reconsider. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). The combined motion will be dismissed. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
ORDER: The motion to reopen is dismissed. 
FURTHER ORDER: The motion to reconsider is dismissed.
