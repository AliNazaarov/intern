Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a physics research professor, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary 
ability. See section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act), 8 U.S.C. 
§ 1 l 53(b)(1 )(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens 
who can demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim 
and whose achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
SCOPS denied the Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (petition), concluding the 
Petitioner had not established she had a major, internationally recognized award, or that she met at 
least three of the ten regulatory criteria. The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F .R. 
§ 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproofto demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christa's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act makes immigrant visas available to individuals with extraordinary 
ability if: they have extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which 
has been demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim; they seek to enter the United 
States to continue work in the area of extraordinary ability; and their entry will substantially benefit 
the United States prospectively. The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in 
"that small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
The implementing regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis of the evidence 
to determine whether a petitioner has sustained national or international acclaim and whether their 
achievements have been recognized in the field. First, a petitioner can provide evidence of either "a 
major, international recognized award," or satisfaction of at least three of ten evidentiary criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3).1 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration 
Services (USCIS) then considers the totality of the material provided in a final merits determination 
and assesses whether the record shows sustained national or international acclaim and demonstrates 
that the individual is among the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. 
See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1121 (9th Cir. 2010) (discussing a two-step review where the 
documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the required number of criteria, considered in the 
context of a final merits determination); see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 
(D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011); see generally 6 USCIS 
Policy Manual F.2(8), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual (providing, as guidance, USCIS' two-step 
extraordinary ability analysis). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner has not indicated or established that she has received a major, internationally recognized 
award under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) and must therefore satisfy at least three of the alternate regulatory 
criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). In support of the appeal, the Petitioner submits a brief. 
A. Evidentiary Criteria 
SCOPS determined the Petitioner established the regulatory criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi) by 
submitting evidence of authoring scholarly articles in the field in professional or major trade 
publications or other major media. We agree. 
SCOPS, without providing any analysis, concluded that the Petitioner also satisfied 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv), which requires evidence of the Petitioner's participation as a judge of the 
work of others in the same or an allied field of specification for which classification is sought. We 
disagree. Types of judging that may qualify include serving as a member of a Ph.D. dissertation 
committee that makes the final judgment as to whether a candidate' s body of work satisfies the 
requirements for a doctoral degree, or a peer reviewer for a scholarly journal or government research 
funding programs. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, supra, at F.2(8)(1). In support of this 
criterion, the Petitioner provided letters evidencing her role as a thesis jury member, where she 
reviewed the research work submitted by students working to attain their bachelor's degree. Her role 
ensured students fulfilled all academic and research requirements set by the institution for degree 
attainment. She also submitted letters evidencing her work as a tutor where she guided students' work. 
However, she has not sufficiently established that she judged the work of "others in the same or allied 
field" for which classification is sought, as required under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv), rather than 
candidates for entry into the field. Without more, we cannot conclude that she meets this criterion and 
we withdraw SCOPS determination to the contrary.

2. Remaining Evidentiary Criteria not Met 
a. Membership in Associations 
In order to satisfy the plain language of 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii), petitioners must demonstrate 
(i) membership in associations in the field for which classification is sought; and (ii) the association 
requires outstanding achievements of their members as judged by recognized national or international 
experts in their disciplines or fields. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, supra, at F.2(B)(l). 
The Petitioner submitted letters indicating her membership in over twenty associations and committees 
at thd I In a recommendation letter dated February 2024, the author explained she 
was appointed by the faculty and members of the physics depaiiment of the University. SCOPS 
determined the Petitioner did not submit evidence that these memberships require outstanding 
achievements of its members, as judged by recognized national or international experts in their 
disciplines or fields. On appeal, the Petitioner's counsel argues the Petitioner's memberships are 
reserved exclusively for individuals of the University's academic staff, members are the most 
prestigious and well-known professors of Venezuela, and that the University is ranked among the top 
1,200-1,400 universities worldwide. The record does not include supporting documentation of the 
University's ranking or evidence of the criteria for membership in the associations, and that 
membership is judged by recognized national or international experts. Counsel's unsubstantiated 
assertions do not constitute evidence. See, e.g., Matter of S-M-, 22 I&N Dec. 49, 51 (BIA 1998) 
("statements in a brief, motion, or Notice of Appeal are not evidence and thus are not entitled to any 
evidentiary weight"). As a result, the Petitioner has not demonstrated she satisfies this criterion. 
b. Original Contributions of Major Significance 
In order to meet the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v), a petitioner must establish that she not only 
made original contributions but that they have been of major significance in the field. Relevant 
supporting evidence may include, published materials, testimonials, letters, and affidavits about the 
significance of the person's original work; documentation that the person's original work was cited at 
a level indicative ofmajor significance in the field; patents or licenses deriving from the person's work 
or evidence of commercial use of the person's work. See generally 6 USCJS Policy Manual, supra, at 
F.2(B)(l). 
The Petitioner submitted her published articles, along with a Google Scholar printout of the citation 
rates for her aiiicles, and letters of recommendation. SCOPS determined that the Petitioner had not 
submitted independent objective evidence in support ofthis criterion to demonstrate her contributions 
as original and of major significance to the field. On appeal, counsel asserts that Petitioner's 
publications detail, for example, groundbreaking research on the structural, thermal, and optical

B. Summary of Evidentiary Criteria 
The Petitioner has not established that she has received a major, internationally recognized award 
under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), or that she satisfies at least three of the alternate regulatory criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). As a result, we need not provide the type of final merits determination 
referenced in Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (stating 
that agencies are not required to make "purely advisory findings" on issues that are unnecessary to the 
ultimate decision); see also Matter ofL-A-C-, 26 I&N Dec. 516, 526 n.7 (BIA 2015) (declining to 
reach alternative issues on appeal where an applicant is otherwise ineligible). Nevertheless, we advise 
that we have reviewed the record in the aggregate, concluding that it does not support a conclusion 
that the Petitioner has established the acclaim and recognition required for the classification sought. 
The Petitioner seeks a highly restrictive visa classification, intended for individuals already at the top 
of their respective fields, rather than for individuals progressing toward the top. See 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(2) (explaining "extraordinary ability" refers only to those in "that small percentage who 
have risen to the very top ofthe field ofendeavor"). USCIS has long held that even athletes performing 
at the major league level do not automatically meet the "extraordinary ability" standard. Matter of 
Price, 20 I&N Dec. 953,954 (Assoc. Comm'r 1994). Here, the record as a whole does not demonstrate 
a level of recognition that indicates the required sustained national or international acclaim or 
demonstrates a "career of acclaimed work in the field" as contemplated by Congress. H.R. Rep. No. 
101-723, 59 (Sept. 19, 1990); see also section 203(b )(1 )(A) of the Act. Moreover, the record does not 
otherwise demonstrate that the Petitioner is one of the small percentage who has risen to the very top 
of the field of endeavor.
