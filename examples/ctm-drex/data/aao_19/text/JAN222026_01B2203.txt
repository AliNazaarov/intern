Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an athletic trainer, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability in the 
sciences. See Immigration and Nationality Act section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). This 
first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens who can demonstrate 
their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements 
have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
SCOPS denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not establish either that he received a 
major, internationally recognized award, or that he satisfied at least three of the 10 initial evidentiary 
criteria. The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will withdraw the decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with the 
following analysis. 
The record establishes that SCOPS sent a request for evidence (RFE) to the Petitioner in March 2025 . 
The record also establishes that the Petitioner timely responded to the RFE in June 2025. However, 
SCOPS' decision, dated July 18, 2025, claims, "As of this date, the [P]etitioner has not responded [to 
the RFE]." SCOPS' decision addresses evidence the Petitioner submitted at the time he filed the Form 
1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers, without addressing the evidence he submitted in 
response to SCOPS' RFE. SCOPS' decision concludes that the Petitioner did not establish eligibility. 
On appeal, the Petitioner asserts, among other things, that SCOPS "ignored a timely-filed RFE 
response.... Under 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(8)(iv), USCIS needs to consider timely responses. By failing 
to do so, USCIS committed a procedural error." 
The Petitioner incorrectly asserts that 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(8)(iv) requires SCOPS to consider RFE 
responses. Rather, 8 C.F.R. § 103 .2(b)(8)(iv) describes when, and how, SCOPS may issue an RFE, 
and it provides the maximum response time for RFEs. Instead, 8 C.F.R. § 103 .2(b)(l 1) provides that 
even partial responses to RFEs "will be considered a request for a decision on the record," which 
includes evidence submitted in response to the RFE in question, provided such evidence may establish 
eligibility. See, e.g., 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l) (requiring petitioners to establish eligibility at the time of 
filing a benefit request, not based on a new set of facts that did not exist at the time of filing). 
SCOPS' statement that the Petitioner did not respond to the RFE is erroneous. SCOPS' decision, 
disregarding the Petitioner's RFE response, does not indicate that SCOPS adequately reviewed the 
totality of evidence in the record at the time of the decision. See generally 8 C.F.R. § 103 .2(b )(11 ). 
Based on the foregoing, we will remand this matter for SCOPS to review the record in its entirety for 
the entry of a new decision. SCOPS may request any additional evidence considered pertinent to the 
new determination. As such, we express no opinion regarding the ultimate resolution of this case on 
remand. 
ORDER: The decision is withdrawn. The matter is remanded for the entry of a new decision 
consistent with the foregoing analysis.
