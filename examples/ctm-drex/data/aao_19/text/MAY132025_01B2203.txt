Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a quantum chemist, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S .C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference (EB-1) classification makes immigrant visas available to aliens who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their fields through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the record did not establish 
that the Petitioner had satisfied at least three often initial evidentiary criteria. The matter is now before 
us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. On appeal, the Petitioner contends that SCOPS did not 
give due consideration to the totality of the relevant evidence and this error constitutes an abuse of 
discretion. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christa 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will withdraw the decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with the 
following analysis. 
I. LAW 
Section 
203(b)(l) of the Act makes visas available to aliens with extraordinary ability if: 
(i) the alien has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or 
athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained national or international 
acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, 
(ii) the alien seeks to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability, and 
(iii) the alien's entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively 
the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor. 8 C.F .R. § 204.5(h)(2). See also Onaghise v. Dep 't of 
Homeland Sec., No. 3:20-CV-03033-X, 2022 WL 4073344, at *2 (N.D. Tex. Sept. 2, 2022) ("EB-1 
petitions, nicknamed Einstein visas, are coveted visas reserved for only the most talented noncitizens 
with extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics"). The implementing 
regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate 
sustained acclaim and the recognition of the beneficiary's achievements in the field through a one­
time achievement that is a major, internationally recognized award. If that petitioner does not submit 
this evidence, then they must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of 
the ten categories listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) (including items, such as awards, published 
material in certain media, and scholarly articles). Additionally, ifthe criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) 
do not readily apply to the beneficiary's occupation, the petitioner may submit comparable evidence 
to establish the beneficiary's eligibility. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the beneficiary is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) 
( discussing a two-part review where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the 
required number of criteria, considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also 
Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 
(W.D. Wash. 2011). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner received a bachelor of science de ree from I IUniversity in China in 2011 and a 
doctor ofphilosophy degree from The University in 2016. The Petitioner asserts that he was 
a postdoctoral research fellow at.____..... University from 2016 to 2019. He has been a theoretical and 
computational scientist, a research position, in thel II lsince 2019. 
Because the Petitioner has not indicated or established that he received a major, internationally 
recognized award, he must satisfy at least three of the alternate regulatory criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). The Petitioner claims to have satisfied three of these criteria relating to 
judging, original contributions, and scholarly articles at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv),(v), and (vi), 
respectively. SCOPS decided that the Petitioner had satisfied only two criteria relating to judging and 
scholarly articles. On appeal, the Petitioner maintains that he meets all three claimed criteria and that 
he has sustained national or international acclaim and is one of the small percentage who has risen to 
the very top of his field of endeavor. 
Upon review of the record, we agree with the Petitioner that he has satisfied the criterion at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(v) relating to original scientific contributions of major significance in the field. A review 
of the record of proceedings reflects that the Petitioner submitted sufficient documentary evidence 
establishing that he minimally meets the plain language of the regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v) 
based on his publication record, citation statistics, and support letters from experts in the field. In 
denying the petition, SCOPS acknowledged the citation of the Petitioner's research work but concluded 
2 
that the record did not show that the impact ofhis work on the overall field oftheoretical chemistry rises 
to the level of original contribution of major significance. SCOPS also acknowledged the Petitioner's 
submission of support letters from experts in the field but concluded that these writers did not provide 
any information or objective documentary evidence to show that the Petitioner's research work has been 
of major significance in the field. 
At the time the Petitioner filed his petition in 2023, his Google Scholar profile showed that he co-authored 
17 scholarly articles in his field, which were published in professional publications. The Google Scholar 
database showed over 3,000 citations ofhis article published in 2015, over 900 citations ofhis two articles 
published in 2020 and 2021, and over 100 citations of his three articles published in 2014, 2015, and 
2016. The Petitioner's I Ipercentile ranking indicates that he is one of the top 1 % of the most 
highly cited authors publishing on the topic of chemistry from 2014 to 2023. Documentation that 
published research has been highly cited relative to others' work in that field may be probative of the 
significance ofthe person's contributions to the field ofendeavor. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual 
F.2(B)(l), https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. These citations do speak to the field's reliance on the 
cited works. 
In addition, the support letters from experts in the field establish by a preponderance of the evidence the 
Petitioner's original scientific contributions of major significance in the field. The Petitioner submitted 
five support letters from experts in the field, and four of these letters were from independent researchers 
who do not know the Petitioner personally but have relied on his work to further their own studies. These 
writers provide detailed descriptions of some of the Petitioner's research projects, explain how his work 
has influenced subsequent studies in quantum chemistry and the development of analogous theoretical 
methodologies, and emphasize the significance of the Petitioner's work and his contributions to the field 
ofquantum chemistry. Detailed letters from experts in the field explaining the nature and significance of 
the person's contribution may provide valuable context for evaluating the claimed original contributions 
of major significance, particularly when the record includes documentation corroborating the claimed 
significance. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, supra, at F.2(B)(l). Submitted letters should 
specifically describe the person's contributions and their significance to the field and should also set forth 
the basis of the writers' knowledge and expertise. Id. 
The Petitioner has, therefore, overcome the only stated ground for denial of the petition. Nevertheless, 
the record does not support approval of the petition. Granting the third initial criterion does not suffice 
to establish eligibility for the classification the Petitioner seeks. SCOPS must undertake a final merits 
determination to analyze the Petitioner's accomplishments and weigh the totality of the evidence to 
determine if he establishes extraordinary ability in his field of endeavor. See section 203(b )(1 )(A)(i) 
of the Act; 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2), (3); see also Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20. 
When considering the evidence submitted to show the Petitioner's acclaim and recognition in the field, 
SCOPS must consider whether or not the record corroborates the Petitioner's assertions. For example, 
participation in the peer review process does not automatically demonstrate that an individual has 
sustained national or international acclaim at the very top oftheir field. The record includes thank you 
emails from publishers for the Petitioner's reviews of various manuscripts from 2018 to 2023. 
Although the record shows that the Petitioner reviewed numerous manuscripts, it does not demonstrate 
how his reviews differentiate him from the field, such as evidence showing how his work compares in 
quantity and quality to that of his peers. Without evidence that sets him apart from others in his field, 
3 
such as evidence that he has a consistent history ofreviewing or judging recognized, acclaimed experts 
in his field, evidence that he has served in editorial positions for distinguished journals or publications, 
or other sufficient evidence, the Petitioner has not established that his peer review experience places 
him among that small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
SCOPS must keep such factors in mind when weighing the record for the final merits determination. 
III. CONCLUSION 
Because the Petitioner has overcome the only stated 
ground for denial, we remand this proceeding so 
that SCOPS can render a final merits determination in keeping with the Kazarian framework. 
ORDER: The decision is withdrawn. The matter is remanded for the entry of a new decision 
consistent with the foregoing analysis.
