Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a journalist, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See section 
203(b)(l)(A) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding the Petitioner was not the 
recipient of a major, internationally recognized award and did not satisfy at least three of the ten 
evidentiary criteria to meet the initial evidentiary requirements of the classification. The matter is now 
before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, In c., 26 l&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act makes immigrant visas available to aliens with extraordinary ability if: 
they have extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been 
demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim; they seek to enter the United States to 
continue work in the area of extraordinary ability; and their entry will substantially benefit the United 
States prospectively. The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small 
percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204. 5(h)(2). 
The implementing regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204. 5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis of the evidence 
to determine whether a petitioner has sustained national or international acclaim and whether their 
achievements have been recognized in the field. First, a petitioner can provide evidence of either "a 
major, international recognized award," or satisfaction of at least three of ten evidentiary criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration 
Services (USCIS) then considers the totality of the material provided in a final merits determination 
and assesses whether the record shows sustained national or international acclaim and demonstrates 
that the individual is among the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian 
v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1121 (9th Cir.2010) ( discussing a two-step review where the documentation 
is frrst counted and then, if fulfilling the required number of criteria, considered in the context of a 
final merits determination); see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); 
Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011); see generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual 
F.2(B), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual (providing, as guidance, USCIS' two-step extraordinary ability 
analysis). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner is a national ofKazakhstan. According to her Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien 
Workers, her job title is a journalist. She submitted two undated personal statements describing herself 
as a poet, writer, member of the performing arts, model, and actor. She stated she has a bachelor's 
degree in law and is a professional coach. She also said she aspires to establish a center for 
psychological assistance to women and children affected by domestic violence and a talent 
developmental center for children. She intends to publish books and poems and to be a print journalist, 
writing for newspapers, magazines, and other publications. She also included employment letters 
indicating she has been working for a home care agency since July 2024, but the letters do not explain 
her job title or duties. 
A. Issues Waived 
SCOPS determined the Petitioner had not established that she has received a major, internationally 
recognized award under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). On appeal, the Petitioner does not contest this finding. 
We therefore deem this issue waived. See Matter ofO-R-E-, 28 I&N Dec. 330, 336 n.5 (BIA 2021) 
(citing Matter ofR-A-M-, 25 I&N Dec. 657,658 n.2 (BIA 2012)) (waiving an issue not fully developed 
on appeal). 
She must therefore satisfy at least three of the regulatory criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). 
Based on our de novo review of the record, the Petitioner will not meet this showing. The Petitioner 
did not assert in the record below or on appeal that she meets the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii), 
(ix), or (x), regarding her performance in a leading or critical role for organizations or establishments, 
commanding a high salary or significant remuneration for services, and commercial success in the 
performing arts. We therefore deem the Petitioner's eligibility under these three criteria also waived.

The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4) allows a petitioner to submit "comparable evidence to 
establish the beneficiary's eligibility" where the standards at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) "do not 
readily apply to the beneficiary's occupation." To the extent she is requesting us to evaluate her 
evidence as comparable, she must first demonstrate that this criterion is not applicable to journalists. 
The fact that the Petitioner has not authored scholarly articles in the field, in professional or major 
trade publications or other major media, does not establish that this criterion does not readily apply to 
journalists. As a result, the Petitioner has not met this criterion and has not established that we should 
consider her comparable evidence as meeting this criterion.

3. Issues Reserved 
SCOPS determined the Petitioner did not establish she meets the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii), 
requiring evidence of published material about the Petitioner in professional or major trade 
publications or other major media, relating to the Petitioner's work in the field for which classification 
is sought. As the Petitioner would be unable to establish three of the ten threshold "initial evidence" 
requirements at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), we cannot conclude she is an alien of extraordinary ability and 
reserve our analysis of the remaining criterion as well as a final merits determination in this matter. 
See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (per curiam) (holding that agencies are not required 
to make "purely advisory findings" on issues that are unnecessary to the ultimate decision); see also 
Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20 (discussing a two-step review, where if the required number of criteria 
are met by the documentation, then it is considered in the context of a final merits determination). 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner has not demonstrated eligibility as an individual of extraordinary ability.
