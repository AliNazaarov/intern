## I. Employment in a Critical or Essential Capacity for an Organization with a Distinguished Reputation
### (8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(B)(7))

The Beneficiary, Ms. Mira Okafor-Lund, is employed by the Petitioner, Kestrelbay Robotics, Inc. ("Kestrelbay" or the "Company"), in a critical and essential capacity (E1-offer-letter.md). As set forth below, the Beneficiary holds the position of Founding Engineer, serves as the technical lead of the Company's Motion pod, and has been directly responsible for the development of the motion-planning technology that enables the Company's autonomous orchard-harvesting robots to perform their commercial work (E1-offer-letter.md; E3-org-chart.md; E6-field-report.md).

**A. The Beneficiary's Role and Responsibilities.** The Beneficiary has served as the Company's Founding Engineer since March 18, 2024, and as the designated technical lead of the Motion pod (E1-offer-letter.md; E3-org-chart.md).

By offer letter dated March 4, 2024, the Company offered the Beneficiary the position of Founding Engineer, effective March 18, 2024, with the Beneficiary's acceptance on March 5, 2024 (E1-offer-letter.md). As Founding Engineer, the Beneficiary is responsible for the motion-planning software of the Company's autonomous orchard-harvesting robot and serves as technical lead of the Motion pod, reporting to Theo Vance, Chief Technology Officer (E1-offer-letter.md). The September 2025 organizational chart confirms that the Beneficiary is the designated technical lead of the Motion pod, a team of six engineers (E3-org-chart.md).

The Beneficiary is one of the Company's earliest hires (E2-press-article.md). Press coverage in the trade publication *Orchard & Machine Weekly* describes the Beneficiary as "one of the minds behind the robot's motion planning, which lets the arm reach around branches instead of through them" (E2-press-article.md).

**B. The Beneficiary's Essential Contribution to the Company's Products and Operations.** The Beneficiary's technical work has made an essential contribution to the Company's products and operations (E6-field-report.md; E5-email-comp-discussion.md).

The Company's 2025 Harvest Season Field Report, prepared by Abebe Mulugeta, Head of Field Operations, establishes that the Beneficiary's technical leadership is directly tied to the Company's operational success (E6-field-report.md). The report states that the branch-avoidance motion planner — developed by the Motion pod under the Beneficiary's technical leadership — reduced arm collisions with branches by 63% compared with the 2024 season, and that field technicians reported fewer manual resets as a result (E6-field-report.md). During the 2025 season, eight machines deployed across three partner farms in the Yakima Valley harvested approximately 1.9 million apples at a bruise rate of 2.4% (against a hand-picking benchmark of 3.1%) and an average of 410 picks per arm per hour (E6-field-report.md).

The Company's CEO, Ines Calloway, expressly affirmed the Beneficiary's essential role in writing: "You've been essential to getting the harvesters through this season" (E5-email-comp-discussion.md).

**C. The Distinguished Reputation of the Petitioner.** The Petitioner is a recognized and growing participant in its field, having been profiled in the trade press and having raised a $12 million Series A (E2-press-article.md).

In June 2025, the trade publication *Orchard & Machine Weekly* profiled the Company's harvesting technology and its deployment of eight machines on three farms in the Yakima Valley (E2-press-article.md). The same article reports that Kestrelbay raised a $12 million Series A in 2024 (E2-press-article.md). The press and financing achievements described above are offered as evidence of the Petitioner's distinguished reputation in autonomous agricultural robotics [client to provide evidence of the Petitioner's distinguished reputation]. The Beneficiary holds a leading engineering position within the Company, as Founding Engineer and designated technical lead of the Motion pod (E1-offer-letter.md; E3-org-chart.md).

## II. High Salary or Other Significantly High Remuneration
### (8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(B)(8))

The Beneficiary receives high remuneration for her services to the Petitioner, including an annual base salary of $182,000 and an equity option covering 0.8% of the Company's fully diluted shares (E1-offer-letter.md; E4-payroll-2025.md).

**A. Base Salary.** The Beneficiary's annual base salary is $182,000, paid semi-monthly (E1-offer-letter.md).

The Company's Employee Earnings Statement for the period January 1, 2025 through October 31, 2025, confirms that the annual base salary rate of $182,000 has remained unchanged since hire, and that the Beneficiary received in base salary alone $151,666.70 in the ten months of 2025 reported, for a gross pay YTD of $151,666.70 (E4-payroll-2025.md).

**B. Equity Compensation.** In addition to base salary, the Beneficiary was granted an option to purchase 0.8% of the Company's fully diluted shares, vesting over four years with a one-year cliff (E1-offer-letter.md). The earnings statement notes that equity awards are not included in the salary figures reported (E4-payroll-2025.md).

**C. The Company's Recognition of the Beneficiary's Compensation Value.** The Company has recognized the value of the Beneficiary's contributions in writing, including in its compensation discussions with the Beneficiary (E5-email-comp-discussion.md).

In an email of August 21, 2025, the CEO — in response to a compensation check-in — stated that the Company wants the Beneficiary's pay to "reflect" her essential role, and communicated the Company's goal of bringing the Beneficiary to a $250,000 package in 2026, a goal the CEO candidly described as contingent on closing the Series B and on Board approval of the 2026 compensation plan (E5-email-comp-discussion.md). This statement, from the Company's highest executive, evidences the Company's own valuation of the Beneficiary's contributions and the upward trajectory of her compensation (E5-email-comp-discussion.md).
