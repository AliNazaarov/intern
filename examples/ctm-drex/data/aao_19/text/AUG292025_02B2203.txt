Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a teacher, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See section 
203(b)(l)(A) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
SCOPS denied the Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (petition), concluding the 
Petitioner had not established she had a major, internationally recognized award, or that she met at 
least three of the ten regulatory criteria. The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F .R. 
§ 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproofto demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christa's, Inc. , 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act makes immigrant visas available to aliens with extraordinary ability if: 
they have extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been 
demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim; they seek to enter the United States to 
continue work in the area of extraordinary ability; and their entry will substantially benefit the United 
States prospectively. The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small 
percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
The implementing regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis of the evidence 
to determine whether petitioners have sustained national or international acclaim and whether their 
achievements have been recognized in the field. First, a petitioner can provide evidence of either "a 
major, international recognized award," or satisfaction of at least three of ten evidentiary criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3).1 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration 
Services (USCIS) then considers the totality of the material provided in a final merits determination 
and assesses whether the record shows sustained national or international acclaim and demonstrates 
that the individual is among the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. 
See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1121 (9th Cir. 2010) (discussing a two-step review where the 
documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the required number of criteria, considered in the 
context of a final merits determination); see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 
(D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011); see generally 6 USCIS 
Policy Manual F.2(8), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual (providing, as guidance, USCIS' two-step 
extraordinary ability analysis). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner has not indicated or established that she has received a major, internationally recognized 
award, she must therefore satisfy at least three of the alternate regulatory criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). The Petitioner claimed she has satisfied the regulatory criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii), (iv), (v), (vi), (viii), and (ix). SCOPS determined the Petitioner only 
established the regulatory criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii). On the Form I-290B, Notice ofAppeal 
or Motion, the Petitioner provides the basis of her appeal and claims she will file a brief. As of the 
date of this decision, we have not received a brief. For the reasons discussed below, we conclude the 
Petitioner has not established any of the claimed criteria. 
A. Evidentiary Criteria 
1. Membership in Associations 
In order to satisfy the plain language of 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii), petitioners must demonstrate 
(i) membership in associations in the field for which classification is sought; and (ii) the association 
requires outstanding achievements of their members as judged by recognized national or international 
experts in their disciplines or fields. In the record below, the Petitioner stated she is a member of the 
National Board for Professional Teaching Standards and identified the eligibility requirements as 
possessing a bachelor's degree from an accredited institution, holding a valid state teaching license or 
meeting state licensure requirements, and having completed three years of teaching. While the 
Petitioner has established that she is a member of an association for which classification is sought, she 
has not established that the _______________.....,...____requires outstanding 
achievements as judged by recognized national or international experts in their disciplines or fields for 
membership. As a result, we withdraw SCOPS' determination that the Petitioner has established her 
eligibility for this criterion.

5. Leading or Critical Role 
The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii) requires petitioners to first demonstrate that they have 
performed in a leading or critical role for an organization, establishment, or a division or department 
of an organization or establishment. A supporting role may be considered critical if the petitioner's 
performance in the role is (or was) important. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, supra, at 
F .2(8)( 1) (providing, as guidance, examples of leading or critical roles and considerations for 
evaluating the evidence). In the underlying record, the Petitioner claims her roles as summer school 
coordinator,! I and after-school program coordinator were critical as they 
significantly impacted student achievement and enhanced the school's educational offerings. She 
added that her role as a Microsoft ce1iified trainer and site coordinator was leading as it significantly 
contributed to her school's technological advancement and ability to provide uninterrupted education 
during the pandemic. In support of her statements, the Petitioner refers to support letters authored by 
colleagues. The Petitioner also claimed that she held leading and critical roles in the Philippines. 
SCOPS determined the Petitioner's evidence, including her support letters, did not contain detailed 
and probative information demonstrating she held leading or critical roles. 
The Petitioner contends that she has demonstrated her leadership in programs that improve graduation 
rates, test scores, and student retention. However, the Petitioner's statements are not supported in the 
record. None ofthe Petitioner's letters refer to the Petitioner's role as leading within any establishment 
or department thereof. See id. (providing, as guidance, for a leading role, the evidence should establish 
that the person is ( or was) a leader within the organization or establishment or a division or department 
thereof). The letters also do not demonstrate that her role was critical. To establish she played a 
4 
critical role, the Petitioner must establish that she contributed in a way that is of significant importance 
to the outcome of the organization or establishment's activities or those of a division or department of 
the organization or establishment. Id. While complementary about her skills as a teacher and as a 
trainer, the letters do not corroborate her statements about improving graduation rates, test scores, and 
student retention or demonstrate she contributed significantly to the outcome of the organizations she 
was affiliated with or any departments within them. Further, we note that while the Petitioner also 
claimed she held leading and critical roles in the Philippines, she does not provide supporting evidence 
that her roles were leading or critical, nor does she identify what establishment or organization she 
held leading or critical roles. 
The criterion then requires a petitioner to establish that the organization or establishment, or the 
department or division for which the person holds or held a leading or critical role, has a distinguished 
reputation. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii). Distinguished is defined as "marked by eminence, 
distinction, or excellence" or "befitting an eminent person." See Distinguished, 
Merriam-Webster. corn, www .rnerriarn-we bster. corn. The underlying record contained printouts about 
the ranking of the school the Petitioner was employed with at the time of filing. According to the data 
provided, her school ranked 13,242 out of 17,655, with national mathematics proficiency at 2%. The 
Petitioner's supporting documents do not demonstrate the school or any of the establishments, or any 
department or division therein to which she claims to have had a leading or critical role, has a 
distinguished reputation. As a result, the Petitioner has not met this criterion. 
6. High Salary and/or Remuneration 
In order to meet 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix), petlt10ners must demonstrate that their salary or 
remuneration is high relative to others working in the field. The Petitioner submitted pay records, 
employment contracts, tax forms, and salary data for teachers. SCOPS determined her evidence did 
not demonstrate she satisfied this criterion and on the Form I-290B, the Petitioner asserts her salary is 
ten times higher than the average salary for teachers in the Philippines and significantly above New 
Mexico's median salary. The difference in pay between a teacher in the Philippines and that in the 
United States does not support the Petitioner's high remuneration. Rather, we look at salary data 
relevant to the applicable work location. For example, the Petitioner submitted a certificate of 
employment for a teacher II position in the Philippines for 2019 evidencing her basic salary was 22,938 
pesos. She also included salary data from the Philippines Department of Education, indicating that 
the basic monthly salary for a teacher II position in 2019 was the exact amount she was paid. Her 
evidence does not support that she commanded a higher salary than other teachers while employed in 
the Philippines. The Petitioner also provided salary information for her work as a teacher in the United 
States and argued below that her total compensation package is much higher than the average salary 
of teachers. Here, the Petitioner's 2023 employment contract lists her pay as $57,237. According to 
the submitted data from salary.corn for teachers in New Mexico, the median salary in 2023 was 
$52,794, the range of pay was $44,084 to $64,391, and teachers at the 90th percentile earned about 
$75,000. Based on the data provided by the Petitioner, she has not established that her salary is high 
relative to others working in the field. For these reasons, the Petitioner has not shown that she meets 
this criterion.

B. Summary of Evidentiary Criteria 
The Petitioner has not established that she has received a major, internationally recognized award 
under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), or that she satisfies at least three of the alternate regulatory criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). As a result, we need not provide the type of final merits determination 
referenced in Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (stating 
that agencies are not required to make "purely advisory findings" on issues that are unnecessary to the 
ultimate decision); see also Matter ofL-A-C-, 26 I&N Dec. 516, 526 n.7 (BIA 2015) (declining to 
reach alternative issues on appeal where an applicant is otherwise ineligible). Nevertheless, we advise 
that we have reviewed the record in the aggregate, concluding that it does not support a conclusion 
that the Petitioner has established the acclaim and recognition required for the classification sought. 
The Petitioner seeks a highly restrictive visa classification, intended for individuals already at the top 
of their respective fields. See 8 C.F .R. § 204.5 (h)(2) ( explaining "extraordinary ab ility" refers only to 
those in "that small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor"). USCIS has 
long held that even athletes performing at the major league level do not automatically meet the 
"extraordinary ability" standard. Matter of Price, 20 I&N Dec. 953 , 954 (Assoc. Comm'r 1994). 
Here, the record as a whole does not demonstrate a level of recognition that indicates the required 
sustained national or international acclaim or demonstrates a "career of acclaimed work in the field" 
as contemplated by Congress. H.R. Rep. No. 101-723, 59 (Sept. 19, 1990); see also section 
203(b)(l)(A) of the Act. Moreover, the record does not otherwise demonstrate that the Petitioner is 
one of the small percentage who has risen to the very top of the field of endeavor. 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner has not demonstrated eligibility as an individual of extraordinary ability.
