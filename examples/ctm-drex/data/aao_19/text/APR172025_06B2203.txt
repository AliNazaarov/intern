Appeal of Texas Service Center Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an information technology project manager, seeks classification as an individual of 
extraordinary ability in business. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b )(1 )(A), 
8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to 
aliens who can demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international 
acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Id. 
The Director of the Texas Service Center denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not 
establish either that he received a major, internationally recognized award, or that he satisfied at least 
three of the 10 initial evidentiary criteria. The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F .R. 
§ 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 
203(b)(l) of the Act makes visas available to immigrants with extraordinary ability if: 
(i) the alien has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or 
athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained national or international 
acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, 
(ii) the alien seeks to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability, and 
(iii) the alien's entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively 
the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate sustained 
acclaim and the recognition of their achievements in the field through a one-time achievement (that 
is, a major, internationally recognized award). If that petitioner does not submit this evidence, then 
they must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the 10 categories 
listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) (including items such as awards, published material in certain 
media, and scholarly articles). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010). 
II. ANALYSIS 
At the time the Petitioner filed the Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers, he described 
his endeavor as a plan "to continue my career as a [sic] Information Technology Project Manager in 
the United States." 
As noted above, the Director concluded the record does not establish the Petitioner received a one­
time achievement of a major, internationally recognized award. The Director further determined that 
the record does not satisfy, in the alternative, at least three of the 10 criteria listed at 8 C.F.R. 
§§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). Specifically, the Director determined that the record satisfies the criterion at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv). However, the Director also concluded that the record does not satisfy the 
criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(ii), or (v)-(ix), noting that the Petitioner did not assert eligibility 
under the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i), (iii), or (x). On appeal, the Petitioner reasserts that, in 
addition to the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv), the record satisfies the criteria at 8 C.F.R. 
§§ 204.5(h)(3)(ii), (v)-(vi), and (viii)-(ix). The Petitioner does not assert on appeal-and the record 
does not support the conclusion-that the record satisfies the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i), 
(iii), (vii), or (x). The record does not satisfy at least three of the 10 criteria listed at 8 C.F.R. 
§§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) for the reasons discussed below.

B. Contributions of Major Significance in the Field 
The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v) requires "[e]vidence of the alien's original scientific, 
scholarly, artistic, athletic, or business-related contributions of major significance in the field." 
The Director acknowledged that the Petitioner authored a book regarding project management. 
However, the Director observed that the record does not contain "evidence that people throughout the 
field currently consider his work important or that his work has provoked widespread public 
commentary in the field or has been widely cited." Therefore, the Director concluded the record does 
not satisfy the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v). 
On appeal, the Petitioner asserts that the record satisfies the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v) 
because his book "has introduced innovative frameworks combining project management principles 
with philosophical insights, which have been adopted by various organizations." The Petitioner also 
states that "letters of support from industry experts, such as I Iunderscore the 
widespread impact of [his] work." 
3 
We first note that the Petitioner filed the Form I-140 on July 27, 2023. However, the Petitioner stated 
several times throughout the record that his book "is scheduled to be lauched [sic] and published on 
7 /28/2023," after the petition filing date. 
A petitioner must establish eligibility for a requested benefit at the time the petition is filed. See 
8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l). A visa petition may not be approved based on speculation offoture eligibility 
or after a petitioner becomes eligible under a new set of facts. See Matter of Katigbak, 14 I&N 
Dec. 45, 49 (Reg'l Comm'r 1971). A petitioner may not make material changes to a petition in an 
effort to make a deficient petition conform to USCIS requirements. See Matter ofIzummi, 22 I&N 
Dec.169, 176(Assoc.Comm'r 1998). 
Because the record does not establish that the book in question was published as of the petition filing 
date, it cannot establish eligibility. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l); Matter ofKatigbak, 14 I&N Dec. at 
49; Matter of Izummi, 22 I&N Dec. at 176. Relatedly, because the Petitioner filed the Form I-140 
before he published the book in question, any subsequent commentary on its importance from others 
working in the field, or citations to it, also cannot establish eligibility because such commentary or 
citations would present a new set of material facts that did not exist at the time the Petitioner filed the 
Form I-140. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l); Matter ofKatigbak, 14 I&N Dec. at 49; Matter ofIzummi, 
22 I&N Dec. at 176. 
Even if the Petitioner's book could establish eligibility, which it cannot, contrary to the Petitioner's 
assertions, the record does not contain letters of support that demonstrate his book constitutes an 
original contribution ofmajor significance to the field ofinformation technolojy project management, 
business administration, or any other field. The letter froml.._________, o which the Petitioner 
refers appears to be his five-sentence statement used as one of the book's forewords.I I

written and published in the initial printing of the book, I ppinion does not provide an 
informed opinion on how the book has, in fact, contributed to the field of information technology

forl lthat expresses "confidence that [the Petitioner's] book will become a valuable guide

frameworks combining project management principles with philosophical insights" in a way that 
would demonstrate the book is an original work of major significance in the field of information 
technology project management, business administration, or any other field. 
In summation, the record does not establish that, at the time the Petitioner filed the Form I-140, he had 
made original scientific, scholarly, artistic, athletic, or business-related contributions of major 
significance in the field. Therefore, it does not satisfy the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v).

C. Leading or Critical Role 
The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii) requires "[e]vidence that the alien has performed in a 
leading or critical role for organizations or establishments that have a distinguished reputation." 
The Director acknowledged that the record contains information regarding the Petitioner's work for 
various employers. However, the Director observed that the 
record "does not indicate that the role the 
[P]etitioner has performed ... was leading or critical." The Director further determined that the record 
does not establish that his former employers "have a distinguished reputation." More specifically, the 
Director observed that the record does not "demonstrate the organizations have a distinguished 
reputation because the evidence did not document the organizations' or establishments' eminence, 
distinction, or excellence and documentary evidence was not submitted to substantiate the claims." 
Therefore, the Director concluded the record does not satisfy the criterion at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(viii). 
On appeal, the Petitioner reasserts that the record satisfies the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii) 
because "letters from senior management" attest he "has played a leading or critical role in several 
high-profile organizations, includin~ Isuch as "instrumental involvement 
in implementing complex IT solutions that significantly advanced corporate objectives." The 
Petitioner also generally states that he "has served in a critical role for organizations with a 
distinguished reputation," without clarifying what information in the record illustrates any particular 
organization's distinguished reputation. 
The Petitioner submitted a copy of his resume, which indicates he worked for I I as a 
"technical support analyst" from June 2002 to December 2004, and as a "customer service 
representative" from December 2004 to August 2008. In tum, the resume indicates that the Petitioner 
worked for Softplan as a "Project Manager III" from June 2015 to November 2017. The record 
contains a letter from the "team leader for the customer service team" who "collaborat[ ed] with" the 
Petitioner for "over two years" of the latter's six-year employment a~ IThe record also 
contains a letter from an international project manager at Softplan who asserts he "worked together" 
with the Petitioner in 2016 and 2017, when the petitioner worked as a "senior IT project manager" 
while the letter author "served as a project management coordinator ... overseeing a team of 30 
employees." The letters summarize the Petitioner's duties during that period and projects he 
completed. 
We acknowledge that the letters establish that the Petitioner contributed in a way that is of significant 
importance to the outcome of a division or department of the respective organizations or 
establishments and, thus, he has performed in critical roles. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual 
F.2(8)(1 ), https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. However, the record does not establish that his 
employers qualify as organizations or establishments that have a distinguished reputation. Merriam­
Webster's online dictionary defines "distinguished" as "marked by eminence, distinction, or 
excellence" or "befitting an eminent person." Distinguished, Merriam-Webster.com, www.merriam­
webster.com. As the Director observed, the record does not contain documentary evidence to 
substantiate the Petitioner's general statements regarding his former employers' notoriety in order to 
determine whether their notoriety can be described as marked by eminence, distinction, or excellence, 
or befitting an eminent person.

In summation, the record does not establish the Petitioner has performed in a leading or critical role 
for organizations or establishments that have a distinguished reputation. Therefore, it does not satisfy 
the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii). 
D. High Salary or Other Significantly High Remuneration 
The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix) requires "[e]vidence that the alien has commanded a high 
salary or other significantly high remuneration for services, in relation to others in the field." 
The Director acknowledged that the record contains IRS Forms W-2 for the Petitioner from multiple 
employers dated 2022, and salary information for project managers in Florida in 2023 from 
Indeed.com and Glassdoor. However, the Director noted that, because "wages fluctuate annually and 
data from different years is not comparable," the salary information does not compare the Petitioner's 
2022 salary or other remuneration to others in the field for that year. Therefore, the Director concluded 
the record does not establish whether the Petitioner's salary or other remuneration was high in relation 
to others in the field and, thus, does not satisfy the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix). 
On appeal, the Petitioner reasserts that the record satisfies the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix) 
because, regardless of "the timing of salary data," the 2022 Forms W-2 and salary information 
establish his salary or other remuneration for services is "significantly higher than the average salary 
for professionals in similar roles." 
The Petitioner submitted information regarding his income earned in 2022, for employers with 
addresses in Georgia and New York. The Petitioner's resume indicates that, from June 2022 to 
September 2023, he performed "remote work" for the employer located in New York and that, from 
December 2021 through March 2023 he also performed "remote work" for the employer located in 
Georgia. In contrast, the Petitioner submitted a salary estimate for a "project manager" in Florida in 
2023 from Indeed.com and Glassdoor. 
The record does not clarify whether the Petitioner's income for remote work performed for employers 
located in New York and Georgia is based on wage levels in those locations or in the location where 
the Petitioner actually performed the remote work. Therefore, beyond the issue of comparing the 
average salary from one year to salaries earned in a different year addressed by the Director, the record 
does not establish how many different locations' average salaries ought to be compared, and which 
locations' average salaries ought to be compared. 
We further note that the information provided in the Indeed.com and Glassdoor documents is overly 
broad. Both sources report estimates for the entire state of Florida, although salaries may vary within 
various metropolitan areas. Moreover, they appear to reflect salaries for project managers working in 
many disparate industries, including: "Agriculture; Arts, Entertainment & Recreation; Pharmaceutical 
& Biotechnology; Management & Consulting; Construction, Repair & Maintenance Services; 
Personal Consumer Services; Education; Financial Services; Government & Public Administration; 
Healthcare; Information Technology; Insurance; Manufacturing; Media & Communication; Nonprofit 
& NGO; Energy, Mining & Utilities; Real Estate; Restaurants & Food Service," and others, rather 
than reflecting only salaries of information technology project managers. Therefore, the information 
6 
provided in the Indeed.corn and Glassdoor documents does not provide estimates for similarly situated 
individuals in the same field as the Petitioner. 
Because the record does not clarify these deficiencies, it does not establish what similarly situated 
information technology project managers' salary or other remuneration was in 2022, precluding a 
comparison of such information to the evidence in the record of the Petitioner's salary or other 
remuneration. 
In summation, the record does not establish the Petitioner has commanded a high salary or other 
significantly high remuneration for services, in relation to others in the field. Therefore, the record 
does not satisfy the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix). 
Because the record does not satisfy the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(ii), (v), and (viii)-(ix), and 
because the Petitioner does not assert the record satisfies the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i), (iii), 
( vii), or ( x ), the record would not satisfy at least three of the 10 initial evidentiary criteria, even if it 
satisfied the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi). Accordingly, we reserve our opinion regarding 
whether the record satisfies the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi). See INS v. Bagamasbad, 
429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (stating that agencies are not required to make "purely advisory findings" on 
issues that are unnecessary to the ultimate decision); see also Matter ofL-A-C-, 26 I&N Dec. 516,526 
n. 7 (BIA 2015) ( declining to reach alternative issues on appeal where an applicant is otherwise 
ineligible). 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner has not established he received a one-time achievement or, in the alternative, provided 
sufficient evidence that meets at least three of the 10 criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). As a 
result, we need not provide the type of final merits determination referenced in Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 
1119-20. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. at 25; see also Matter of L-A-C-, 26 I&N Dec. at 526 
n.7. Nevertheless, we have reviewed the record in the aggregate, concluding that it does not support 
a conclusion that the Petitioner has established the acclaim and recognition required for the 
classification sought. 
The Petitioner has not shown that the significance of his work is indicative of the required sustained 
national or international acclaim or that it is consistent with a "career of acclaimed work in the field" 
as contemplated by Congress. H.R. Rep. No. 101-723, 59 (Sept. 19, 1990); see also section 
203(b)(1 )(A) of the Act. Moreover, the record does not otherwise demonstrate that the Petitioner has 
garnered national or international acclaim in the field, and that he is one of the small percentage who 
has risen to the very top ofthe field ofendeavor. See section 203(b )(l)(A) ofthe Act; see also 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(2). 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
