Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. See Immigration and 
Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference 
classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens who can demonstrate their extraordinary 
ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been 
recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) denied the petition and dismissed subsequent 
motions. We dismissed the Petitioner's appeal and two subsequent combined motions to reopen and 
reconsider. The matter is now before us on a third combined motion to reopen and reconsider. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the 
motion. 
A motion to reopen must state new facts and be supported by documentary evidence. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.5(a)(2). Because the scope of a motion is limited to the prior decision, we will only review the 
latest decision in these proceedings. 8 C.F.R. § 103 .5(a)(l)(i), (ii). We may grant motions that satisfy 
these requirements and demonstrate eligibility for the requested benefit. Accordingly, the issue before 
us is whether the Petitioner's new facts and supporting evidence warrant reopening our April 29, 2026, 
decision dismissing her second combined motion. See Matter ofCoelho, 20 I&N Dec. 464, 473 (BIA 
1992) (requiring that new evidence have the potential to change the outcome). 
In our prior decision, we concluded that the evidence submitted with the Petitioner's motion did not 
establish new facts warranting reopening. We explained that the materials either concerned evidence 
and circumstances already considered in the underlying proceeding or reflected later professional 
activity that did not establish her eligibility at the relevant time. 
On the present motion, the Petitioner again argues that her evidence should be treated as new because 
it includes additional documentation of professional activity, recognition, client engagements, 
publications, remuneration, judging, and other accomplishments. She also maintains that these 
materials demonstrate continued acclaim and further support the evidentiary criteria previously 
claimed. The newly submitted materials, however, do not establish new facts that overcome the 
grounds identified in our prior decision. Much of the evidence was previously submitted in prior 
filings or reflects the continuation of the same type of professional activity that was already 
documented and considered in the underlying proceedings. The Petitioner also outlined 
documentation that was previously submitted to reiterate she meets the criteria of original 
contributions, published material, leading or critical roles, high remuneration, and judging activities. 
Repeating an argument already considered does not identify a new fact showing that our prior motion 
decision should be reopened. 
Moreover, evidence of professional accomplishments that occurred after the petition's filing does not 
establish that the Petitioner was eligible when she filed it. A petitioner must establish eligibility at the 
time of filing and may not qualify through later events. A petitioner must establish eligibility for the 
benefit it is seeking at the time the petition is filed. See 8 C.F.R. § 103 .2(b)(1 ). A visa petition may 
not be approved based on speculation of future eligibility or after a petitioner becomes eligible under 
a new set of facts. See Matter ofKatigbak, 14 I&N Dec. 45, 49 (Reg'l Comm'r 1971). Although 
later-created evidence may sometimes document facts that existed at filing, the Petitioner relies here 
on later professional activity as additional qualifying accomplishments. That activity does not 
demonstrate error in our prior motion decision and does not establish new facts that warrant reopening 
our prior motion decision. The Petitioner largely relies on additional evidence of the same nature as 
the evidence addressed in the previous motion and disagrees with our conclusion that such evidence 
did not qualify as new facts material to the prior adjudication. Merely submitting additional 
documentation or reiterating an argument previously made does not establish a new fact showing that 
our prior motion decision should be reopened. 
The Petitioner therefore has not stated new facts, supported by documentary evidence, that warrant 
reopening our prior decision. Further, the evidence regarding the Petitioner's professional 
accomplishments occurring after the filing of the petition, such evidence does not establish that our 
prior determination concerning evidentiary record was erroneous. 
A motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision was based on an incorrect application of 
law or policy and that the decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the record of proceedings 
at the time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). Because the scope of a motion is limited to the 
prior decision, we will only review the latest decision in these proceedings. 8 C.F.R. § 103.S(a)(l)(i), 
(ii). We may grant motions that satisfy these requirements and demonstrate eligibility for the requested 
benefit. 
In our prior decision, we found that the Petitioner had not shown that our earlier decision was legally 
or procedurally incorrect. On the present motion, the Petitioner again argues that we improperly 
"minimized material newly submitted evidence," "misapplied the legal framework" of new facts, 
failed to evaluate the cumulative significance of her post decision professional accomplishments, and 
applied an unduly restrictive interpretation of the evidentiary standard applicable to motions. 
These assertions do not establish that our prior decision incorrectly applied law ofUSCIS policy. The 
Petitioner largely repeats her disagreement with our evaluation of the evidence and seeks a different 
outcome on the underlying extraordinary ability claim. The Petitioner does not identify a specific 
legal standard that we misstated or misapplied in dismissing the prior motion, not does she explain

how any current authority demonstrates error in that decision. Disagreement with the weight assigned 
to the evidence, without identification of a material factual or legal error, does not satisfy the 
requirements of a motion to reconsider. 
Because this is the third combined motion, the Petitioner must show that our prior motion dismissal 
itself contained a legal or policy error. The petitioner must therefore identify a specific erroneous 
application of law or policy in the prior decision, and authority demonstrating that the decision was 
incorrect as issued. The current arguments are substantively the same as those raised in the prior 
combined motions to reopen and reconsider, and they were already evaluated and dismissed. The 
Petitioner does not point to specific examples of where we misapplied the law. The Petitioner also 
does not identify any portion of the prior decision where we applied an incorrect legal standard. A 
disagreement with our weighing of the evidence does not constitute legal error and is insufficient to 
sustain a motion to reconsider. We reiterate that the purpose of a motion to reconsider is to 
demonstrate a misapplication of law or policy in the most recent prior decision, not to re-adjudicate 
the petition anew. See, e.g., Matter ofO-S-G-, 24 I&N Dec. 56, 58 (BIA 2006) (finding that a motion 
to reconsider is not a process by which the party may submit, in essence, the same brief and seek 
reconsideration by generally alleging error in the prior decision). 
On motion to reconsider, the Petitioner has not established that our previous decision was based on an 
incorrect application of law or policy at the time we issued our decision. Therefore, the motion will 
be dismissed. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
ORDER: The motion to reopen is dismissed. 
FURTHER ORDER: The motion to reconsider is dismissed.
