Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a general and operations manager, seeks classification as an alien ofextraordinary ability. 
See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding the Petitioner did not satisfy at least 
three of the initial evidentiary criteria. In addition, SCOPS found the Petitioner did not show he seeks to 
enter the United States to continue work in his area of expertise and his entry will substantially benefit 
prospectively the United States. The matter is now before us on appeal. 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance ofthe evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
Section 203(b)(l)(A)(ii) of the Act requires "the alien to enter the United States to continue work in 
the area of extraordinary ability." In addition, the regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(5) states that "the 
petition must be accompanied by clear evidence that the alien is coming to the United States to 
continue work in the area of expertise." 1 Further, section 203(b)(l)(A)(iii) of the Act requires "the 
alien's entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively the United States."2 The 
record reflects that as part ofrequest for evidence, SCOPS instructed the Petitioner to submit evidence 
relating to his intent to continue work in the United States and his entry would substantially benefit 
the United States. Specifically, SCOPS stated: 
1 See also generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(A)(2), https://www.uscis.gov/policymanual (providing that to qualify as 
an alien of extraordinary ability, the beneficiary must intend to continue to work in the area of expertise). 
2 See also generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, supra, F.2(A)(3) (indicating that there is not standard rule as to what will

It must be shown how the beneficiary will continue working in the United States in the 
claimed area of expertise. The petition does not indicate that the beneficiary has 
prearranged commitments for working in this field. The petitioner indicates that the 
beneficiary will work as a General and Operations Manager, whereas the documents 
that were submitted suggest instead that the beneficiary may be working [ ] as a Chief 
Executive Officer (CEO) and/or Chief Operations Officer (COO). 
Work as a CEO would not be in the field of endeavor, as a General and Operations 
Manager's responsibilities would be more limited than a CEO and/or COO, and if­
for example-the petitioner were to attempt to provide salary data for a General and 
Operations Manager, then the beneficiary's income acting as a CEO and/or a COO 
would not meet the criterion because the two positions do not have the same 
responsibilities and would therefore have different pay ranges. 
It must be shown how the beneficiary's entry will substantially benefit prospectively 
the United States. The petition does not indicate that the beneficiary's entry will 
substantially benefit prospectively the United States. The beneficiary has not met at 
least three (3) of the criteria; as such, it appears that he does not have sufficient acclaim 
to justify the assertion that his entry as a permanent resident to the United States would 
positively affect the country on a national level. 
In response, the Petitioner's cover letter did not address any of these issues. Moreover, the Petitioner 
resubmitted his intent to work letter without providing any additional documentation relating to these 
statutory requirements. In denying the petition, SCOPS determined that "no new documents were 
submitted to address th[e]s[e] issue[s]." 
On appeal, the Petitioner submits a brief addressing only one of the three grounds for the SCOPS' 
denial. Specifically, the Petitioner contends that he satisfied five categories of evidence. The 
Petitioner does not address or dispute SCOPS' decision regarding his intent to continue work in his 
area of expertise and his entry would substantially benefit the United States. 
Accordingly, we will not farther address these uncontested grounds on appeal, and we deem them to 
be waived. Any ground of ineligibility that is not raised on appeal is waived. See Matter ofO-R-E-, 
28 I&N Dec. 330,336 n.5 (BIA 2021) (citing Matter ofR-A-M-, 25 I&N Dec. 657,658 n.2 (BIA 202)). 
If the affected party does not address issues raised in the decision, and those issues are dispositive of 
the case, the appeal will be dismissed based on those waived issues. See, e.g., Matter ofM-A-S-, 24 
I&N Dec. 762, 767 n.2 (BIA 2009); see also Sarin v. United States, No. 2:22-CV-07498-SVW-JS, 
2023 WL 5667531, at *9 (C.D. Cal. July 27, 2023) (finding the plaintiff waived the issue of his intent 
to continue to work in the United States when he did not raise or contest it in his brief, and the AAO 
properly dismissed the appeal because the waived issued was dispositive). Since the Petitioner waived 
two of the three statutory requirements under section 203(b )(1 )(A)(ii) and (iii) of the Act and those 
are dispositive in establishing eligibility as an alien ofextraordinary ability under section 203(b )(1 )(A) 
of the Act, we decline to reach and hereby reserve the Petitioner's appellate arguments regarding his 
2 
possession ofextraordinary ability under section 203(b )(1 )(A)(i) ofthe Act. 3 See INS v. Bagamasbad,

The Petitioner waived the statutory requirements relating to his intent to continue to work in his area 
of expertise and his substantial benefit to the United States. The appeal will be dismissed for the 
above-stated reasons, with each considered as an independent and alternate basis for the decision. 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
