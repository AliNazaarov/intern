Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a computer and information research scientist, seeks to classify as an individual of 
extraordinary ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. 
§ 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens 
who can demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim 
and whose achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
SCOPS denied the petition, concluding that the record did not establish a petitioner received a major, 
internationally recognized award, or that the record satisfied at least three of the 10 initial evidentiary 
criteria. The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will withdraw the decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with the 
following analysis. 
SCOPS began the decision by stating, "Reference is made to Immigrant Petition for Alien Worker 
(Form 1-140) filed by [C-H-Y-] (the petitioner) on July 17, 2025. The petitioner seeks to classify [C­
H-Y-] (the beneficiary) as an alien of extraordinary ability in accordance with Section 203(b)(l)(A) 
of the Immigration and Nationality Act (Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the INA)." Two sentences later, 
SCOPS asserted, "Reference is made to Immigrant Petition for Alien Worker (Form 1-140) filed by 
[A-J-] (the petitioner) on July 9, 2025. The petitioner seeks to classify [A-J-] (the beneficiary) as an 
alien of extraordinary ability in accordance with Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Immigration and 
Nationality Act (Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the INA)." The remainder of SCOPS' decision references 
"the petitioner" and "the beneficiary" but it does not clarify which of the two petitioners and 
beneficiaries it purports to address. 
Given the decision's material ambiguity, it does not clearly convey which record it purports to address, 
the specific reason(s) for denial, or even which petitioner's Form 1-140 it denies. See 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.3(a)(l)(i) (requiring SCOPS to "explain in writing the specific reasons for denial"). 
Accordingly, we will withdraw SCOPS' decision. On remand, SCOPS should enter a new decision 
that clearly conveys which record it addresses and the specific reason(s) for denial, if any. SCOPS 
may request any additional evidence considered pertinent to the new determination. 
ORDER: The decision is withdrawn. The matter is remanded for the entry of a new decision 
consistent with the foregoing analysis.
