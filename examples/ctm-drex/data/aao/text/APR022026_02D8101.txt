Form 1-129, Petition for a Nonimmigrant Worker (Extraordinary Ability- 0) 
The Petitioner, a badminton club, seeks to classify the Beneficiary as an 0-1 nonimmigrant to work as 
a badminton coach in the United States. This 0-1 nonimmigrant visa classification is available to an alien 
who has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been 
demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim, and whose achievements have been 
recognized in the field through extensive documentation, and who seeks to enter the United States to 
continue work in the area of extraordinary ability. Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) Section 
101(a)(15)(O)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(O)(i). 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not satisfy 
the initial evidentiary requirements of submitting evidence of the Beneficiary's receipt of a major, 
internationally recognized award, 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(A), or at least three of the eight possible 
types of documentation listed under 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(B)(l)-(8). The matter is now before 
us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, Inc. , 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will withdraw the decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with the 
following analysis. 
I. LAW 
Section 101(a)(15)(O)(i) of the Act establishes 0-1 classification for an alien who has extraordinary 
ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics that has been demonstrated by sustained 
national or international acclaim, whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, and who seeks to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability. U.S . Department of Homeland Security (DHS) regulations define "extraordinary 
ability in the field of science, education, business, or athletics" as "a level of expertise indicating that the 
person is one ofthe small percentage who have arisen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2(o)(3)(ii). 
In addition, DHS regulations set forth initial evidentiary criteria for establishing an alien's sustained 
national or international acclaim and recognition for achievements in the field of endeavor. 
A petitioner may submit evidence either of the alien's receipt of "a major, internationally recognized 
award, such as a Nobel Prize," or of at least three of the eight listed categories of documents. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(A)-(B). If the petitioner demonstrates that a particular criterion does not readily 
apply to the alien's occupation, it may submit comparable evidence to establish eligibility. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(C). 
The petitioner's submission of documents satisfying the initial evidentiary criteria does not, in and of 
itself: establish the alien's eligibility for 0-1 classification. See 59 Fed. Reg. 41818 (Aug. 15, 1994) 
('The evidence submitted by the petitioner is not the standard for the classification, but merely the 
mechanism to establish whether the standard has been met."). Accordingly, where a petitioner 
provides qualifying evidence satisfying the initial evidentiary requirements, we will determine 
whether the totality of the record and the quality of the evidence show sustained national or 
international acclaim such that the alien is among the small percentage at the very top of the field of 
endeavor. See Section 10l(a)(15)(0)(i) of the Act; 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(ii), (iii). 1 
Moreover, the U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) Policy Manual explains: 
... [W]hen determining whether the beneficiary is coming to work in the beneficiary's 
"area of extraordinary ability," officers focus on whether the prospective work or 
services involve skillsets, knowledge, or expertise shared with the occupation(s) in 
which the beneficiary has garnered acclaim. In evaluating whether occupations involve 
shared skillsets, knowledge, or expertise to an extent that they may be considered 
within the same area of extraordinary ability, officers evaluate the totality of 
information and evidence presented .... 
See generally 2 USCIS Policy Manual M.4(F)(l), https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-
part-m-chapter-4.

badminton coach in the United States. It appears that the petition is for an alien who is transitioning 
to a new occupation, i.e., an athlete coming to the United States to be a coach. See generally 2 USCIS 
Policy Manual, supra, M.4(F)(l). Upon remand, FOD should evaluate the totality of information and 
evidence presented to determine if the Beneficiary is coming to work in his "area of extraordinary 
ability," "focus[ing] on whether the prospective work or services involve skillsets, knowledge, or 
expertise shared with the occupation(s) in which the [B]eneficiary [claims to have] garnered acclaim." 
See id. The factors to consider include but not limited to: 
• Whether the past and prospective occupations are in the same industry or are

III. CONCLUSION 
Based on the issues we have discussed, including FOD not folly addressing evidence the Petitioner 
has presented concerning the Beneficiary's "membership in associations," referenced under 8 C.F.R. 
§ 214.2(o)(3)(iii)(B)(2), as well as whether the Beneficiary is seeking to enter the United States to 
continue work in his area of extraordinary ability, we are remanding the matter to allow FOD to 
consider all materials in the record and for the entry of a new decision. 
ORDER: The decision is withdrawn. The matter is remanded for the entry of a new decision 
consistent with the foregoing analysis.
