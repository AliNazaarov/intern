Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a poet, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability in the arts. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b )(1 )(A), 8 U.S .C. § l l 53(b )(1 )(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
SCOPS denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not establish either that she received a 
major, internationally recognized award, or that she satisfied at least three ofthe 10 initial evidentiary 
criteria. The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b )( 1) of the Act makes visas available to immigrants with extraordinary ability if: 
(i) the alien bas extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or 
athletics which bas been demonstrated by sustained national or international 
acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, 
(ii) the alien seeks to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability, and 
(iii) the alien's entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively 
the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate sustained 
acclaim and the recognition of their achievements in the field through a one-time achievement (that 
is, a major, internationally recognized award). If that petitioner does not submit this evidence, then 
they must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the 10 categories 
listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) (including items such as awards, published material in certain 
media, and scholarly articles). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner described her endeavor as a plan to introduce her poetry to audiences. 
As noted above, SCOPS observed that the record does not establish the Petitioner received a one-time 
achievement of a major, internationally recognized award. SCOPS concluded that the record satisfies 
the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii), but not those at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(iii), and (v), 
noting that the Petitioner did not assert eligibility under other criteria. On appeal, the Petitioner 
reasserts that the record satisfies the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(iii), and (v), in addition to 
the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii). The Petitioner does not assert on appeal-and the record 
does not support the conclusion-that the record satisfies the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(iv), 
(vi), or (viii)-(x). As discussed below, the record does not satisfy at least three of the 10 criteria listed 
at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). 
A. Membership in Associations 
The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii) requires "[d]ocumentation of the alien's membership in 
associations in the field for which classification is sought, which require outstanding achievements of 
their members, as judged by recognized national or international experts in their disciplines or fields." 
SCOPS acknowledged that the record establishes the Petitioner is a member of the Russian Writers 
Union (RWU) and the International Union of Writers (IUW), and that she is a corresponding member 
of International Academy of Science and Arts (IASA). 1 However, SCOPS observed that the record 
does not establish the associations require outstanding achievements of their members and that 
membership in the associations is judged by recognized national or international experts in their 
disciplines or fields. Therefore, SCOPS concluded that the record does not satisfy the criterion at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii).

C. Original Contributions of Major Significance 
The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v) requires "[e]vidence of the alien's original scientific, 
scholarly, artistic, athletic, or business-related contributions of major significance in the field." 
SCOPS acknowledged that the record contains information about the Petitioner's published work and 
a letter of recommendation. However, SCOPS observed that neither the letter of recommendation nor 
the remainder of the record establishes how the Petitioner's contributions may have had major 
significance in the field. Therefore, SCOPS concluded that the record does not satisfy the criterion at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v). 
On appeal, the Petitioner reasserts that letters and other documentation in the record establish that her 
published work constitutes contributions of major significance in the field, as contemplated by the 
criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v). 
The record contains English translations of published reviews of the Petitioner's books, which 
constitute original artistic contributions. However, the reviews are similar to common literary reviews 
in which authors generally praise each other, and they do not clarify how the Petitioner's original 
contributions may have had major significance in the field, distinguishable from merely contributing 
to the growing number of published books for centuries. Moreover, some purported reviews include 
marketing statements, such as "The book is available for purchase on the website of I II ~" demonstrating that, like some of the awards discussed above, the reviews are

contributions common among other authors, it does not establish that her contributions are of major 
significance in the field. Therefore, the record does not satisfy the criterion at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(v). 
Because the record would not satisfy at least three of the 10 criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x), 
even if it satisfied the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii), we reserve our opinion regarding that 
criterion. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (stating that agencies are not required to 
make "purely advisory findings" on issues that are unnecessary to the ultimate decision); Matter of 
L-A-C-, 26 I&N Dec. 516, 526 n.7 (BIA 2015) (declining to reach alternative issues on appeal where 
an applicant is otherwise ineligible). 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner has not established she received a one-time achievement or, in the alternative, submitted 
evidence that meets at least three of the 10 criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). As a result, we 
need not provide the type of final merits determination referenced in Kazarian, 596 F .3d at 1119-20. 
See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. at 25. Nevertheless, we have reviewed the record in the aggregate; 
7 
however, we determine that it does not support a conclusion that the Petitioner has established the 
acclaim and recognition required for the classification sought. For example, much of the evidence 
discussed above pertains to few, isolated instances shortly before the Petitioner filed the Form I-140 
in 2025, which does not demonstrate sustained national or international acclaim. See Kazarian, 
596 F.3d at 1119-20 (citing section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act; 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(2)-(3)). 
The Petitioner has not shown that the significance of her work is indicative of the required sustained 
national or international acclaim or that it is consistent with a "career of acclaimed work in the field" 
as contemplated by Congress. H.R. Rep. No. 101-723, 59 (Sept. 19, 1990); see also section 
203(b)(l)(A) of the Act. Moreover, the record does not otherwise demonstrate that the Petitioner has 
garnered national or international acclaim in the field, and that she is one of the small percentage who 
has risen to the very top ofthe field of endeavor. See section 203(b )(l)(A) ofthe Act; see also 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(2).
