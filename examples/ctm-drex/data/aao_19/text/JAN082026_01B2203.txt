Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an automotive design engineer, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. 
See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner had satisfied 
only two of the initial evidentiary criteria, of which he must meet at least three. The matter is now 
before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F .R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christa's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
The Petitioner asserts that he qualifies as an alien of extraordinary ability based on his work as an 
automotive design engineer. SCOPS determined that the Petitioner met two of the initial evidentiary 
criteria, however, SCOPS ultimately determined that the Petitioner did not meet the third asserted 
criterion, of having an original contribution of major significance. 8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(v). The 
Petitioner states on appeal that SCOPS misidentified his field ofwork as cloud computing and software 
engineering rather than automative design engineering and this resulted in an error in analysis. We 
acknowledge that SCOPS' decision mistakenly uses the term "cloud computing and software 
engineering" in reference to the Petitioner's field of work. However, upon review, we conclude that 
this claimed error was, at most, harmless. See generally Matter ofO-R-E-, 28 l&N Dec. 330, 336 n.5 
(BIA 2021) (citing cases regarding harmless or scrivener's errors). 
The decision accurately states that the Petitioner "intends to work as a design engineer." Additionally, 
SCOPS properly analyzed the Petitioner's work and the letters of recommendation submitted in 
support of the Petitioner's claim of an original contribution of major significance. For example, 
SCOPS details the Petitioner's work in the development of vehicle body structures and determines 
that the letters of recommendation "recount the Petitioner's work, indicate its use in products for his 
employer, and comment on their potential and possible future applications, [but] do not demonstrate 
that his work has made the required impact in the field." Therefore, we conclude that although SCOPS 
mistakenly referenced an incorrect field of work, SCOPS properly analyzed the evidence and its error 
did not impact the outcome of the decision. 
The Petitioner does not offer any additional errors to evaluate on appeal. We note that the Petitioner 
stated on the Form I-290B, Notice ofAppeal or Motion, that his brief and/ or additional evidence would 
be submitted to the AAO within 30 calendar days of filing the appeal. To date, we have not received 
a brief or additional evidence. Accordingly, we must dismiss the appeal. 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
