Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See Immigration and 
Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference 
classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their extraordinary 
ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been 
recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition and the matter is now before us on appeal 
pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden of proof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
In our review of the Petitioner's Form I-290B, Notice of Appeal or Motion, we determine that the 
signature provided by the Petitioner is not acceptable. U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services 
(USCIS) requires a valid signature on applications, petitions, requests, and certain other documents 
filed with USCIS, including the Form I-290B. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(a)(2) (explaining that an applicant 
or petitioner must sign their benefit request). For purposes of appeals and motions, the affected party 
must personally sign their own Form I-290B before filing it with USCIS. See 
8 C.F.R. § 103.3(a)(l)(iii)(B) (stating that the affected party is the person with legal standing in a 
proceeding). 
The Form I-290B instructions also specify that the affected party must sign the form, except in limited 
circumstances. Instructions for Form I-290B, https://www.uscis.gov/i-290b. Signatures created by 
typewriter, auto-pen, stamp, or other similar device are not acceptable. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(a)(2); 1 
USCIS Policy Manual B.2(B), https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual (providing guidance about 
acceptable signatures). 
Part 4.4 ofthe Form I-290B, the section requiring an applicant or petitioner's signature, was completed 
using a script-like font rather than the Petitioner's actual signature and is identical to the one found in 
Part 4.2.a ofthe Form G-28, Notice of Entry ofAppearance as Attorney or Accredited Representative. 
Although the Form r-290B was receipted and accepted, users "does not provide an opportunity to 
correct ( or cure) a deficient signature", and "[i]f users accepts a request for adjudication and later 
determines that it has a deficient signature, users denies the request." See generally I users Policy 
Manual, supra, at B.2(A). As such, the Form r-290B does not contain a valid signature from the 
Petitioner and we must dismiss the appeal. 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
