Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a business executive, requests classification under the employment-based, first­
preference (EB-1) immigrant visa category as an alien with "extraordinary ability." See Immigration 
and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203 (b)(1 )(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b )(1 )(A). Successful petitioners 
for U.S. permanent residence in this category must demonstrate "sustained national or international 
acclaim" and extensively document recognition of their achievements in their fields. Section 
203(b)( 1 )(A)(i) of the Act. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the record did not establish 
the Petitioner received a one-time achievement of a major, internationally recognized award, or, in the 
alternative, that the Petitioner satisfied at least three of the initial evidentiary criteria. The matter is 
now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christa 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
An alien is eligible for the extraordinary ability classification if he or she has extraordinary ability in 
the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained national 
or international acclaim and his or her achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation; he or she seeks to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability; and his or her entry will substantially benefit prospectively the United States. 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate a one­
time achievement (that is, a major, internationally recognized award). Ifthe petitioner does not submit 
this evidence, then the petitioner must provide documentation that meets at least three of the ten 
categories listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) to (x) (including items such as awards, published material 
in certain media, and scholarly articles). The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4) allows a petitioner 
to submit comparable material if the petitioner is able to demonstrate that the standards at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) do not readily apply to the individual's occupation. 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1119-20 (9th Cir. 
2010); see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. 
Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011). 
II. ANALYSIS 
Because the Petitioner has not claimed or established his receipt of a major, internationally recognized 
award, he must satisfy at least three ofthe alternate regulatory criteria under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)­
(x). SCOPS determined the Petitioner met only one (authorship of scholarly articles in the field in 
professional or major trade publication or other major media under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi)) of the 
seven claimed categories of evidence. On appeal, the Petitioner asserts that he fulfills the criterion 
SCOPS determined he met as well as the remaining criteria he claimed. We disagree. 
Upon our de novo review, we agree with SCOPS and conclude the Petitioner did not demonstrate 
eligibility under the criteria contained in 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i), (ii), (iii), (iv), (v). and (viii). But 
we withdraw SCOPS' determination that the record satisfies the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi). 
As we conclude the Petitioner has not met the initial evidence requirements to demonstrate his 
extraordinary ability, we are not required to consider the totality of the material provided in a final 
merits determination and assess whether the record reflects the Petitioner's national or international 
acclaim and that he is amongst the small percentage at the very top of his field of endeavor. See 
Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1110-20 ( citing section 203(b )(1 )(A) ofthe Act; 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2) and (3)). 
Accordingly, we reserve it. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (stating that agencies are 
not required to make "purely advisory findings" on issues that are unnecessary to the ultimate 
decision). So, we will dismiss the Petitioner's appeal. 1

The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii) requires "[d]ocumentation of the alien's membership in 
associations in the field for which for which classification is sought, which require outstanding 
achievements of their members, as judged by recognized national or international experts in their

The Petitioner claimed that he enjoyed "[i]individual council membership [as a] Council Member of 
the Water Transportation Management Professional Committee of China Institute ofNavigation" and 
submitted a membership certificate with an uncertified English translation. The uncertified translation

a copy ofthe constitution ofChina Institute ofNavigation, and webpages describing the China Institute

In its decision SCOPS concluded the Petitioner did not meet this criterion and we agree. The record 
does not demonstrate that the Petitioner's claimed "[i]individual council membership [as a] Council 
Member of the Water Transportation Management Professional Committee of China Institute of 
Navigation" requires outstanding achievement. An individual member of the China Institute of 
Navigation need only be "[ a ]ny expert, scholar, researcher having relatively great influence in the 
disciplines and relevant fields as well as persons who have certain profession skills or knowledge." 
None of the evidence in the record demonstrates that the requirements of individual members rise to 
a level of a requirement of outstanding achievement for admission as a member. The record also 
does not contain evidence to establish the Petitioner's admittance to membership and evaluation of 
achievements was judged by recognized national or international experts in their disciplines or fields. 
In response to the RFE, the Petitioner contends that he has council membership in the China Institute 
ofNavigation. The evidence submitted in the record denotes criteria for various levels ofmembership. 
But council level membership is not described in the constitution of the China Institute of Navigation 
constitution. Nor does any of the evidence in the record reflect the Petitioner's council level 
membership. As stated previously, the certificate the Petitioner submitted merely reflects that he was 
selected as deputy director of a committee organized within China Institute of Navigation. 
The Petitioner also states that his election as deputy director qualifies him not as an individual member 
as described in his memberships certificate, but as a senior member. But the record does not 
preponderantly support this assertion with material, relevant, or probative evidence. The record does 
not contain evidence that would establish that leadership positions in the China Institute of 
Navigations' Transportation Management Professional Committee has any relation to level of 
membership, public or core corporate. 
We recognize that there is no express requirement that the nomenclature used by associations in the 
field offering membership specify "outstanding achievement" as the requirement. Other expressions 
of eligibility, such as "distinguishment" can potentially meet the requirements. However, in every 
5 
case, the Petitioner bears the burden to demonstrate that the specific requirements or expression of 
requirements demonstrates outstanding achievement. And that is where the Petitioner's claim to 
eligibility, such that it is, falters. The Petitioner has not submitted adequately persuasive material, 
relevant, or probative evidence that demonstrates that "relatively great influence," "significant 
professional achievements," or "great influence" as described in the individual and senior membership 
entries in the China Institute of Navigation constitution relates to a requirement of outstanding 
achievements for membership. 
Moreover, it is not sufficiently evident if the achievements required of any level of membership in 
the China Institute of Navigation ( council, individual, or senior) are judged by recognized national or 
international experts in their disciplines or fields. The Petitioner submitted an uncertified translation 
of a certificate noting the Petitioner was selected as a deputy director by "the Chairperson's 
Committee." And, in response to the RFE, the Petitioner stated that the constitution of the China 
Institute of Navigation provides for judging of membership applications by "the Council." However, 
the record does not contain evidence describing the credential of the individuals who make up the 
Council. We are unable to determine if the Council is a body of recognized national or international 
experts in the discipline or field. 
So, we conclude the Petitioner has not demonstrated eligibility under this criterion.

F. "Evidence ofthe alien's authorship ofscholarly articles in the field, in professional or major

The record does not preponderantly establish that the scholarly articles the Petitioner claims to have 
authored were published in professional or major trade publications or other major media. The articles 
contained in the record were rublishr in publications named China Shipping Gazette and The 
Frontiers of Chinese Talents The record contains several documents describing the

prohibits an evaluation of the nature of the journal and if it is a professional or major trade publication

conclusion to the contrary. 
G. We Reserve The Remaining Claimed Criteria 
We note the Petitioner claims he met an additional criterion related to performing in a leading or 
critical role for organizations or establishments that have a distinguished reputation. However, as 
noted above, the Petitioner's unsuccessful demonstration of his eligibility under the criteria we 
discussed means he cannot numerically meet the required number of at least three criteria out of the 
10 available and the seven he claimed. Accordingly, discussion of the Petitioner's remaining claim 
would serve no legal purpose and we reserve it. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (per 
curiam) (holding that agencies are not required to make "purely advisory findings" on issues that are 
unnecessary to the ultimate decision). 
III. CONCLUSION 
Congress set a very high benchmark for aliens of extraordinary ability by requiring the petitioner 
demonstrate "sustained national or international acclaim" and present "extensive documentation" to 
show his or her achievements have been recognized in the field. Section 203(b )(1 )(A)(i) of the Act. 
Here, because the Petitioner has not satisfied the threshold "initial evidence" requirements at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3), we cannot conclude that he is an alien of extraordinary ability and need not further 
analyze the merits under the statute. See Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20; see also Nagaiah v. Allen, -

Regardless, the Petitioner seeks a highly restrictive visa classification, intended for individuals already 
at the top of their respective fields, rather than those progressing toward the top. Matter ofPrice, 20 
I&N Dec. 953, 954 (Assoc. Comm'r 1994) (concluding that even major league level athletes do not 
automatically meet the statutory standards for classification as an individual of "extraordinary 
ability,"); Amin v. Mayorkas, 24 F.4th at 394 (stating that extraordinary ability is such an elite level of 
accomplishment); Lapteva v. Schuurmans, No. 8:24CV83, 2025 WL 815087 at *l (stating that EB-1 
visa are intended to be available to only an exceedingly small amount ofindividuals with extraordinary 
ability). In this case, the Petitioner has not shown the significance of his work is indicative of the 
required sustained national or international acclaim or it is consistent with a career of acclaimed work 
in the field. See Section 203(b )(1 )(A) of the Act. Whilst the Petitioner need not establish that there is 
no one more accomplished to qualify for the classification sought, the record is insufficient to 
demonstrate that he is among the small percentage at the top of his field. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
For the reasons discussed above, the Petitioner has not demonstrated his eligibility as an alien of 
extraordinary ability. The appeal will be dismissed for the above-stated reasons, with each considered 
as an independent and alternate basis for the decision.
