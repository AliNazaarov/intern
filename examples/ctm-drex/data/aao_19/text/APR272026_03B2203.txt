Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a floral designer, seeks classification as an alien ofextraordinary ability. See Immigration 
and Nationality Act (the Act) Section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference 
classification makes immigrant visas available to aliens who can demonstrate their extraordinary ability 
through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in 
their field through extensive documentation. 
SCOPS denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not satisfy the initial evidence 
requirements for this classification, either by documenting her receipt of a major internationally 
recognized award, or by meeting at least three ofthe ten evidentiary criteria set forth in the regulations. 
The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance ofthe evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa's, Inc. , 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will withdraw the decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with the 
following analysis. 
I. LAW 
To qualify for this immigrant visa category, a petitioner must demonstrate that they: 
• Have "extraordinary ability" in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has 
been demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim; 
• Seek to continue work in their field of expertise in the United States; and 
• Would offer substantial prospective benefits to the country. 
Section 203(b )(1 )(A)(i)-(iii) of the Act. 
The term "extraordinary ability" means expertise commensurate with "one of that small percentage 
who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). Unless a petitioner 
received "a major, international recognized award," they must satisfy at least three of ten initial 
evidentiary criteria. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). If a petitioner meets either evidentiary standard and the 
requirements at sections 203(b )(1 )(A)(ii), (iii) of the Act, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services 
(USCIS) must then make a final merits determination. To merit approval, the record must establish a 
petitioner's "sustained national or international acclaim" and show that they have recognized 
achievements placing them among the small percentage at the very top of their field. See Kazarian v. 
USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1119-20 (9th Cir. 2010) (establishing a two-step process for adjudicating 
extraordinary ability petitions); see also Amin v. Mayorkas, 24 F.4th 383,391 (5th Cir. 2022) (finding 
USCIS' two-step extraordinary ability analysis "consistent with the governing statute and regulation"); 
see generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner is a professional floral designer with approximately 15 years of experience in the field. 
She began her career in Ukraine and later founded I l a Florida-based retail floristry 
business that serves clients inl l's luxury hospitality industry. 
A. Evidentiary Criteria 
The Petitioner does not claim that she has received a major internationally recognized award. 
Therefore, she must demonstrate that she meets at least three of the ten initial evidentiary criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). SCOPS denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner submitted 
evidence satisfying only two of seven claimed criteria. Specifically, SCOPS determined that the 
Petitioner demonstrated that she has participated as a judge of the work of others in her field and has 
displayed her floral design work at artistic exhibitions or showcases. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv) 
and (vii). The record supports SCOPS' conclusion that the Petitioner satisfied these two criteria. 
On appeal, the Petitioner contests SCOPS' determinations that she did not satisfy the criteria at 8 
C.F.R. 204.5(h)(3)(i), (v) and (viii). She asserts that she has received a lesser nationally recognized 
award, made original artistic contributions of major significance in her field, and served in a leading 
role with an organization that has a distinguished reputation. 1 
Upon de novo review, we conclude that the Petitioner submitted sufficient evidence to meet the 
criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) and has therefore satisfied the initial evidence requirements for 
the requested classification. 
The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) calls for "[d]ocumentation of the alien's receipt oflesser 
nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards for excellence in the field of endeavor." We 
first determine whether the alien is the recipient of prizes or awards, and then whether the award is 
nationally or internationally recognized and was granted based upon excellence in the alien's field of 
endeavor. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, supra, at F.2(B)(l).

Examples of qualifying awards under this criterion may include certain awards from well-known

field and that the award, which was issued to her based on her outstanding performance in a long­

questioned whether the award is one which would have garnered the Petitioner "sustained national or 
international acclaim," but such an inquiry is more appropriate at the final merits stage. See generally, 
id. (noting that the question of whether a petitioner is one of that small percentage who have risen to 
the very top of the field of endeavor and enjoys sustained national or international acclaim should be 
addressed in the final merits determination). 
For the reasons discussed, we find the Petitioner has established by a preponderance of the evidence 
that she satisfies the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i). 
B. Final Merits Determination 
Based on the foregoing discussion, the Petitioner has satisfied at least three of the ten evidentiary 
requirements at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x).2 We will therefore withdraw SCOPS' decision, because 
that decision was based on a determination that the Petitioner did not satisfy the initial evidence 
requirements for this classification. 
However, granting three criteria does not suffice to establish eligibility for the classification the 
Petitioner seeks. USCIS must now make a final merits determination on the Petitioner's filing and 
consider whether the record establishes the sustained national or international acclaim and recognized 
achievements sufficient to place the Petitioner among the small percentage at the very top of her field. 
See section 203(b )(l)(A)(i) ofthe Act; 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2), (3); see also Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-
20. SCOPS did not make such a finding. Rather than make this determination in the first instance, 
we will remand the matter to SCOPS. 
The final merits determination should weigh the evidence submitted in support of all claimed initial 
evidentiary criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x), any other potentially relevant evidence in the 
record (including evidence that does not fit one of the evidentiary criteria), and the Petitioner's claims 
and evidence on appeal. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, supra, at F.2(B)(2).
