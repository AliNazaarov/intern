Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an accounting and tax consultant, requests classification under the employment-based, 
first-preference (EB-1) immigrant visa category as an alien with "extraordinary ability." See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b )(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b )(l)(A). Successful 
petitioners for U.S. permanent residence status in this category must demonstrate "sustained national 
or international acclaim" and extensively document recognition of their achievements in their fields. 
Section 203(b )(1 )(A)(i) of the Act. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition and dismissed the Petitioner's six following 
combined motions to reopen and reconsider. SCOPS concluded that she met one of ten initial 
evidentiary requirements for the requested immigrant visa category - two less than needed for a final 
merits determination. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). We dismissed the Petitioner's appeal, affirming 
SCOPS' findings that her sixth combined motions did not meet applicable requirements. See In Re: 
30146595 (AAO Aug. 27, 2025). 
The matter returns to us on the Petitioner's combined motions to reopen and reconsider. She bears the 
burden of demonstrating eligibility for the requested benefit by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we conclude that neither 
motion meets applicable requirements nor demonstrates her eligibility for the requested benefit. We 
will therefore dismiss them. 
I. LAW 
A motion to reopen must state new facts, supported by documentary evidence. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2). 
In contrast, a motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision misapplied law or policy 
based on the record at the time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). On motion, regulations limit 
our scope of review to our prior decision. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(l)(i), (ii) (referring to "the prior 
decision" and "the latest decision in the proceeding"). We may grant motions that satisfy these 
requirements and demonstrate eligibility for the requested benefit. 
II. ANALYSIS 
Our prior decision upheld SCOPS' findings that, contrary to applicable requirements, the Petitioner's 
last motion to reopen did not state new facts or contain documentary evidence. See 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.5(a)(2). We also found that her last motion to reconsider did not demonstrate SCOPS' 
misapplication oflaw or facts. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). 
A. The Motion to Reopen 
On motion, the Petitioner submits a letter from herself and a copy of a 2018 tax registration certificate 
for a business she operates. The letter states new facts, indicating that she has petitioned U.S. 
Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) "for another work visa in a different category." But 
the Petitioner did not submit documentary evidence of the other petition. Also, the new information 
does not demonstrate that her last motion to reopen contained new facts or documentary evidence, or 
that her last motion to reconsider demonstrated SCOPS' misapplication of law or facts. See 8 C.F .R. 
§ 103.5(a)(l)(i), (ii) (limiting the scope of our jurisdiction to our prior decision). 
The remainder of the Petitioner's letter discusses her arrival in the United States 19 years ago and her 
business activities in this country. The record, however, already contained this information. Thus, 
the letter's remainder lacks new facts and does not demonstrate her eligibility for the requested benefit. 
The copy of the 2018 tax registration certificate is documentary evidence. But the record shows that 
the Petitioner's initial petition filing included the document. Thus, the tax registration certificate is 
not a new fact and, having been already considered, does not demonstrate her eligibility for the 
requested benefit. We will therefore dismiss the motion to reopen. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4) 
(requiring dismissal of"[a] motion that does not meet applicable requirements"). 
B. The Motion to Reconsider 
The Petitioner contends that she previously submitted "numerous affidavits reflecting new facts and 
other new information." The record, however, shows that her last motions did not contain new facts 
or new information. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.S(a)(l)(i), (ii) (limiting the scope of our jurisdiction to our 
prior decision). Her last filings included only her Form 1-290B, Notice of Appeal or Motion, and her 
letter, which reasserted previously stated facts. 
The Petitioner also argues that she qualifies for adjustment of status in the United States. See section 
245(a) of the Act, 8 U.S.C. § 1255(a). Referring to an adjustment bar under section 245(c)(2) of the 
Act, she states: "[M]y inability to maintain my lawful nonirnrnigrant status was through no fault of 
my own or for technical reasons." We, however, generally lack jurisdiction to review an alien's 
eligibility to adjust their status. See 8 C.F.R. § 245.2(a)(5)(ii) ("No appeal lies from the denial of an 
application [for adjustment of status]."). Also, the Petitioner's eligibility to adjust (or lack thereof) 
does not demonstrate that her last motion to reopen in this petition proceedings stated new facts and 
contained documentary evidence or that our prior decision misapplied law or policy. See 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.S(a)(l)(i), (ii) (limiting the scope of our jurisdiction to our prior decision).

III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner's motions to reopen and reconsider neither meet applicable requirements nor 
demonstrate her eligibility for the requested benefit. We will therefore affirm her appeal's dismissal. 
ORDER: The motion to reopen is dismissed. 
FURTHER ORDER: The motion to reconsider is dismissed.
