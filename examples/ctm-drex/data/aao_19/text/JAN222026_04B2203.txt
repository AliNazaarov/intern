Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an epidemiologist, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See section 
203(b)(l)(A) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding the Petitioner was not the 
recipient of a major, internationally recognized award, and, while he demonstrated five of the ten 
regulatory criteria, he did not show sustained national or international acclaim and that he is among 
the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. The matter is now before us on appeal 
pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden of proof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 l&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b )(1 )(A) of the Act makes immigrant visas available to aliens with extraordinary ability if: 
they have extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which have been 
demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim; they seek to enter the United States to 
continue work in the area of extraordinary ability; and their entry will substantially benefit the United 
States prospectively. The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small 
percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
The implementing regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis of the evidence 
to determine whether a petitioner has sustained national or international acclaim and whether their 
achievements have been recognized in the field. First, a petitioner can provide evidence of either "a 
major, international recognized award," or satisfaction of at least three of ten evidentiary criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). 1 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration 
Services (USCIS) then considers the totality of the material provided in a final merits determination 
and assesses whether the record shows sustained national or international acclaim and demonstrates 
that the individual is among the small percentage at the very top ofthe field of endeavor. See Kazarian 
v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1121 (9th Cir. 2010) (discussing a two-step review where the documentation 
is frrst counted and then, if fulfilling the required number of criteria, considered in the context of a 
final merits determination); see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); 
Rijal v. USCJS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011); see generally 6 USCJS Policy Manual 
F.2(B), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual (providing, as guidance, USCIS' two-step extraordinary ability 
analysis).

The criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix) requires evidence that the Petitioner demonstrate he has 
commanded a high salary or other significantly high remuneration for services, in relation to others in 
the field. However, the Petitioner has not provided comparative data to demonstrate that his salary is 
high compared to others in the field. For example, the Petitioner provided a letter stating he has been 
working as a technician for the I I since 2019 with a monthly remuneration 
of 8,500 Brazilian Reals (R$) per month. He provided a salary stub from 2018 showing he was paid 
R$7,624 for work in technical coordination of environmental surveillance. However, he does not 
provide comparative wage or remuneration data for epidemiologists during this time. Rather, he 
provides general 2025 pay data for biologists, with no explanation for how this amounts to comparative 
data in the field of epidemiology. Relevant caselaw has confirmed that "in relation to others in the 
field" should be interpreted as allowing comparisons to those employed in the same occupation at a 
similar level. See Matter of Price, 20 I&N Dec. 953, 954 (Assoc. Comm'r 1994) (considering a 
professional golfer's earnings versus other PGA Tour golfers); see also Skokos v. U.S. Dept. of 
Homeland Sec., 420 F. App'x 712, 713-14 (9th Cir. 2011) (finding salary information for those 
performing lesser duties is not a comparison to others in the field); Crimson v. INS, 934 F. Supp. 965, 
968 (N.D. Ill. 1996) (considering NHL enforcer's salary versus other NHL enforcers); Muni v. INS, 
891 F. Supp. 440, 444-45 (N. D. Ill. 1995) (comparing salary of NHL defensive player to salary of 
other NHL defensemen). Even if we were to use the pay data as comparison, we note that it only 
provides an average and does not identify salaries for those who are high earners. Nor does the data 
consider geographical or position-appropriate compensation. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, 
supra, at F .2(8)( 1) (providing, as guidance, evidence regarding whether the person's compensation is 
high relative to that of others working in the field may include geographical or position-appropriate 
compensation surveys and organizational justifications to pay above the compensation data) ( emphasis 
added). 
As a result, the Petitioner has not established the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii), (viii), and (ix) 
and we withdraw SCOPS' findings to the contrary.

E. Final Merits Determination 
Congress set a very high benchmark for aliens of extraordinary ability by requiring the petitioner 
demonstrate "sustained national or international acclaim" and present "extensive documentation" to 
show his or her achievements have been recognized in the field. Section 203(b)(l)(A)(i) of the 
Act. The visa classification is highly restrictive, intended for individuals already at the top of their 
5 
respective fields. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2) (explaining "extraordinary ability" refers only to those 
in "that small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor"). Here, because the 
Petitioner has not satisfied the threshold "initial evidence" requirements at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), we 
cannot conclude that he is an alien of extraordinary ability. See also Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20. 
However, because SCOPS dismissed on a final merits determination, we will review the record in the 
aggregate. 
The Petitioner received two awards in the field at two scientific meetings held in 2021 and 2022 but 
did not demonstrate that his awards were nationally or internationally recognized. While the Petitioner 
argues on appeal that SCOPS did not give enough weight to his awards, he does not explain how they 
demonstrate he has garnered recognition on a national or international scale, consistent with being 
among the small percentage at the very top ofthe field of endeavor. The Petitioner also contends that 
SCOPS did not properly consider that he is a member of the COE, but as discussed above, he did not 
show that his membership required outstanding achievements as judged by recognized national or 
international experts in the field and he does not explain how his membership demonstrates sustained 
national or international acclaim. In addition, although the Petitioner claimed he held leading and 
critical roles for programs run by the I land the municipality ofl lhe 
does not demonstrate that the roles he had were for distinguished organizations or establishments and 
that they show he is among the small percentage who has risen to the very top of his field. 
We also consider that the Petitioner provided his salary data but, as noted above, he did not provide 
comparative data for epidemiologists and has therefore not demonstrated his salary is for one who has 
risen to the very top of his field of endeavor. We also reviewed the published material about the 
Petitioner. In addition to concluding that they did not appear in professional or major trade 
publications or other major media, we also determine that the materials did not contain a substantial 
discussion of his work to show his achievements have been recognized in the field. For example, the 
Petitioner submitted a 2013 article from! !which identifies him as, "a biologist and 
technical reference on accidents by venomous animals at the 
----,----------,-----~
and summarizes him saying that "accidents by venomous animals increase during the rainy season and 
thatl lis one ofthe states that most often reports accidents by venomous animals in Brazil." 
The article did not elaborate more than this to, for example, discuss his experience or background or 
how he came by this conclusion in his work. Similarly, he submitted a 2019 article froml l 
which included only a brief passing reference about him. The article identified him as a biologist and 
technical coordinator and noted his presence at a health surveillance meeting of a neighboring town. 
He also included references to himself on government websites which use the same quote as above 
and reference his presence at a training on venomous animals. As a result, the submitted published 
material did not demonstrate a level of success among that small percentage who have risen to the 
very top of the field of endeavor, achieved sustained national or international acclaim, or a career of 
acclaimed work. See Bodhankar v. United States Citizenship & Immigr. Servs., No. 
119CV706MADCFH, 2020 WL 777211, at *5 (N.D.N.Y. Feb. 18, 2020) (finding the lack of press 
about the plaintiff was not consistent with national or international acclaim).

significance in the field. For example, the Petitioner submitted recommendation letters, which discuss

author, who identifies herself as a public servant at the '------------~ claims the 
Petitioner implemented innovative strategies that resulted in significant reduction of poisoning

him as being at the top of his field. 
We acknowledge that the Petitioner met the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v) and submitted 
evidence that he authored articles in professional journals such as the Journal of Applied 
Pharmaceutical Science and the American Journal ofField Epidemiology. The Petitioner claims that 
his publication record supports his extraordinary ability claim. However, authorship and publication 
of original research do not automatically place an individual at the top of their field. Here, the 
Petitioner has not provided evidence that his published research has been cited by others to 
demonstrate sustained national or international acclaim and that he is among the small percentage at 
the top of the field. We also give weight to the evidence demonstrating that the Petitioner served on 
a debating panel for graduate students in microbiology at thel Iin 
2016, a panel by the.__ ___________,evaluating candidates of an applied epidemiology 
training program and served as a member of the scientific committee at a clinal pharmacology 
symposium judging abstracts submitted to the Journal of Applied Pharmaceutical Sciences in 2020. 
However, the Petitioner did not provide background information on the panels and symposium to 
demonstrate the significance of his experience as the judge of others and whether it indicates the 
required extraordinary ability for this highly restrictive classification. Moreover, the Petitioner did 
not show, for example, how his judging experience compares to others at the very top of the field, or 
that he gained wide attention from the field based on his evaluation work. See Hamal v. United States 
Dep 't ofHomeland Sec., No. 21-5160, 2023 WL 1156801, at *2 (finding the petitioner did not show 
that he served as a judge of renowned directors as opposed to aspiring artists or the competitions were 
considered prestigious or attracted significant attention by the field). 
Based on our de novo review, the Petitioner has not shown that his achievements have been recognized 
in the field through extensive documentation and that the significance of his work is indicative of the 
required sustained national or international acclaim. See section 203(b )( 1 )(A) of the Act. While the 
Petitioner need not establish that there is no one more accomplished to qualify for the classification 
sought, the record is insufficient to demonstrate that he is among the small percentage at the very top 
of his field. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2).3 
III. CONCLUSION 
For the reasons discussed above, the Petitioner has not demonstrated eligibility as an individual of 
extraordinary ability.
