Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a business consultant, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S .C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
SCOPS denied the petition in August 2023, concluding that the record did not establish that the 
Petitioner had either received a major, internationally recognized award or satisfied at least three of 
ten initial evidentiary criteria, as required. The Petitioner filed a motion to reconsider, which SCOPS 
granted. SCOPS again denied the petition in March 2024. We dismissed a subsequent appeal in 
January 2025. The matter is now before us on motion to reconsider. 
The Petitioner bears the burden of proof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the motion. 
A motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision was based on an incorrect application oflaw 
or policy and that the decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the record of proceedings at the 
time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). Because the scope of a motion is limited to the prior 
decision, we will only review the latest decision in these proceedings. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(l)(i), (ii). We 
may grant motions that satisfy these requirements and demonstrate eligibility for the requested benefit. 
In denying the petition in March 2024, SCOPS determined that the Petitioner had not satisfied any of 
six claimed criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). The Petitioner disputed this conclusion regarding five 
of those criteria. In our appellate decision, we addressed three of the criteria in detail, concluding that 
the Petitioner had not satisfied the criteria relating to published material about the alien; original 
contributions of major significance; and authorship of scholarly articles. The Petitioner contests all 
these conclusions on motion.

necessary translation.

The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi) calls for evidence of the alien's authorship of scholarly 
articles in the field, in professional or major trade publications or other major media. 
As defined in the academic arena, a scholarly article reports on original research, experimentation, or 
philosophical discourse. In general, it should have footnotes, endnotes, or a bibliography, and may 
include graphs, charts, videos, or pictures as illustrations of the concepts expressed in the article. See 
generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
For other fields, a scholarly article should be written for learned persons in that field. Learned persons 
include all persons having profound knowledge of a field. Id. 
In evaluating whether a submitted publication is a professional publication or major media, relevant 
factors include the intended audience ( for professional journals) and the circulation or readership relative 
to other media in the field (for major media). Id.

On motion, the Petitioner states: "It appears that AAO used the evaluation standard for scholarly articles 
to determine whether the publication qualifies as a professional publication. This approach deviates from 
USCIS's Policy Manual." The Petitioner asserts: 
It is pertinent to mention that "for learned persons in that field" is the standard used to

On appeal, the Petitioner had relied on the distinction between "professional ... publication" and "major 
media" to assert that "circulation and readership evidence is not relevant in this matter." But readership 
statistics are less important in "professional publications" precisely because such publications are geared 
toward a narrower, smaller audience. See Krasniqi v. Dibbins, 558 F. Supp. 3d 168, 186 (D.N.J. 2021) 
(citing Braga v. Poulos, No. CV 06-5105 SJO FMOX, 2007 WL 9229758, at *6 (C.D. Cal. July 6, 2007), 
aff'd, 31 7 F. App 'x 680 (9th Cir. 2009) ). 
A publication that is not specifically aimed at a particular industry, trade, profession, or field necessarily 
has a larger intended audience. Therefore, the Petitioner cannot avoid the requirement for circulation data 
by asserting that her article appeared in a "professional publication." Likewise, the specialized nature of 
her particular article does not imply that the article must have appeared in a professional or trade 
publication, because the regulation indicates that a scholarly article may appear "in professional or major 
trade publications or other major media." It therefore follows that not every scholarly article appears in 
a professional publication, and not every publication that carries scholarly articles is a qualifying 
publication under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi).

The Petitioner's arguments regarding her published article do not show proper cause for reconsideration. 
On motion, the Petitioner observes that we reserved discussion on two claimed criteria, relating to 
membership in associations at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii) and performance in a leading or critical role for 
organizations or establishments with a distinguished reputation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii). The 
Petitioner asks that we address these criteria on motion. 
As we explained in our appellate decision, we reserved these two criteria because, without at least one 
other granted criterion, discussion of these two criteria could not change the outcome of the appeal 
because 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) requires a petitioner to meet at least three ofthe criteria. The Petitioner 
discusses the two reserved criteria at some length on motion, but because the Petitioner has not 
established that we erred with regard to our conclusions concerning published material, scholarly 
articles, and original contributions, the Petitioner likewise has not shown that we erred in reserving 
the remaining two criteria. 
On motion to reconsider, the Petitioner has not established that our previous decision was based on an 
incorrect application of law or policy at the time we issued our decision. Therefore, we will dismiss 
the motion. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
ORDER: The motion to reconsider is dismissed.
