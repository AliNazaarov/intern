Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Exceptional Ability) 
The Petitioner, a postdoctoral research associate, seeks first preference immigrant classification as an 
individual of extraordinary ability. See section 203(b )(1 )(A) of the Immigration and Nationality Act 
(the Act), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant visas 
available to those who can show their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international 
acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the evidence did not satisfy 
at least three of the ten regulatory criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). This matter is before us on 
appeal, which we review de novo. Matter ofChristo 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). 
The Petitioner must establish her eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. Matter ofChawathe, 
25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon de novo review, we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act makes visas available to immigrants with extraordinary ability if: 
(i) the alien has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has 
been demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been 
recognized in the field through extensive documentation; (ii) the alien seeks to enter this country to 
continue work in the area of extraordinary ability; and (iii) the alien's entry into the United States will 
substantially benefit prospectively the United States. 
"Extraordinary ability" refers only to those in "that small percentage who have risen to the very top of 
the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth two 
options for satisfying this classification's initial evidence requirements: first, an alien can show 
sustained acclaim and the recognition of achievements in the field through a one-time achievement 
(that is, a major, internationally recognized award); or in the alternative, the alien may submit 
qualifying documentation that satisfies at least three of the ten criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) 
(including items such as awards, published material in certain media, and scholarly articles). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial regulatory evidence requirements, we then consider the totality 
of the material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCJS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) 
( discussing a two-part review where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the 
required number of criteria, considered in the context of a final merits determination).

Considering the foregoing, we acknowledge that the Petitioner has been a productive and dedicated 
researcher in the field, and that her published findings and data have been cited and relied upon by 
others in their own research. But the record does not show that her contributions of molecular biology 
research were of major significance in the field. The Petitioner thus has not met her burden of 
satisfying at least three of the threshold evidentiary criteria set forth in 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v). 
Congress set a very high benchmark for aliens of extraordinary ability by requiring them to establish 
sustained national or international acclaim and present extensive documentation to demonstrate their 
achievements have been recognized in the field. Section 203(b )(1 )(A)(i) of the Act. The Petitioner's 
inability to satisfy the above regulatory criterion means she has not met the initial evidentiary threshold 
as regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) requires. Thus, we cannot conclude that she is an alien of 
extraordinary ability and need not farther analyze the merits under the Act. See Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 
1119-1120; see also Nagaiah v. Allen, F.Supp.3d, 2025 WL 1508028, at *10 (E.D. Pa. May 27, 2025)
