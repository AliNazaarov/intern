Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a hairstylist, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See Immigration 
and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). This first preference 
classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their extraordinary ability 
through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in 
their field through extensive documentation. 
USCIS denied the petition, concluding that the record did not establish that the Petitioner had satisfied 
at least three of the initial evidentiary criteria for this classification, as required. The matter is now 
before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F .R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will withdraw the decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with the 
following analysis. 
USCIS determined the Petitioner satisfied the criteria at 8 C.F.R § 204.5(h)(3)(iii) and (iv), but that 
she did not satisfy the other claimed criteria. On appeal, the Petitioner asserts that USCIS' assessment 
of the evidence was largely based on errors of law and false assumptions of fact. 1 Upon review, we 
agree that USCIS' decision contains errors concerning certain eligibility requirements and the 
relevance of the evidence submitted. For example, USCIS determined that the Petitioner did not meet 
the awards criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i). Although the decision lists evidence in the record 
concerning her awards, USCIS did not adequately analyze the evidence. Instead, USCIS stated that 
while the Petitioner demonstrated that she was the recipient of several awards in her field, she "did 
not include major trade publications or other major media" and that there is "no evidence of 
contemporaneous publicity surrounding [the] awards or evidence showing they command a substantial

Further, we note that USCIS' decision includes language identical to its request for evidence (RFE). 
Specifically, the decision states "in short, we request that you submit major media ..." to establish 
eligibility, which is copied verbatim from the RFE. 
Since the decision letter contains language identical to the RFE and does not provide analysis of the 
evidence, it is not clear whether USCIS reviewed all of the documentation in the record. An officer 
must fully explain the reasons for denying a visa petition. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.3(a)(l)(i). This 
explanation should be sufficient to allow the Petitioner a fair opportunity to contest the decision and 
to allow us an opportunity for meaningful appellate review. See, e.g., Matter ofM-P-, 20 I&N Dec. 
786 (BIA 1994) (finding that a decision must fully explain the reasons for denying a motion to allow 
the respondent a meaningful opportunity to challenge the determination on appeal). 
In addition, USCIS' determination that the Petitioner did not meet the criterion at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(vii) is based on an erroneous interpretation ofthe criterion. The decision states that "this 
criterion pertains to the work of artists, not that of hairstylists ...." Conversely, neither the plain 
language of the regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii) nor the USCIS Policy Manual preclude 
hairstylists from satisfying this criterion. The regulation requires "[ e ]vidence of the display of the 
alien's work in the field at artistic exhibitions or showcases" and the Policy Manual requires that the 
exhibitions or showcases be artistic in nature. 5 Further, the decision does not provide an analysis of 
the evidence submitted to demonstrate the Petitioner's eligibility under this criterion. Instead, USCIS 
erroneously stated that the Petitioner had requested that the evidence be considered comparable 
evidence under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4) when she did not make such a request. 
Finally, in determining whether the Petitioner met the requirements of 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii), 
USCIS improperly limited what evidence may be considered to demonstrate eligibility. The record 
includes a letter from the president of theI Iexplaining that the 
Petitioner currently serves as president of the organization's branch in Armenia and that she is a judge 
for one of the organization's prominent competitions. USCIS stated that "this evidence has already 
been considered" under another criterion, and that "the regulatory criteria are separate and distinct 
from one another and thus are not interchangeable." USCIS appears to state that evidence cannot be 
used to demonstrate eligibility under more than one criterion-in this case, both the criteria for serving 
in a leading or critical role and for judging the work of others in one's field.

Because the decision lacks analysis and contains several substantive errors, we will remand the matter. 
We note that, while USCIS determined that the Petitioner met the criterion concerning published 
material at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii), the record does not include documentation demonstrating that 
the publications are professional or major trade publications or other major media. USCIS should 
review the evidence pertaining to all ofthe claimed criteria in accordance with relevant law, regulation, 
and USCIS policy. 
III. CONCLUSION 
We withdraw USCTS' decision and remand this matter for further consideration and entry of a new 
decision. On remand, USCIS should review the entire record and determine whether the Petitioner 
meets at least two of the remaining claimed criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i), (iv), (vii), and (viii), 
in addition to 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii). Tf USCTS concludes that the Petitioner's evidence does not 
meet a specific eligibility requirement, the decision should discuss the insufficiencies in the evidence 
and adequately explain the reasons for ineligibility. If the Petitioner meets at least three criteria, 
USCTS should provide a final merits analysis to determine whether she meets the requirements for 
classification as an alien ofextraordinary ability. In remanding, we express no opinion as to the ultimate 
resolution of this case. 
ORDER: The decision is withdrawn. The matter is remanded for the entry of a new decision 
consistent with the foregoing analysis.
