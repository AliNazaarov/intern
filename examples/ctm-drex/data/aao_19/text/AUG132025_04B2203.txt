Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an estate surveyor and valuer, seeks immigrant classification as an individual of 
extraordinary ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S .C. 
§ 1153(b)(l)(A). 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the record did not establish 
that the Petitioner met at least three of the initial evidentiary criteria, as required. The matter is now 
before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter oJChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christa's, Inc. , 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(1 )(A) of the Act makes immigrant visas available to individuals with extraordinary 
ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained 
national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, provided that the individual seeks to enter the United States to continue 
work in the area of extraordinary ability, and the individual's entry into the United States will 
substantially benefit prospectively the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, petitioners can demonstrate 
international recognition of their accomplishments in the field through a one-time achievement (that 
is, a major, internationally recognized award). If petitioners do not submit this evidence, then they 
must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the ten criteria listed at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4) allows petitioners to submit 
comparable evidence if they are able to demonstrate that the standards at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)­
(x) do not readily apply to their occupation. 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) 
( discussing a two-part review where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the 
required number of criteria, considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also 
Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 
(W.D. Wash. 2011). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The record shows the Petitioner earned a doctorate in estate management and has worked as an estate 
surveyor and valuer in Nigeria for over 20 years. The Petitioner claimed to meet six of the initial 
evidentiary criteria relating to lesser awards, membership, published material, judging, original 
contributions, and authorship at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(vi). SCOPS determined the Petitioner met 
the criteria for published material and authorship, but did not meet the remaining criteria he claimed 
to satisfy. On appeal, the Petitioner asserts SCOPS erred in determining he did not meet additional 
criteria and claims he meets the criteria for lesser awards and judging. The Petitioner does not reassert 
that he meets the criteria for membership and original contributions on appeal.

B. Final Merits Determination 
The Petitioner has established that he meets the evidentiary criteria relating published material, 
judging, and authorship at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii), (iv), and (vi). As the Petitioner submitted the 
requisite initial evidence, we will evaluate whether he has demonstrated, by a preponderance of the 
evidence, his sustained national or international acclaim, that he is among the small percentage at the 
very top of his field of endeavor, and that his achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation. In a final merits determination, we analyze petitioners' accomplishments 
and weigh the totality of the evidence to determine if their successes are sufficient to demonstrate that 
they have extraordinary ability in the field of endeavor. See section 203(b)(l)(A)(i) of the Act; 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2), (3); see also Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20.

When considered in the aggregate, the evidence shows the Petitioner is a highly skilled and 
accomplished estate surveyor and valuer who has won awards, engaged in teaching and research, held 
significant positions with several organizations in his field, and has been quoted in news articles. The 
record does not, however, demonstrate that the Petitioner achieved sustained national or international 
acclaim as an estate surveyor and valuer among the small percentage at the very top of his field. The 
record does not indicate that the awards the Petitioner has won, his academic and organizational 
appointments, and his publication and citation record are comparable to those at the very top of his 
field. 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner seeks a highly restrictive visa classification, intended for individuals already at the top 
of their respective fields, rather than for individuals progressing towards the top. U.S. Citizenship and 
Immigration Services has long held that even athletes performing at the major league level do not 
automatically meet the "extraordinary ability" standard. Matter of Price, 20 I&N Dec. 953, 954 
(Assoc. Comm'r. 1994). The Petitioner has not shown that the significance and recognition of his 
work are indicative of sustained national or international acclaim as one of that small percentage who 
have risen to the very top of his field, as extraordinary ability is described at section 203(b )(1 )(A) of 
the Act and 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The Petitioner is consequently ineligible for classification as an 
alien of extraordinary ability. 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
