Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a chief technology officer, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. 
See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). This 
first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the record did not establish 
that the Petitioner met the statutory and regulatory requirements for the requested classification. The 
Petitioner appealed and we remanded the matter to SCOPS, who then denied the petition a second 
time. We dismissed a subsequent appeal. The matter is now before us on a combined motion to reopen 
and motion to reconsider. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the 
combined motion. 
I. MOTION TO REOPEN 
We incorporate by reference our analysis in our prior decision. By way of summation, and in relevant 
part, we determined that the record satisfies two of the 10 criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x), of 
which at least three must be met if, as in this case, a petitioner does not have a one-time achievement 
of a major, internationally recognized award. More specifically, we concluded that the record satisfies 
the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(iv) and (vi), but it does not satisfy the criteria at 8 C.F.R. 
§§ 204.5(h)(3)(i), (iii), (v), (viii)-(ix). 
A motion to reopen must state new facts and be supported by documentary evidence. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103 .5(a)(2). Because the scope of a motion is limited to the prior decision, we will only review the 
latest decision in these proceedings. 8 C.F.R. §§ 103.5(a)(l)(i)-(ii). We may grant motions that satisfy 
these requirements and demonstrate eligibility for the requested benefit. See Matter ofCoelho, 20 I&N 
Dec. 464,473 (BIA 1992) (requiring that new evidence have the potential to change the outcome). 
On motion, the Petitioner submits evidence and he asserts that, in addition to satisfying the criteria at 
8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(iv) and (vi), the record satisfies the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(v), 
(viii)-(ix). He does not specifically assert on motion-and the record does not support the 
conclusion-that he meets the criteria at 8 C.F.R. §§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(iii), (vii), or (x). 
To address the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v), the Petitioner submits letters from various 
individuals, copies of research papers he co-authored, and copyright information. Similar to the 
deficiencies in the letters that we addressed in our prior decision, the letters the Petitioner submits on 
motion also do not provide details necessary to establish whether his published research or work in 
robotics, healthcare technology, or any other field have been widely adopted by the respective field(s) 
or that his research or work otherwise has been cited, referenced, or modeled indicative of being an 
original contribution of major significance. 
The copies ofthe research papers the Petitioner submits on motion are either already in the record and, 
thus, not documentary evidence of new facts, see 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2), or dated December 2021 and 
throughout 2023, after the Petitioner filed the Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers, in 
November 2021. A petitioner must establish eligibility for the benefit it is seeking at the time the 
petition is filed. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l). A visa petition may not be approved based on speculation 
of future eligibility or after a petitioner becomes eligible under a new set of facts. See Matter of 
Katigbak, 14 I&N Dec. 45, 49 (Reg'l Comm'r 1971). A petitioner may not make material changes to 
a petition in an effort to make a deficient petition conform to USCIS requirements. See Matter of 
Izummi, 22 I&N Dec. 169, 176 (Assoc. Comm'r 1998). Therefore, the research papers the Petitioner 
co-authored dated after he filed the Form 1-140 in November 2021 cannot-and do not-establish 
eligibility. Even ifthe research papers presented new facts that could establish eligibility, they indicate 
that the Petitioner has made original contributions, but they do not establish whether his contributions 
have had major significance in any particular field, as required by the criterion at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(v). Similarly, the copyright information the Petitioner submits on motion is dated 
September 2022, after he filed the Form 1-140 in November 2021. As we explained in our prior 
decision, copyright information dated 2022, after the Petitioner submitted the Form 1-140 cannot­
and it does not-establish eligibility. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l); Matter ofKatigbak, 14 I&N Dec. at 
49; Matter ofIzwnmi, 22 I&N Dec. at 176. 
To address the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii), the Petitioner submits a one-page excerpt from 
a SCOPS decision that states in relevant part simply, "The [P]etitioner submitted sufficient evidence 
to meet this criterion," without further elaboration on what evidence SCOPS believes satisfies the 
criterion. We first note that this one-page excerpt from a SCOPS decision is not new to the record 
and, thus, is outside the scope of a motion to reopen. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(2) (requiring a motion 
to reopen to state new facts). Moreover, even if the one-page excerpt from a SCOPS decision 
presented a fact that is new to the record, we are not bound by SCOPS' decisions. See, e.g., La. 
Philharmonic Orchestra v. INS, 248 F.3d 1139 (5th Cir. 2001) (per curiam). We addressed the reasons 
why the record does not satisfy this criterion in our prior decision and, even if the one-page excerpt 
from a non-binding SCOPS decision presented a new fact as required for a motion to reopen, SCOPS' 
conclusory statement-without identifying any particular item of evidence or explaining how it 
satisfies the criterion-does not overcome our prior analysis concluding that the record does not satisfy 
the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii).

Because the Petitioner does not establish eligibility through a new fact, the submission does not satisfy

II. MOTION TO RECONSIDER 
A motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision was based on an incorrect application of 
law or policy and that the decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the record of proceedings 
at the time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(3). Because the scope of a motion is limited to the 
prior decision, we will only review the latest decision in these proceedings. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(l)(i), 
3 
(ii). We may grant motions that satisfy these requirements and demonstrate eligibility for the requested 
benefit. 
On motion, the Petitioner contests the correctness of our prior decision; however, he does not identify 
a law or policy we may have misapplied. Because the Petitioner does not identify a law or policy we 
may have misapplied, the submission does not satisfy the requirements of a motion to reconsider; 
therefore, we will dismiss it. See 8 C.F.R. §§ 103.5(a)(3)-(4). 
ORDER: The motion to reopen is dismissed. 
FURTHER ORDER: The motion to reconsider is dismissed.
