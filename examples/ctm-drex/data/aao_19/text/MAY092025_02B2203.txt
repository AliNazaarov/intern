Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Skilled Worker) 
The Petitioner, a healthcare company, seeks to employ the Beneficiary as a registered nurse. The 
company requests her classification under the employment-based, third-preference (EB-3) immigrant 
visa category as a "skilled worker." See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 
203(b )(3)(A)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b )(3)(A)(i). Businesses may sponsor aliens for U.S. permanent 
residence in this category to work in jobs requiring at least two years of training or experience. Id. 1 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not 
demonstrate its required ability to pay the combined proffered wages of this and other immigrant visa 
petitions it filed. On appeal, the company submits a copy of its 2023 federal income tax return. 
The Petitioner bears the burden of demonstrating eligibility for the requested benefit by a 
preponderance of the evidence. Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). 
Exercising de novo appellate review, see Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 l&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 
2015), we conclude that the record lacks regulatorily required evidence of the company's ability to 
pay the proffered wage in 2024, the year of the petition's priority date. As evidence of the Petitioner's 
ability to pay in 2024 should become available soon, we will withdraw SCOPS' decision and remand 
the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with the following analysis. 
I. LAW 
Immigration as a skilled worker usually follows a three-step process. First, to permanently fill a job 
with a foreign worker, a prospective U.S. employer must obtain U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) 
certification. Section 212(a)(5)(A)(i) of the Act, 8 U.S.C. § l 182(a)(5)(A)(i). If DOL certifies a job, 
an employer must next submit the labor certification with an immigrant visa petition to U.S. 
Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS). Section 204(a)(l)(F) of the Act, 8 U.S.C. 
§ l 154(a)(l)(F). Finally, ifUSCIS grants the petition, a noncitizen beneficiary may apply abroad for 
an immigrant visa or, if eligible, "adjustment of status" in the United States. See section 245 of the 
Act, 8 U.S.C. § 1255.

DOL, however, has already determined that the United States lacks sufficient professional nurses and 
that permanent employment of aliens in these "Schedule A" jobs will not harm the wages or working

Therefore, in this matter, users not only adjudicates the petition but also its accompanying Schedule 
A labor certification application. See 20 e.F.R. § 656.15( e) ("The Schedule A determination of DRS 
[Department of Homeland Security] is conclusive and final."] 
II. ANALYSIS 
A petitioner must demonstrate its continuing ability to pay an offered job's proffered wage, from a 
petition's priority date until a beneficiary obtains U.S. permanent residence. 8 e.F.R. § 204.5(g)(2). 
Ability-to-pay evidence must generally include copies of a petitioner's annual reports, federal tax 
returns, or audited financial statements for relevant years. Id. 
When determining ability to pay, users examines whether a petitioner paid a beneficiary the foll 
proffered wage each year beginning with the year of a petition's priority date. See generally 6 USCIS 
Policy Manual E.4(e)( l ), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. If a petitioner did not annually pay the full 
wage or did not pay a beneficiary at all, users considers whether the business's annual amounts of 
net income and net current assets covered the differences between the proffered wage and any wages 
paid. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual E.4(e)(2). If net income and net current assets are 
insufficient, users may consider other factors potentially affecting a petitioner's ability to pay a 
proffered wage. See Matter of Sonegawa, 12 I&N Dec. 612, 614-15 (Reg'l eomm'r 1967); see 
generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual E.4(e)(3) ("The ability to pay analysis is more nuanced than simply 
reviewing wages paid, net income, and net current assets.")2 
The Petitioner's Schedule A labor certification application states the proffered wage ofthe offered job 
of registered nurse as $73,840 a year. The petition's priority date is September 16, 2024, its filing 
date. See 8 e.F.R. § 204.5(d) (explaining how to determine a petition's priority date). 
The petition indicates that the Beneficiary resides abroad and has not worked for the Petitioner. Thus, 
based solely on wages paid, the company did not demonstrate its ability to pay the proffered wage. 
At the time ofthe petition's filing in September 2024, regulatorily required evidence ofthe Petitioner's 
ability to pay the proffered wage in 2024 was not yet available. The company's initial ability-to-pay 
evidence consisted of a copy of its compiled financial statement for 2023. See generally 6 USCIS 
Policy Manual E.4(A) n.5 ("In the event that a tax return, annual report, or audited financial statement 
is not available for the priority date year at the time of filing, users may consider one of these three 
documents for the year before the priority date as part of the totality analysis.")

Later in September 2024, SCOPS issued a request for additional evidence (RFE) explaining that the 
Petitioner's compiled financial statements did not meet the regulatory requirement of "audited 
financial statements." See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(g)(2) (emphasis added). The RFE requested regulatorily 
required evidence of the company's ability to pay the proffered wage, as well as information and 
documentation regarding approved or pending Forms I-140, Immigrant Petitions for Alien Workers, 
that the Petitioner had filed for other beneficiaries. Petitioners for multiple beneficiaries who received 
less than the proffered wages from the businesses must demonstrate their abilities to pay all their 
beneficiaries' combined proffered wages each year, starting from the priority dates of the petitions 
under review. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual E.4(D)(2). 3 
The Petitioner's November 2024 RFE response included a copy of part of its 2023 federal income tax 
return and information about 61 I-140 petitions the company filed for other beneficiaries in 2023 and 
2024. Because of the incomplete tax return, SCOPS concluded that it could not determine the 
company's ability to pay the combined proffered wages. See generalZv 6 USCIS Policy Manual 
E.4(A)(2) ("Submitted tax returns should be complete, including all required schedules.") 
On appeal, the Petitioner submits a complete copy of its 2023 federal income tax return. The return 
shows the company's generation of net income that year of more than $1.1 million. 
By regulation, however, a petitioner must demonstrate its ability to pay "at the time the priority date 
is established." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(g)(2). Upon timely request, the U.S. Internal Revenue Service (IRS) 
automatically extends income tax filing deadlines for up to six months after the original April 15 due 
date. IRS, "Taxpayers who need more time to file a federal tax return should request an extension," 
www.irs.gov/newsroom. Thus, a copy of the Petitioner's 2024 federal income tax should be available 
by October 15, 2025. We will therefore remand the matter for the gathering of additional evidence of 
the company's ability to pay the proffered wage in 2024, the year of the petition's priority date. 
On remand, SCOPS should ask the Petitioner to submit regulatorily required evidence of its ability to 
pay the offered job's proffered wage in 2024 and afford the company a reasonable opportunity to 
respond. The Petitioner may submit additional evidence of its ability to pay, including materials 
supporting other factors potentially affecting the company's ability to pay. See Matter ofSonegawa, 
12 I&N Dec. at 614-15. 
Also, the record casts doubt on whether the Petitioner would truly employ the Beneficiary in the 
offered job. In its RFE response and again on appeal, the company describes "the actual place of [the] 
Beneficiary's employment" as "an entity affiliated with [the] Petitioner." This statement suggests that 
a different entity than the Petitioner would employ the Beneficiary. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(g)(2) 
(requiring a demonstration of the ability to pay a proffered wage by "the prospective United 
States employer"); see also Sitar Rest. v. Ashcroft, No. CIV.A.02-30197-MAP, 2003 WL 22203713, 
at *2 (D. Mass. Sept. 18, 2003) ("[N]othing in the governing regulation, 8 C.F.R. § 204.5, permits the

III. CONCLUSION 
The record lacks regulatorily required evidence of the Petitioner's ability to pay the proffered wage in 
2024, the year of the petition's priority date. 
ORDER: The decision is withdrawn. The matter is remanded for entry of a new decision 
consistent with the foregoing analysis.
