Appeal of U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) Decision 
Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a scientist in the field of materials chemistry, seeks first preference immigrant 
classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) 
section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant 
visas available to those who can demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or 
international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive 
documentation. 
USCIS denied the petition, concluding that the petitioner has not shown that he is eligible to file Form 
I-140 under section 203(b)(l)(B) of the Act, which applies to a petitioner seeking to classify a 
beneficiary as an outstanding professor or researcher, had been satisfied. Namely, the denial states 
the following, in relevant part: 
[I]t appears that the beneficiary bas self-petitioned. . . . The beneficiary signed the 
Form I-140 in Part 8 as petitioner. Additionally, the Form G-28 shows that the Attorney 
is representing only the beneficiary. EB-1-2 cases require that the sponsoring institution 
file a petition on behalf of the beneficiary; he or she cannot self-petition. 
8 C.F.R. Section 204.5(i)(l) states that any United States employer desiring and 
intending to employ a professor or researcher who is outstanding in an academic field 
under section 203(b)(l)(B) may file an I-140 visa petition for such classification. 
The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance ofthe evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter ofChristo 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). 
Upon de novo review, we conclude that USCIS did not apply the correct statute and regulations in the 
matter at hand. As stated above, the Petitioner seeks immigrant classification as an individual of 
extraordinary ability under section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act. USCIS, however, considered the petition 
under section 203(b )(1 )(B) of the Act, which applies to the immigrant classification of an outstanding 
professor or researcher. Because USCIS did not properly consider the pet1t10n and supporting 
evidence under the statute and regulatory provisions that apply to the requested classification, we will 
withdraw the decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision that is consistent with the 
benefit request indicated in the Petitioner's Form I-140. 
ORDER: The decision is withdrawn. The matter is remanded for the entry of a new decision 
consistent with the foregoing analysis.
