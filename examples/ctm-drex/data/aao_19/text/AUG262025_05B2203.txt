Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a molecular biologist, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S .C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
SCOPS denied the petition, concluding that the record did not establish that the Petitioner qualifies as 
an individual of extraordinary ability either as the recipient of a one-time achievement that is a major, 
internationally recognized award, or as someone who initially satisfied at least three ofthe ten required 
regulatory criteria listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) - (x). The matter is now before us on appeal 
pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103 .3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christa 's, Inc. , 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act makes visas available to immigrants with extraordinary ability if: 
(i) the alien has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or 
athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained national or international 
acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, 
(ii) the alien seeks to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability, and 
(iii) the alien's entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively 
the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate sustained 
acclaim and the recognition of achievements in the field through a one-time achievement (that is, a 
major, internationally recognized award) or qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the 
ten categories listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) - (x) (including items such as awards, published 
material in certain media, and scholarly articles). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) 
( discussing a two-part review where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the 
required number of criteria, considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also 
Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 
(W.D. Wash. 2011). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner is a molecular biologist working in related areas of computational biology. Because the 
Petitioner has not indicated or established that he has received a major, internationally recognized 
award, he must show that he satisfies at least three of the ten regulatory criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). The Petitioner submitted evidence related to the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 
204.5(h)(3)(iv), (v), and (vi) and SCOPS concluded that he satisfied one of the criteria. Specifically, 
that the Petitioner met his burden to demonstrate his authorship of scholarly articles under 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(vi). However, SCOPS determined that the Petitioner did not establish his service as a 
judge of the work of others or the criteria for original contributions of major significance under 8 
C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv)-(v). 
In response to a request for evidence (RFE), the Petitioner submitted emails and communications 
demonstrating that he has judged the work of others in his field by peer reviewing manuscripts for 
professional journals and articles. The material he reviewed appeared in journals including 
Epigenetics & Chromatin, Biomedicines, and Genes. Because the record indicates the Petitioner has 
judged the work of others, he satisfied the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv).

in the adjudication of the petition and asserts that SCOPS abused its discretion and claims the 
Petitioner meets the plain language requirements of the criteria. Specifically, the Petitioner maintains 
that SCOPS failed to thoroughly analyze the evidence presented and resorted to boilerplate assertions 
in its review of the evidence. However, SCOPS' determination did discuss the letters of support, the 
citation record, the patent information where the Petitioner's work was referenced, the Petitioner's 
research, and his work in the development of the I I and I I In reviewing SCOPS' 
decision, we do not identify any indication that the standard was raised beyond the preponderance of 
the evidence by demanding more evidence than the regulation calls for.

For the above-stated reasons, of the three claimed criteria, the Petitioner has demonstrated that he 
meets two of the criteria. He has not shown that he satisfies the criterion related to original 
contributions of major significance at 8 C.F.R. 204.5(h)(3)(v). Accordingly, we conclude that the 
Petitioner has not met three of the initial evidentiary criteria, as required. 
III. CONCLUSION 
Congress set a very high benchmark for aliens of extraordinary ability by requiring the petitioner 
demonstrate "sustained national or international acclaim" and present "extensive documentation" to 
show his or her achievements have been recognized in the field. Section 203(b)(1 )(A)(i) of the 
Act. Here, because the Petitioner has not satisfied the threshold "initial evidence" requirements at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), we cannot conclude that they are an alien of extraordinary ability and need not 
further analyze the merits under the statute. See Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20; see also Nagaiah v. 
Allen, -- F.Supp.3d ---, 2025 WL 1508028, at *10 (E.D. Pa. May 27, 2025) (finding the regulation 
"establishes a necessary, not sufficient, evidentiary requirement for a petitioner who seeks an 
extraordinary ability visa."). Therefore, we decline to reach and hereby reserve any final merits 
determination in this matter. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (per curiam) (holding
