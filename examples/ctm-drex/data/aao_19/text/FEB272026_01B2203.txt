Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a site reliability engineer, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. 
See section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act), 8 U.S.C. § l 153(b)(l)(A). 
This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens who can 
demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose 
achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding the Petitioner was not the 
recipient of a major, internationally recognized award, and, did not satisfy at least three of the ten 
evidentiary criteria to meet the initial evidentiary requirements of the classification. The matter is now 
before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, In c., 26 l&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act makes immigrant visas available to aliens with extraordinary ability if: 
they have extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been 
demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim; they seek to enter the United States to 
continue work in the area of extraordinary ability; and their entry will substantially benefit the United 
States prospectively. The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small 
percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204. 5(h)(2). 
The implementing regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204. 5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis of the evidence 
to determine whether a petitioner has sustained national or international acclaim and whether their 
achievements have been recognized in the field. First, a petitioner can provide evidence of either "a 
major, international recognized award," or satisfaction of at least three of ten evidentiary criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration 
Services (USCIS) then considers the totality of the material provided in a final merits determination 
and assesses whether the record shows sustained national or international acclaim and demonstrates 
that the individual is among the small percentage at the very top ofthe field of endeavor. See Kazarian 
v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1121 (9th Cir. 2010) (discussing a two-step review where the documentation 
is frrst counted and then, if fulfilling the required number of criteria, considered in the context of a 
final merits determination); see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); 
Rijal v. USCJS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011); see generally 6 USCJS Policy Manual 
F.2(B), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual (providing, as guidance, USCIS' two-step extraordinary ability 
analysis). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner has not indicated or established that he has received a major, internationally recognized 
award under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). He must therefore satisfy at least three of the regulatory criteria 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). Based on our de novo review, the Petitioner has not met this showing. 
A. Evidentiary Criteria Waived 
The Petitioner did not assert in the record below or on appeal that he meets the criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i), (vi), (vii), or (x) regarding receipt of lesser nationally or internationally 
recognized prizes or awards, authorship of scholarly articles, display ofhis work at artistic exhibitions 
or showcases, and commercial success in the performing arts. We therefore deem the Petitioner's 
eligibility under these four criteria waived. See Matter of O-R-E-, 28 I&N Dec. 330, 336 n.5 (BIA 
2021) (citing Matter ofR-A-M-, 25 I&N Dec. 657, 658 n.2 (BIA 2012))(An eligibility ground that is 
not raised on appeal is waived.)

C. Remaining Evidentiary Criteria 
1. Criteria Not Established 
SCOPS determined the Petitioner did not establish the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii), which 
requires he provide documentation of his membership in associations in the field for which 
classification is sought, which require outstanding achievements of their members, as judged by 
recognized national or international experts in their disciplines or fields. In his initial filing, the 
Petitioner claimed that his membership in the International Association of Engineers (IAENG) met 
this criterion, but in response to SCOPS request for evidence, he did not pursue this argument, rather 
he claimed membership in a different organization, Sigma XI, the Scientific Research Honor Society. 
However, he does not state when he became a member of Sigma XI and his supporting documents 
evidencing his membership do not clarify when he became a member. The Petitioner filed his petition 
in April 2025. It is his burden to establish that this membership in Sigma XI was in existence at the 
time of filing, which he has not done. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l) (An applicant or petitioner must

Further, we agree with SCOPS' determination that the Petitioner has not established that either of 
these associations require outstanding achievements of its members, and that membership eligibility 
is judged by recognized national or international experts in their field. The Petitioner submitted a 
January 2025 letter evidencing he is a member ofIAENG. With respect to membership requirements, 
the Petitioner provided printouts from IAENG's website, which state "IAENG welcome all the 
engineering professions to join us" and, to the extent there are additional requirements for 
membership, the Petitioner did not provide such information. As a result, the Petitioner did not 
establish IAENG requires outstanding achievement of its members as judged by recognized national 
and international experts in his field. 
The Petitioner also submitted a certificate indicating that he is an "associate member" of Sigma XI 
and its bylaws defines an associate member as: 
Any individual who has through initial research achievement in a field of pure or applied 
science shown aptitude for research which is expected in due course to lead to the fulfillment 
of the requirements for full membership and any individual who is a bench scientist or 
technician who "does" the research but may never be recognized as an author or cannot be an 
author but holds full time job in science research, is eligible for election to associate 
membership in the Society. 
( emphasis added). The bylaws further state, "any individual who has shown noteworthy achievement 
as an original investigator in a field of pure or applied science is eligible for election or promotion to 
full membership in the Society." On appeal, the Petitioner argues that he has demonstrated that 
membership in Sigma XI requires noteworthy original investigation or significant research aptitude. 
However, he has not demonstrated he is a member of Sigma XI, but rather an associate member, which 
only required him to show, in relevant part, an aptitude for research or to hold a job in research. 
Further, even ifhe had demonstrated he was a member, he does not explain how requiring "noteworthy 
original investigation or significant research aptitude" amounts to requiring outstanding achievements 
ofits members. Moreover, he also argues on appeal that election for membership requires nomination, 
independent evaluation by scientific peers, and documented evidence of achievement. Here again, 
however, it is important to note that the Petitioner has not demonstrated he is a member or that he went 
through this nomination process. Even if he had, he also has not submitted any documentation 
evidencing that membership eligibility in Sigma XI is judged by recognized national or international 
experts in the field. The Petitioner has therefore not established this criterion. 
SCOPS found the Petitioner had not established the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v), which

statement what his original contributions are or any impact to the field. His recommendation letters 
are from his co-workers and describe him, for example, optimizing operational processes, 
implementing initiatives, deploying new technology, re-designing infrastructure, spearheading design

2. Issues Reserved 
SCOPS determined the Petitioner did not establish he meets the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii), 
requiring evidence of published material about the Petitioner in professional or major trade 
publications or other major media, relating to the Petitioner's work in the field for which classification 
is sought; and 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix), requiring evidence the Petitioner has commanded a high 
salary or other significantly high remuneration for services, in relation to others in the field. As the 
Petitioner would still be unable to establish that he meets at least three of the ten threshold "initial 
evidence" requirements at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), we cannot conclude he is an alien of extraordinary 
ability and reserve our analysis of the remaining two criteria as well as a final merits determination in 
this matter. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) (per curiam) (holding that agencies are 
not required to make "purely advisory findings" on issues that are unnecessary to the ultimate 
decision); see also Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20 ( discussing a two-step review, where if the required 
number of criteria are met by the documentation, then it is considered in the context of a final merits 
determination). 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner has not demonstrated eligibility as an individual of extraordinary ability.
