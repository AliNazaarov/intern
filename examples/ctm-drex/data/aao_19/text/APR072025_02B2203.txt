Appeal of Texas Service Center Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a sports journalist, requests classification under the employment-based, first­
preference (EB-1) immigrant visa category as an alien with "extraordinary ability." See Immigration 
and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b )(1 )(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b )( 1 )(A). Successful petitioners 
for U.S. permanent residence in this category must demonstrate "sustained national or international 
acclaim" and extensively document recognition of their achievements in their fields. Section 
203(b)(1 )(A)(i) of the Act. 
The Director of the Texas Service Center denied the petition. The Director concluded that the 
Petitioner met one of ten initial evidentiary requirements - two less than needed for a final merits 
determination. On appeal, the Petitioner claims that he also submitted evidence of his: 
• Receipt of lesser nationally or internationally recognized awards for excellence in his field; 
• Display of work at artistic exhibitions or showcases; and 
• Performance in a leading or critical role for organizations with distinguished reputations. 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i), (vii), (viii). 
The Petitioner bears the burden of demonstrating eligibility for the requested benefit by a 
preponderance of the evidence. Matter of Chawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). 
Exercising de novo appellate review, see Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 53 7, 53 7 n.2 (AAO 
2015), we conclude that he submitted comparable evidence of the display of his work at artistic 
exhibitions or showcases. But, because his evidence does not meet the minimum number of criteria, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
To qualify for this immigrant visa category, a petitioner must demonstrate that they: 
• Have "extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics;" 
• Seek to continue work in their field of expertise in the United States; and 
• Would substantially benefit the country as a permanent resident. 
Section 203(b)(l)(A)(i)-(iii) of the Act. 
The term "extraordinary ability" means expertise commensurate with "one of that small percentage 
who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). Unless a petitioner 
received "a major, international recognized award," they must satisfy at least three of ten lesser 
evidentiary criteria. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). 1 
If a petitioner meets either evidentiary standard and the requirements at section 203(b )(1 )(A)(ii), (iii) 
of the Act, USCIS must make a final merits determination. To merit approval, the record- as a whole 
- must establish a petitioner's "sustained national or international acclaim" and recognized 
achievements placing them among the small percentage at their field's very top. See Amin v. 
Mayorkas, 24 F.4th 383, 391 (5th Cir. 2022) (finding USCIS' two-step extraordinary ability analysis 
"consistent with the governing statute and regulation"); see generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B), 
www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
TI. ANALYSIS 
A. The Petitioner and His Field 
The record shows that the Petitioner, a Venezuelan native and citizen, earned a bachelor's degree in 
communications from a university in his home country. He then worked for newspapers and television 
networks in Venezuela, covering sporting events around the world. He states his intent to continue 
sports reporting in the United States. 
The Petitioner claims that the Director found insufficient evidence of his intent to continue working 
in his field in the United States or that his U.S. immigration would substantially benefit the country. 
See section 203(b)(1 )(A)(ii), (iii) of the Act. The Director raised these issues in her request for 
additional evidence (RFE). But the decision does not deny the petition on those grounds. We therefore 
need not review the Petitioner's compliance with the statutory requirements. 
B. Evidentiary Criteria 
The record does not indicate - nor does the Petitioner claim - his receipt of a major internationally 
recognized award. He must therefore meet at least three of the ten evidentiary criteria at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(i-x). 
The record supports the Director's finding that the Petitioner submitted evidence of his participation 
as a judge of others' work in the field. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv). 
The Director found that the Petitioner's evidence did not demonstrate his authorship of scholarly 
articles in the field. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi). On appeal, the Petitioner does not challenge this 
finding. He has therefore effectively "waived" that issue. See Matter ofO-R-E-, 28 I&N Dec. 330, 
336 n.5 (BIA 2021) (citing Matter ofR-A-M-, 25 I&N Dec. 657,658 n.2 (BIA 2012)).

We will now review the remaining evidentiary requirements that the Petitioner claims he met. To

1. Display of Work at Artistic Exhibitions or Showcases 
This criterion requires "[e]vidence of the display of the alien's work in the field at artistic exhibitions 
or showcases." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii). 
When adjudicating this requirement, USCIS first determines whether the displayed work is a 
petitioner's product. See generalZv 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l ), Criterion 7. If so, the Agency 
next determines whether the work's display occurred at artistic exhibitions or showcases. Id. The 
word "exhibition" means a public showing (as of works of art, objects of manufacture, or athletic 
skill). Merriam-Webster Online Dictionmy, www.merriam-webster.com. 
The Petitioner submitted evidence that television, radio, and social media have internationally 
broadcast his sports journalism. The Director correctly concluded that this evidence does not meet the 
criterion because the Petitioner's broadcasting work did not appear in artistic exhibitions or showcases. 
See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1122 (9th Cir. 2010) (affirming our decision that self­
publication of a textbook, college lectures, and conference presentations did not constitute displays at 
artistic exhibits or showcases); see generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l ), Criterion 7 (noting 
that the regulation's plain language includes the modifier "artistic," explicitly requiring that 
exhibitions or showcases be artistic in nature). 
The Petitioner alternatively argues that USCIS should consider evidence of his broadcasting work as 
comparable to evidence ofthe display ofwork at artistic exhibitions or showcases. In support, he cites 
a non-precedent decision of ours. See Matter ofM-L-R- (AAO Jan. 25, 2018). 
Petitioners may submit comparable evidence to establish eligibility if evidentiary requirements "do 
not readily apply" to their occupations. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4). "When evaluating such comparable 
evidence, officers must consider whether the regulatory criteria are readily applicable to the person's 
occupation and, if not, whether the evidence provided is truly comparable to the criteria listed in that 
regulation." 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l ), Comparable Evidence. 
In M-L-R-, we found that the petitioner - a musical composer, lyricist, and performer - not only met 
three evidentiary criteria but also submitted evidence comparable to a fourth: display of work at 
artistic exhibitions or showcases. We found that evidence of a favorably reviewed performance of his 
songs at a distinguished venue compared to a visual artistic display. We reasoned that the artistic 
display criterion did not readily apply to a composer because "' [ d]isplay' implies a visual component 
to the exhibition or showcase whereas the Petitioner's compositions are for listening entertainment." 
M-L-R- is not a precedent decision. We therefore need not follow it in this matter. See 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103. l 0(b) (stating that only precedent decisions bind USCIS in other proceedings involving the same 
issue or issues). Also, inM-L-R- we found the artistic display criterion not readily applicable to a song 
composer's non-visual work. Here, the Petitioner's sports journalism broadcasting is partly visual, at 
least when on television and some social media. 
3 
Nevertheless, we find M-L-R- to be analogous and persuasive. The Petitioner persuasively argues that 
the display of work at an artistic exhibition or showcase does not readily apply to his occupation 
because artistic exhibitions and showcases do not generally display journalism. Also, a journalist's 
broadcasts on television, radio, and social media generally are comparable to public displays of work 
at artistic exhibitions or showcases. We therefore accept the Petitioner's broadcast journalism 
evidence to meet this evidentiary requirement. 
The Petitioner submitted evidence comparable to the display of his work at artistic exhibitions or 
showcases. We will therefore withdraw the Director's contrary decision. 
2. Performance in a Leading or Critical Role 
To meet this requirement, a petitioner must submit "[ e ]vidence that the alien has performed in a 
leading or critical role for organizations or establishments that have a distinguished reputation." 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(viii). 
USCIS first determines whether a petitioner has performed in a leading or critical role for an 
organization, establishment, or a division or department of an organization or establishment. See 
generally 6 USCJS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l ), Criterion 8. A leading role means that a petitioner is ( or 
was) a leader within the organization, establishment, division or department. Id. In contrast, a critical 
role means a petitioner "contributed in a way that is of significant importance to the outcome of the 
organization or establishment's activities or those of a division or department of the organization or 
establishment." Id. 
The Agency then determines whether the organization, establishment, department, or division for 
which a petitioner holds or held a leading or critical role has a distinguished reputation. See generally 
6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), Criterion 8. "[D]istinguished" means "marked by eminence, 
distinction, or excellence" or "befitting an eminent person." Merriam-Webster Online Dictionary, 
supra. Relevant factors include not only the relative size or longevity of an organization or 
establishment but also other information such as the scale of its customer base or relevant media 
coverage. See generally 6 USCJS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), Criterion 8. 
The Director found that a letter from an international press association demonstrated the Petitioner's 
performance in a leading or critical role. But the Director found insufficient evidence that the 
association has a distinguished reputation. 
On appeal, the Petitioner claims that the Director disregarded evidence of his selection to a committee 
that annually chooses Venezuela's 'I I' Letters from members of a sports journalist 
association that selects the committee's members state the Petitioner's place on the nine-member panel 
since 2007. He contends that he "holds an esteemed position within the selection committee 
responsible for awarding the highest honor in sports recognition" in Venezuela. He also states that, 
since the association's establishment in 1943, it has had a distinguished reputation. 
Based on the association's history and role in choosing Venezuela's athlete of the year, the Petitioner 
has submitted evidence that the association has a distinguished reputation. But the association letters 
4 
do not demonstrate his purported critical role on the panel. The documents do not explain why his 
role is more important than those of other panel members. 
The Petitioner has not submitted evidence explaining his purported critical role on the committee. We 
will therefore affirm the Director's adverse finding regarding this evidentiary criteria. 
3. Lesser Nationally or Internationally Recognized Awards 
The Petitioner's RFE response included documentation intended to meet this evidentiary requirement. 
But the Director's decision did not address the criterion. We will therefore decide this requirement in 
the first instance. 
This criterion requires "[ d]ocumentation of the alien's receipt of lesser nationally or internationally 
recognized prizes or awards for excellence in the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i). 
USCIS first determines if a petitioner - as opposed to their employer, for example - received prizes 
or awards. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), Criterion 1. If so, the Agency then 
determines whether an award has national or international recognition and honors excellence in the 
applicable field. Id. 
The Petitioner submitted evidence that he won the international press association's "Sports Journalist 
of the Year" award in 2020 for his work during the COVID-19 pandemic. A letter from the 
association's general secretary states the Petitioner's selection from more than 50 journalists based on 
his outstanding reporting, innovative storytelling, ethical standards, consistency and dedication, 
impact on the field, and recognition and accomplishments. "In a year marked by the global pandemic 
and significant disruptions to the sports world," the general secretary wrote, "[the Petitioner] 
demonstrated unparalleled resilience, adaptability, and excellence in reporting." 
The letter demonstrates the Petitioner's receipt of an award for excellence in his field. The record, 
however, lacks evidence that the award has national or international recognition. The Petitioner must 
show how others outside the issuing association regard the award. See Krasniqi v. Dibbins, 
558 F.Supp.3d 168, 182 (D.N.J. 2021) (quoting Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F.Supp.3d 126, 136 (D.D.C. 
2013)) (holding that a petitioner must show "how a larger audience viewed [the] awards"). The record 
does not indicate whether Venezuelans or populations outside that country know of the award. The 
Petitioner submitted recommendation letters from others in the field. But none of the letters mention 
that he won this award. 
The Petitioner established his receipt of an award for excellence in his field. But the record does not 
establish that the award has national or international recognition. He therefore has not met this 
evidentiary requirement. 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner submitted materials comparable to evidence of the display of his work at artistic 
exhibitions or showcases. But he has not met the requested immigrant visa category's minimum 
amount of evidentiary criteria. We will therefore affirm the petition's denial.
