Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a cloud technology expert, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See 
Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
On two prior occasions, SCOPS denied the petition and then reopened it on its own motion. SCOPS has 
now denied the petition a third time, concluding that although the record established that the Petitioner 
satisfied the initial evidentiary requirements, it did not establish, as required, that the Petitioner has 
sustained national or international acclaim and is an alien in the small percentage at the very top of the 
field. The matter is now before us on appeal under 8 C.F.R. § 103.3 . 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christa 's, Inc., 26 l&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will withdraw SCOPS' decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with 
the following analysis. 
For clarity and context, below is a brief chronology of earlier stages of this proceeding. 
• May 31, 2024: The Petitioner filed the Form 1-140 petition 
• June 21 , 2024: SCOPS issued a request for evidence (RFE). 
• September 25 , 2024: SCOPS denied the petition. 
• January 22, 2025: SCOPS reopened the petition sua sponte and issued a second RFE. 
• May 15, 2025: SCOPS denied the petition for a second time. 
• December 2, 2025: SCOPS moved to reopen the proceeding, stating: "USCIS [U.S. Citizenship 
and Immigration Services] will review and reevaluate the already submitted evidence and issue a 
new decision." 
• February 2, 2026: SCOPS denied the petition for a third time, concluding that the evidence 
submitted with the petition and in response to the January 2025 RFE did not suffice to establish 
sustained national or international acclaim. 
On appeal, the Petitioner cites the regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(5)(ii), which reads, in part: "When a 
[USCIS] officer, on his or her own motion, reopens a [USCIS] proceeding ... , and the new decision may 
be unfavorable to the affected party, the officer shall give the affected party 30 days after service of the 
motion to submit a brief" The Petitioner correctly observes that the December 2025 reopening notice 
did not afford the Petitioner 30 days to submit a brief 
The record does not indicate that SCOPS afforded the Petitioner an opportunity to submit a brief after the 
December 2025 reopening notice. SCOPS subsequently issued an unfavorable decision. We will 
therefore remand the matter to SCOPS. 
ORDER: SCOPS' decision is withdrawn. The matter is remanded for the entry of a new decision 
consistent with the foregoing analysis.
