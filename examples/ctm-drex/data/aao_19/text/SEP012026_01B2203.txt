Appeal of U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) Decision 
Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an artificial intelligent staff product manager, seeks classification as an alien of 
extraordinary ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 
1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can 
demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose 
achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
USCIS denied the petition, concluding that although the Petitioner established that she satisfied the 
initial evidentiary requirements, she did not demonstrate her sustained national or international 
acclaim and that she is among the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. The matter 
is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, Inc. , 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act makes visas available to immigrants with extraordinary ability if: 
(i) the alien has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or 
athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained national or international 
acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, 
(ii) the alien seeks to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability, and 
(iii) the alien's entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively 
the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner can demonstrate sustained 
acclaim and recognition of his or her achievements in the field through a one-time achievement (that 
is, a major, internationally recognized award). If that petitioner does not submit this evidence, then 
he or she must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the ten 
categories listed at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x) (including items such as awards, published material 
in certain media, and scholarly articles). If an evidentiary standard under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)­
(x) does not "readily apply" to a petitioner's occupation, the petitioner may submit "comparable 
evidence" to establish eligibility. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USC IS, 596 F .3d 1115, 1121 (9th Cir. 2010) 
( discussing a two-part review where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the 
required number of criteria, considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also Amin 
v. Mayorkas, 24 F.4th 383, 394 (5th Cir. 2022) (upholding USCIS' two-step review process as 
"consistent with the governing statute and regulation"). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner claims eligibility for this classification as an individual of extraordinary ability in the 
field of artificial intelligence (AI) product innovation and strategy, with a focus on AI solutions for 
businesses and government agencies for cybersecurity threat intelligence, fraud detection in identity 
verification, and privacy-safe digital advertising. She earned bachelor's and master's degrees in 
engineering in Tunisia and France, respectively, and has worked in various product manager positions 
since 2016. She indicates that her career has focused on developing and launching AI platforms, 
including conversational AI tools, fraud detection systems, and machine learning features to improve 
user engagement and product performance. Since May 2023, she has worked in the United States for 
an AI platform used to aggregate news and track cyber threats. In June 2024, 
promoted the Petitioner to the position of staff product manager. The Petitioner filed this 
petition in December 2025.

USCIS determined that the Petitioner fulfilled four of the claimed criteria but concluded in a final 
merits determination that she did not establish her sustained national or international acclaim in the 
field or show that she is one ofthat small percentage at the very top ofthat field and denied the petition 
accordingly. A final merits determination involves analyzing an alien's accomplishments and 
weighing the totality of the evidence to determine if his or her successes are sufficient to demonstrate 
that they have extraordinary ability in the field of endeavor. See section 203(b)(l)(A)(i) of the Act; 8 
C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2), (3); see also Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20; see generally 6 USCIS Policy 
Manual F.2(B)(2), https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual (stating that USCIS officers should then 
evaluate the evidence together when considering the petition in its entirety to determine if the 
petitioner has established, by a preponderance of the evidence, the required high level of expertise for 
the immigrant classification). 
Because extraordinary ability is an elite level ofaccomplishment whose recognition necessarily entails 
a judgement call, it cannot be established through meeting at least three of the evidentiary criteria 
alone. See Amin v. Mayorkas, 24 F.4th at 394-395. The final merits determination is the ultimate 
statutory inquiry of whether the Petitioner has extraordinary ability, as demonstrated by a 
preponderance of the evidence, her sustained national or international acclaim, she is one of the small 
percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor, and her achievements have been recognized in the 
field through extensive documentation. 
Upon de novo review, we agree with USCIS' determination that the Petitioner has not established 
eligibility.1 
On appeal, the Petitioner cites a district court decision, Mukhe,ji v. Miller, No. 4:24-CV-3170 (D. 
Neb. Jan. 28, 2026), in which the court objected to USCIS' reliance on Kazarian to justify its use of 
the final merits determination as the basis for dismissing an appeal. We agree with the Petitioner that 
a final merits determination involves weighing the totality of the evidence to determine whether the 
record shows sustained national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the petitioner is among 
the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d at 
1121. However, we disagree with the Petitioner's reliance on this non-precedent decision. In contrast

F.4th 355, 359-60 (3d Cir. 2025) ("[W]e have cautioned over and over again that non-precedential 
opinions carry little to no weight for future cases."); Wang v. Holder, 569 F.3d 531, 538 n.5 (5th Cir.

Importantly, we note that the Mukherji decision was issued by a district court in Nebraska. In the

The final merits determination considers the totality ofthe evidence in the broader context of sustained 
national or international acclaim as required by the statute. Issues that "are not relevant to the 
antecedent procedural question of whether the petitioner has provided at least three types of evidence" 
"might be relevant to the final merits determination of whether a petitioner is at the very top of his or 
her field of endeavor." Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d at 1121. Considerations that might 
impermissibly amount to "a novel evidentiary requirement" within the narrow confines of an initial 
criterion nevertheless "might be relevant to a final merits determination." Id. The final merits 
determination hinges on whether the Petitioner has established sustained national or international 
acclaim and has reached the small percentage at the very top ofthe field. This determination is related 
to, but separate from, the antecedent question of whether the Petitioner has satisfied at least three of 
the initial criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). We agree with the Petitioner that her evidence 
should be viewed in the context ofthe entire record, rather than in isolation. But we agree with USCTS 
that this evidence does not consistently show her achievements are reflective of a "career of acclaimed 
work in the field" as contemplated by Congress. H.R. Rep. No. 101-723, 59 (Sept. 19, 1990).

most requirements or has equivalent transferable skills, and may also have relevant certifications," and

and may also have specialized ce1iifications." We note that the Petitioner's reported annual salary of 
$200,000 is less than Robert Half high-salary data of $226,800 and closer to the mid-salary data of 
$195,413. While the Petitioner provided her total remuneration, salary with bonuses and stock equity, 
the Robert Half data for her occupation and geographic region reflects salary, not other remuneration. 
The Petitioner did not show that her 2025 salary of$200,000 and the additional $14,999.92 for bonuses 
and stock equity, is a high salary or significantly high remuneration, or that her earnings are 
comparable to those of individuals at the very top of the field. The Petitioner further claims her 
compensation is validated through sustained interest from other technology companies recruiting her 
over the last two years with base salaries ranging from $200,000 to $325,000. While email 
correspondence from recruiters conveys interest in speaking with the Petitioner about employment 
opportunities in her field, the correspondence does not reflect that recruiters have specific knowledge 
of her work or otherwise reflects recognition of her work and her sustained national or international 
acclaim. Furthermore, the Petitioner has not presented multiple years of earnings showing she has 
consistently maintained a high salary or significantly high remuneration indicative of sustained 
national or international acclaim necessary for this highly restrictive visa classification. See section 
203(b)(l)(A) of the Act; see also Jafarov v. USCIS, No. 23 CrV. 3372 (PAE), 2024 WL 69056, at *9 
(S.D.N.Y. Jan. 5, 2024) (finding the failure to show consistent high wage earnings did not reflect 
sustained national or international acclaim and a single year's, or even a few years', worth of salary 
data unlikely to satisfy this requirement). 
The record as a whole, including the evidence discussed above, does not establish the Petitioner's 
eligibility for the benefit sought. The Petitioner seeks a highly restrictive visa classification, intended 
for individuals already at the top of their respective fields, rather than those progressing toward the 
top. Matter ofPrice, 20 I&N Dec. 953, 954 (Assoc. Comm'r 1994) ( concluding that even major league 
level athletes do not automatically meet the statutory standards for classification as an individual of 
"extraordinary ability,"); Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d at 131 (internal quotation marks omitted) 
(finding that the extraordinary ability designation is "extremely restrictive by design"); Amin v. 
Mayorkas, 24 F.4th at 394 (stating that extraordinary ability is such an elite level of accomplishment); 
Lapteva v. Schuurmans, No. 8:24CV83, 2025 WL 815087, at *1 (D. Neb. Mar. 10, 2025) (stating that 
EB- I visa are intended to be available to only an exceedingly small amount of individuals with 
extraordinary ability). 
Here, the Petitioner has not shown the significance of her work is indicative of the required sustained 
national or international acclaim or it is consistent with a "career of acclaimed work in the field" as 
contemplated by Congress. H.R. Rep. No. 101-723, 59 (Sept. 19, 1990); see also section 203(b)(l)(A) 
of the Act. The evidence reflects that she has received some recognition for her contributions and 
expe1iise in her field but falls short of establishing that she satisfied the requirements for this 
classification at the time of filing. While the Petitioner need not establish that there is no one more 
accomplished to qualify for the classification sought, the record is insufficient to demonstrate that she 
is among the small percentage at the top of her field. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
B. 0-1 Nonimmigrant Status 
The record reflects that in 2024 the Petitioner received 0-1 status, a classification reserved for 
nonimmigrants of extraordinary ability. Although users has approved at least one 0-1 nonimmigrant 
visa petition filed on behalf of the Petitioner, this approval does not preclude users from denying an 
immigrant visa petition which is adjudicated based on a different standard in statute, regulations, and 
case law. Many Fonn 1-140 immigrant petitions are denied after users approves nonimmigrant 
petitions. See, e.g., Sunlift Int'l v. Mayorkas, et al., 2021 WL 3111627 (N.D. Cal. 2021); Q Data 
Consulting, Inc. v. INS, 293 F. Supp. 2d 25 (D.D.C. 2003); IKEA US v. US Dept. ofJustice, 48 F. 
Supp. 2d 22 (D.D.C. 1999); Fedin Bros. Co., Ltd. v. Sava, 724 F. Supp. 1103, 1108 (E.D.N.Y. 1989), 
ajf'd, 905 F. 2d 41 (2d. Cir. 1990). Furthennore, our authority over the users service centers, the 
office adjudicating the nonimmigrant visa petition, is comparable to the relationship between a court 
ofappeals and a district court. Even if a service center has approved a nonimmigrant petition on behalf 
of an individual, we are not bound to follow that finding in the adjudication of another immigration

Nor are we required to approve applications or petitions where eligibility has not been demonstrated

In'!, 19 I&N Dec. 593, 597 (Comm'r 1988); see also Constr. & Design Co. v. Bureau ofCitizenship

III. CONCLUSION 
For the reasons discussed above, the Petitioner has not demonstrated her eligibility as an alien of 
extraordinary ability. The appeal will be dismissed for the above stated reasons.
