Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a producer, seeks classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. See section 
203(b)(l)(A) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first 
preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those aliens who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have 
been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. Id. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding the Petitioner was not the 
recipient of a major, internationally recognized award, and, while she demonstrated four of the ten 
regulatory criteria, she did not show sustained national or international acclaim and that she is among 
the small percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. The matter is now before us on appeal 
pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden of proof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, In c., 26 l&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b )(1 )(A) of the Act makes immigrant visas available to aliens with extraordinary ability if: 
they have extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been 
demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim; they seek to enter the United States to 
continue work in the area of extraordinary ability; and their entry will substantially benefit the United 
States prospectively. The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small 
percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
The implementing regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5 (h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis of the evidence 
to determine whether a petitioner has sustained national or international acclaim and whether their 
achievements have been recognized in the field. First, a petitioner can provide evidence of either "a 
major, international recognized award," or satisfaction of at least three of ten evidentiary criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration 
Services (USCIS) then considers the totality of the material provided in a final merits determination 
and assesses whether the record shows sustained national or international acclaim and demonstrates 
that the individual is among the small percentage at the very top ofthe field of endeavor. See Kazarian 
v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1121 (9th Cir. 2010) (discussing a two-step review where the documentation 
is frrst counted and then, if fulfilling the required number of criteria, considered in the context of a 
final merits determination); see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); 
Rijal v. USCJS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011); see generally 6 USCJS Policy Manual 
F.2(B), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual (providing, as guidance, USCIS' two-step extraordinary ability 
analysis). 
II. ANALYSIS 
The Petitioner has not indicated or established that she has received a major, internationally recognized 
award under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). She must therefore satisfy at least three of the regulatory criteria 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). Based on our de novo review, the Petitioner has not met this showing. 
A. Evidentiary Criteria Waived 
The Petitioner did not assert in the record below or on appeal that she meets the criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v), (vi), or (ix) regarding contributions of major significance, authorship of 
articles, and high remuneration for services. We therefore deem the Petitioner's eligibility under these 
three criteria waived. See Matter ofO-R-E-, 28 I&N Dec. 330,336 n.5 (BIA 2021) (citing Matter of 
R-A-M-, 25 I&N Dec. 657, 658 n.2 (BIA 2012))(An eligibility ground that is not raised on appeal is 
waived.) 
B. Evidentiary Criterion Established 
SCOPS determined the Petitioner met the criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv), which requires that 
she establish her participation, either individually or on a panel, as a judge of the work of others in the 
same or an allied field of specification for which classification is sought. We agree.

E. Final Merits Determination 
Congress set a very high benchmark for aliens of extraordinary ability by requiring the petitioner 
demonstrate "sustained national or international acclaim" and present "extensive documentation" to 
show his or her achievements have been recognized in the field. Section 203(b)(l)(A)(i) of the 
Act. The visa classification is highly restrictive, intended for individuals already at the top of their 
respective fields . See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2) (explaining "extraordinary ability" refers only to those 
in "that small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor"). Here, because the 
Petitioner has not satisfied the threshold "initial evidence" requirements at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), we 
cannot conclude that she is an alien of extraordinary ability and need not further analyze the merits 
under the statute. See also Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20. Therefore, we decline to reach and hereby 
reserve any final merits determination in this matter. See INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25 (1976) 
(per curiam) (holding that agencies are not required to make "purely advisory findings" on issues that 
are unnecessary to the ultimate decision). 
III. CONCLUSION 
For the reasons discussed above, the Petitioner has not demonstrated eligibility as an individual of 
extraordinary ability.
