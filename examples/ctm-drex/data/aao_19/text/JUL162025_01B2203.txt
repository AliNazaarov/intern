The Petitioner, a doctoral candidate and researcher in bioinfom1atics, seeks classification as an 
individual of extraordinary ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 
203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the record did not establish 
that the Petitioner has sustained national or international acclaim and is one of that small percentage 
who has risen to the very top of his field. The matter is now before us on appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.3 . 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
Section 203(b )(1 )(A) of the Act makes immigrant visas available to individuals with extraordinary 
ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained 
national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation, provided that the individual seeks to enter the United States to continue 
work in the area of extraordinary ability, and the individual's entry into the United States will 
substantially benefit prospectively the United States. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, petitioners can demonstrate 
international recognition of their accomplishments in the field through a one-time achievement (that 
is, a major, internationally recognized award). If petitioners do not submit this evidence, then they 
must provide sufficient qualifying documentation that meets at least three of the ten criteria listed at 8 
C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(4) allows petitioners to submit 
comparable evidence if they are able to demonstrate that the standards at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)­
(x) do not readily apply to their occupation. 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows sustained 
national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small percentage 
at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) 
( discussing a two-part review where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the 
required number of criteria, considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also 
Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 
(W.D. Wash. 2011).

A. Evidentiary Criteria 
Because the Petitioner has not shown that he received a major, internationally recognized award, he 
must satisfy at least three of the alternate regulatory criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). SCOPS 
determined the Petitioner met the three criteria relating to judging the work of others in the same field, 
original scientific contributions of major significance, and authorship of scholarly articles at 8 C.F .R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(iv)- (vi). The record supports SCOPS' determinations as evidenced by the Petitioner's 
peer-reviews of articles for scientific journals, his co-authorship of 15 articles published in scientific 
journals which, at the time of filing, had been cited a total of 2,681 times, and the fonding of some of 
his research by the National Cancer Institute (NCI) and the National Institutes of Health (NIH). 
B. Final Merits Determination 
The Petitioner has established that he meets at least three of the evidentiary criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). As the Petitioner submitted the requisite initial evidence, we will evaluate 
whether he has demonstrated, by a preponderance of the evidence, his sustained national or 
international acclaim, that he is among the small percentage at the very top of his field of endeavor, 
and that his achievements have been recognized in the field through extensive documentation. In a 
final merits determination, we analyze petitioners' accomplishments and weigh the totality of the 
evidence to determine if their successes are sufficient to demonstrate that they have extraordinary 
ability in the field of endeavor. See section 203(b )(1 )(A)(i) of the Act; 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2), (3); 
see also Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20.

did not compare him to others).

Petitioner's scientific contributions to his field, but is insufficient to show he is among the small 
percentage at the very top of his field. The Petitioner did not submit evidence to show his research 
funding and role, and publication and licensing records are comparable to scientists at the very top of 
his field. 
The record also documents the Petitioner's service as a peer-reviewer ofarticles for scientific journals. 
The Petitioner submitted evidence that he completed 16 peer reviews of articles for highly ranked 
scientific journals in his field. This evidence does not demonstrate sustained national or international 
acclaim, however, because the reviews were conducted only between 2022 and 2024 and the Petitioner 
did not submit evidence that the number of his reviews is significantly high compared to other 
scientists in his field. The Petitioner also did not submit evidence that he served on the editorial board 
of any highly ranked scientific journal, was a member of a selection committee for a distinguished 
conference, or otherwise assessed the work of other researchers in a manner consistent with being 
among the small percentage of those scientists at the very top of his field. Consequently, the 
Petitioner's peer-review service does not evidence extraordinary ability. See Bhanu v. Garland, No. 
22-103, 2023 WL 6119357, at *9 (E.D. Pa. Sept. 18, 2023) (upholding USCIS' conclusion in a final 
merits determination that a researcher did not provide evidence that set him apart from others in his 
field, such as a consistent history of completing a substantial number of review requests relative to 
others, serving in editorial positions for distinguished journals or publications, or chairing technical 
committees for reputable conferences).

the Petitioner had published 15 articles in highly ranked scientific journals that had been cited a total

The Petitioner also submitted 21 articles that cite his co-authored publications. These citations show 
that other researchers have relied upon the Petitioner's work, but they do not show he is among the 
small percentage at the very top of his field. The most the Petitioner's work is cited in one article is 
five times. Most of the articles cite the Petitioner's work after just one sentence in the introduction, a 
summary of related research, or a description of the tools and methods used. The Petitioner also 
submitted evidence that 20 other researchers in his field have lower citation counts than him. This 
evidence and the citing articles are insufficient to show his citation record places him among the small 
percentage at the very top of his field as the citing articles do not discuss his work in depth and there 
is no evidence that the 20 other researchers are highly ranked in his field. 
When considered in the aggregate, the evidence shows the Petitioner is an accomplished scientist 
whose research has been widely cited. The Petitioner's publications, peer reviews, funding, citations, 
licensing of his software, and recommendation letters show he is a well-respected researcher whose 
work has made major contributions to his field. However, the record shows the Petitioner's citation 
count and h-index are significantly lower than those at the very top of his field. In addition, articles 
citing the Petitioner's work do not discuss his research in-depth and the Petitioner's peer review 
service does not demonstrate sustained national or international acclaim. When considered as a whole, 
the record is insufficient to establish that the Petitioner is among that small percentage at the very top 
of his field. 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner seeks a highly restrictive visa classification, intended for individuals already at the top 
oftheir respective fields, rather than for individuals progressing towards the top. USCIS has long held 
that even athletes performing at the major league level do not automatically meet the "extraordinary 
ability" standard. Matter ofPrice, 20 I&N Dec. 953,954 (Assoc. Comm'r. 1994). Here, the Petitioner 
has not shown that the significance and recognition of his work are indicative of sustained national or 
international acclaim as one of that small percentage who have risen to the very top of his field, as 
extraordinary ability is described at section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act and 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The 
Petitioner is consequently ineligible for classification as an alien of extraordinary ability. 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
