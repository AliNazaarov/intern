Appeal of Service Center Operations (SCOPS) Decision 
Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a researcher specializing in cybersecurity, seeks classification as an individual of 
extraordinary ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. 
§ ll 53(b)(1 )(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who 
can demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and 
whose achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that while the record showed that 
the Petitioner met the initial evidence requirement for this classification by fulfilling at least three of 
the evidentiary criteria under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3), the record did not establish that he was among 
the very top of his field or that he possessed the requisite sustained national or international acclaim 
to demonstrate his eligibility as an individual of extraordinary ability. The matter is now before us on 
appeal pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. 
Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter of Christo 's, Inc., 26 l&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
An individual is eligible for the extraordinary ability classification if they have extraordinary ability 
in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained 
national or international acclaim and their achievements have been recognized in the field through 
extensive documentation; they seek to enter the United States to continue work in the area of 
extraordinary ability; and their entry into the United States will substantially benefit prospectively the 
United States. Section 203(b)(l)(A) of the Act. 
The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those individuals in "that small percentage who have 
risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). The implementing regulation 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a petitioner may demonstrate 
international recognition of their achievements in the field through a one-time achievement (that is, a 
major, internationally recognized award). Absent such an achievement, a petitioner must provide 
sufficient qualifying documentation demonstrating that they meet at least three ofthe ten criteria listed 
at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). 
Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider the totality of the 
material provided in a final merits determination, assessing whether the record shows that the 
individual possesses the acclaim and recognition required for this highly exclusive immigrant visa 
classification. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010) (discussing a two-part review 
where the documentation is first counted and then, if fulfilling the required number of criteria, 
considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also Visinscaia v. Beers, 4 F. Supp. 3d 
126, 131-32 (D.D.C. 2013); Rijal v. USCIS, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011). 
TI. ANALYSIS 
A. Evidentiary Criteria 
The Petitioner is a doctoral candidate and researcher in the field of cybersecurity as it relates to 
networks ofinternet-connected devices that exchange data with other devices and systems, also known 
as the "Internet of Things," or IoT. He intends to continue his work as an assistant researcher in the 
United States. 
SCOPS concluded that the Petitioner met three ofthe ten evidentiary criteria to qualify as an individual 
of extraordinary ability: 
• 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii), published material about the alien in professional or major 
trade publications or other major media, relating to the alien's work in the field for 
which classification is sought; 
• 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv), participation, either individually or on a panel, as a judge 
of the work of others in the same or an allied field of specialization for which 
classification is sought; and 
• 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vi), authorship of scholarly articles in the field, in professional 
or major trade publications or other major media. 
On appeal, the Petitioner disagrees with SCOPS' conclusion that he did not meet the following criteria: 
• 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i), receipt of lesser nationally or internationally recognized 
prizes or awards for excellence in the field of endeavor; and 
• 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v), original scientific, scholarly, artistic, athletic, or business-
related contributions of major significance in the field. 
We agree that the Petitioner met at least three of the evidentiary criteria required to establish the initial 
evidentiary requirements for classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. Because the 
Petitioner has met the initial requirements, will not separately address in our decision the evidence 
pertaining to the criteria concerning awards and original contributions to the field. Instead, we will 
consider that evidence as part of the totality of the record in a final merits determination.

B. Final Merits Determination 
As noted above, once an individual has established that they meet the initial evidence requirements, 
we conduct a final merits determination. In a final merits determination, we examine and weigh the 
totality of the evidence to determine whether an individual has sustained national or international 
acclaim and is among the small percentage at the very top of their field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. 
USCIS, 596 F.3d at 1115, 1121; see also Amin v. Mayorkas, 24 F.4th 383,394 (5th Cir. 2022). 
On appeal, the Petitioner asserts that SCOPS mischaracterized and failed to fully evaluate the evidence 
of record in reaching a final merits determination. The Petitioner reasserts his qualifications under the 
criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i),(iii), (iv), (v), and (vi) as evidence of his extraordinary ability, and 
he also points to the integrity of the journals in which his work has been published. 1 As discussed 
more fully below, the totality of the record does not establish that the Petitioner is eligible for the 
requested classification. 
Concerning whether the Petitioner has received lesser nationally or internationally recognized prizes 
or awards for excellence in his field, he initially pointed to his receipt of a fellowship from his 
university and a scholarship through an education initiative in China. The record shows that the 
fellowship is conferred annually by the university to graduate students and that six fellowships for 
$2,000 each were awarded for the 2023-to-2024 academic year. The record, however, does not include 
evidence of whether this fellowship is recognized nationally or internationally. The record shows that 
the scholarship awarded to the Petitioner is awarded to international students for a year of attendance 
at universities in China by China's Ministry of Education. However, the record does not contain 
evidence sufficient to demonstrate that the scholarship is considered a nationally or internationally 
recognized award; while the record initially included documentation describing application 
requirements for the scholarship and referencing the issuance of scholarships numbering in the 
hundreds each year, this documentation indicated that the scholarships were based on academic 
standing and plans of study, rather than applicants' demonstrations of excellence in their fields. 
On appeal, the Petitioner submits information from the websites ofseveral U.S. universities describing 
the scholarship's application process and selection criteria, which include an applicant's objective for 
participation in the program and a strong record of academic achievement. He asserts that the 
scholarship is "widely regarded as a prestigious award in China and internationally, recognized for its 
rigorous selection process and its support of exceptional academic achievements." His assertion, 
however, is not supported by evidence to demonstrate that the scholarship is awarded for a specific 
level of achievement in a particular field, nor is it supported by evidence showing that the scholarship 
is recognized nationally or internationally as an award given for excellence in a given field. While we 
recognize that receipt of a scholarship to study abroad is a notable academic achievement, it is not 
evident from the record that the Petitioner's scholarship is considered a widely recognized 
achievement attained by individuals at the very top of their fields. The Petitioner has not provided

As evidence of his original contributions of major significance to his field, the Petitioner provided 
letters ofsupport from professors and assistant professors who have served as his mentors and advisors 
or who have reviewed his work, as well as from colleagues with whom he has previously worked. The 
Petitioner's research projects are topics featured in most of the letters, and the authors emphasize the 
relevance and the originality of his innovative approach to cybersecurity techniques for use with IoT, 
such as agricultural automation equipment and self-driving vehicles. However, the authors only 
reference, in broad terms, the potential impact of his research; they do not discuss specific 
contributions the Petitioner has made to the field, such as how his research or methodologies have 
been applied to solve or preempt problems concerning cybersecurity and IoT or how his work has 
been adopted or influenced the field. While the letters' authors laude the Petitioner's work ethic, 
intellect, curiosity, and dedication, they do not cite any specific impact that the Petitioner's research 
has had in the field. The Petitioner must support his assertions with relevant, probative, and credible 
evidence. See Matter ofChawathe, 25 I&N Dec. at 376. The letters do not show that he has enjoyed 
a career of acclaimed work. See Jafarov v. United States Citizenship & Immigr. Servs., No. 23 CIV. 
33 72 (PAE), 2024 WL 69056, at *9 ( finding testimonial letters were insufficient to establish his 
relative status in the field and do not compare him to others). In addition, it is generally expected that 
one whose accomplishments have garnered sustained national or international acclaim would have 
received recognition for their accomplishments well beyond the circle of their personal and 
professional acquaintances. 2 
The Petitioner also highlights on appeal the award of a research grant of $1.2 million from a U.S. 
federal agency, and according to a letter from his advisor, the Petitioner's research was the foundation 
for the federal grant. The record shows that grant funding from another prominent source was also 
awarded based on the Petitioner's research. We acknowledge the significance of the Petitioner's role 
in securing grant funding for research projects. However, the regulatory language referencing 
contributions of major significance connotes that an individual's work has significantly impacted the 
field, and the record does not contain evidence to show how grants to continue his research show how 
his work has impacted the field. The record does not detail specific outcomes of his work 
demonstrating that he is among the small percentage at the top of his field. While the Petitioner's 
work may have led to government funding for additional research, a petitioner must provide sufficient 
context regarding evidence and considerations concerning that funding to demonstrate that the 
evidence meets the relevant criteria to establish the person's extraordinary ability in the totality of 
circumstances. This means that a petitioner must explain the significance of the submitted evidence, 
and how it demonstrates that the person has achieved sustained national or international acclaim and 
recognition in their field of expertise. 3

Further regarding the Petitioner's research, the record shows that brief stories discussing the Petitioner 
and his work have been published by three news outlets in Nigeria, that he has published an article in 
a professional journal, and that he has presented his work at two conferences. Although these 
achievements meet the plain language requirements regarding published material about an individual 
and an individual's authorship of scholarly articles,4 they do not reflect a history of academic 
recognition that one would expect to the associated with a researcher at the top of his field. For 
example, the Petitioner asserts on appeal that his research concerning cybersecurity for agricultural 
IoT "was recognized for pioneering a solution to a previously unaddressed problem in the field." The 
record, however, does not include evidence that the solution has garnered attention in the form of 
major media coverage or references in academic material, as would be expected of a solution of 
importance in theory and in practice. On appeal, the Petitioner provides the following regarding 
recognition for his research: 
While I understand the adjudicator places significant emphasis on the quantity of 
publications and citations, I respectfully argue that impact often outweighs sheer 
volume and numbers, especially in cybersecurity. Academic integrity guides my 
approach to research and publishing. I have deliberately avoided contributing to 
predatory journals or publishing for the sake of meeting numerical criteria or citations 
. . . . Instead, I have focused on producing meaningful, impactful research that 
addresses real-world problems, rather than incremental work or parameter tuning that 
lacks substantial innovation. There exist various vendors who offer citations for 
purchase just to boost citations and h-index or tell friends and students to cite their work 
which I do not subscribe to .... 
While there are undoubtedly individuals who combine quality and quantity, the 
academic community also recognizes the prevalence of publications driven by the 
"publish or perish" culture. Some researchers prioritize meeting metrics through 
superficial contributions or self-referential citations, practices that undermine the 
credibility of academic scholarship. I have chosen integrity and meaningful 
contributions over engaging in such practices, confident that the originality and 
significance of my work speak to its value. 
We acknowledge the Petitioner's point that the absence of academic citations to recently published 
work should not reflect negatively on the importance of that work, as the accrual of citations can be 
artificial. However, recognition of the work of researchers is typically demonstrated, at some point 
following the dissemination oftheir research, through the reference by others to that research; the lack 
of citations of the Petitioner's work indicates the positioning of an individual in the early stages of a 
research career, as opposed to that of more highly referenced researchers who have reached a level of 
recognition indicative of individuals of extraordinary ability at the top of their field. As with Hamal, 
both acclaim and recognition of achievements must be shown. We also recognize the Petitioner's 
point concerning the integrity of the publication that accepted his work, as opposed to the priorities of 
individuals and publications that reinforce certain cultural norms recognized by the academic 
community that may not accurately reflect the actual usage of the work cited. However, like the low 
citations rates of some researchers early in their careers, while the Petitioner's publication history

Similarly, while the Petitioner's service as a judge at several events of note in his field meets the plain 
language requirement of the criterion, 5 participation in the peer review process does not automatically 
demonstrate that an individual has sustained national or international acclaim at the very top of their 
field. Although the record includes an assessment of one of the Petitioner's reviews that gives it a top 
rating, the record does not demonstrate how his reviews generally differentiate him from the field, 
such as evidence showing how his work compares in quantity and quality to that of his peers. Without 
evidence that sets him apart from others in his field, such as evidence that he has a consistent history 
ofreviewing or judging acclaimed experts in his field, evidence that he has served in editorial positions 
for distinguished journals or publications, or other sufficient evidence, the Petitioner has not 
established that his peer review experience places him among that small percentage at the very top of 
the field of endeavor. 
While the evidence presented may demonstrate that the Petitioner has a successful academic record as 
a researcher who has attracted grant fonding for projects in which he has served a prominent role, 
taken together with his brief history judging the work of others and the limited records of publication 
and recognition for his work, the evidence does not demonstrate by a preponderance of the evidence 
that the Petitioner has garnered a level of recognition for expertise in his field that would be expected 
from an individual at the top of his field. Although letters of support show that the Petitioner has been 
a valued researcher recognized by his peers for his original approaches and potential solutions to issues 
within the field of cybersecurity, it is not evident from the record that the Petitioner's work has 
involved achievements at a level that would render him an individual who is considered extraordinary 
in his field and who has sustained national or international acclaim. 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner seeks a highly restrictive visa classification, intended for individuals already at the top 
of their respective fields, rather than for those progressing toward the top. USCIS has long held that 
even athletes performing at the major league level do not automatically meet the "extraordinary 
ability" standard. Matter ofPrice, 20 I&N Dec. 953,954 (Assoc. Comm'r 1994). Here, the Petitioner 
has not shown that the significance of his work is indicative of the required sustained national or 
international acclaim or that it is consistent with a "career of acclaimed work in the field" as 
contemplated by Congress. H.R. Rep. No. 101-723, 59 (Sept. 19, 1990); see also section 203(b )(l)(A) 
of the Act. The record does not demonstrate that the Petitioner has garnered sustained national or 
international acclaim in the field and that he is one of the small percentage who have risen to the very 
top ofhis field. See section 203(b)(l)(A) ofthe Act and 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). We therefore conclude 
that the Petitioner has not established his eligibility as an individual of extraordinary ability. 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
