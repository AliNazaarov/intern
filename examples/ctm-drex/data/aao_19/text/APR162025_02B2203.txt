Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an actor, seeks classification as an individual of extraordinary ability. See Immigration 
and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference 
classification makes immigrant visas available to those who can demonstrate their extraordinary 
ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements have been 
recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
Service Center Operations (SCOPS) denied the petition, concluding that the Petitioner did not 
establish that the Petitioner had satisfied at least three of the ten initial evidentiary criteria for this 
classification, as required. We dismissed a subsequent appeal. The matter is now before us on 
combined motions to reopen and reconsider. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance ofthe evidence. 
Matter of Chawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). Upon review, we will dismiss the 
motions. 
A. Motion to Reconsider 
A motion to reconsider must establish that our prior decision was based on an incorrect application of 
law or policy and that the decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the record of proceedings 
at the time of the decision. 8 C.F.R. § 103 .5(a)(3). We may grant motions that satisfy these 
requirements and demonstrate eligibility for the requested benefit. 
On motion, the Petitioner contends that our decision was incorrect based on the evidence in the record 
at the time of our prior decision, and that we "dismiss[ ed] the evidence as insufficient without any 
substantive explanation." 
In our dismissal notice, we concluded that the Petitioner had not established that she meets the 
evidentiary criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) relating to awards (i), memberships (ii), published 
material (iii), original contributions (v), and high salary (ix). Because our consideration of those five 
criteria was sufficient to determine the outcome of the appeal, we reserved two further criteria relating 
to display of work (vii) and leading or critical role (viii). We will discuss the relevant criteria below. 
Statements in a brief, motion, or Notice of Appeal are not evidence and thus are not entitled to any 
evidentiary weight. Matter ofS-M-, 22 I&N Dec. 49, 51 (BIA 1998). Allegations oferror must be specific 
and supported by the record. As we will discuss below, the Petitioner has not met this burden on motion.

The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ii) calls for documentation of the alien's membership in 
associations in the field for which classification is sought, which require outstanding achievements of 
their members, as judged by recognized national or international experts in their disciplines or fields. 
In our appellate decision, we agreed with SCOPS' determination that the Petitioner had not established

On motion, the Petitioner states "the union's constitution ... does not preclude the Union from

The Petitioner has not established that we erred in our prior determination regarding the criterion.

3. Published Material 
The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii) calls for published material about the alien in professional 
or major trade publications or other major media, relating to the alien's work in the field for which 
classification is sought. The evidence must include the title, date, and author of the material, and any 
necessary translation. 
The Petitioner had claimed to meet this criterion through interviews published in A lash Ainasy in 2014 
and Qazaq Adebieti in 2022. We concluded that the Petitioner had not shown that the named 
newspapers meet the regulatory requirements. We previously acknowledged circulation figures that 
the Petitioner had provided for these publications, but determined that the Petitioner had not 
established that these figures established the newspapers as major media. Raw circulation figures, in 
isolation, lack context, and therefore we consider evidence of "relative circulation." See generally 
6 USCIS Policy Manual, supra, at F.2(B)(l). 
On motion, the Petitioner asserts that she had previously "provided comprehensive documentation 
demonstrating that the publications ... are well-established, widely circulated, and recognized as 
major media outlets within Kazakhstan." The Petitioner repeats assertions she had previously made 
in response to a request for evidence, and asserts that we erred by not taking into account the 
publications' "established credibility, high circulation, and national influence." But the burden is on 
the Petitioner to submit evidence to support these assertions. General claims that the Petitioner had 
previously met its burden of proof do not establish proper cause for reopening. 
The Petitioner has not established that we erred in our prior determination regarding the criterion. 
4. Original Contributions 
The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(v) calls for evidence of the alien's original scientific, 
scholarly, artistic, athletic, or business-related contributions of major significance in the field. 
Detailed letters from experts in the field explaining the nature and significance of the person's 
contribution may also provide valuable context for evaluating the claimed original contributions of 
major significance. Such letters have more weight when the record includes documentation 
corroborating the claimed significance. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual, supra, at F.2(B)(l). 
Submitted letters should specifically describe the contribution and its significance to the field. Id. 
In our appellate decision, we quoted from "recommendation letters from colleagues within the Kazakh 
acting community ... [that] are complimentary of the Petitioner's talents and ability," but we 
concluded that "beyond explaining [the Petitioner's] talents and attributes, the submitted letters 
provide little detail as to how her contributions to the field were original or how they had major 
significance within the field." 
On motion, the Petitioner refers to some of the previously submitted letters and asserts that they 
contain significantly more detail than "general praise." The Petitioner makes several assertions about 
the content of the letters but does not provide quotations to support those assertions.

5. High Salary 
The regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix) calls for evidence that the alien has commanded a high 
salary or other significantly high remuneration for services in relation to others in the field. 
In our appellate decision, we acknowledged the Petitioner's submission of salary certificates showing 
her remuneration from 2020 to 2023. We concluded that the Petitioner had not provided any basis for 
comparison to show that her salary was high in relation to others in the field as the regulation requires. 
On motion, the Petitioner contends: "Attachment 19 in the Initial Petition contained official salary 
records that explicitly documented her earnings in comparison to industry standards, establishing her 
exceptional financial remuneration relative to her peers" and "her high financial standing compared to 
other theater actors in Kazakhstan." Attachment 19 comprises the "salary certificates" that we 
acknowledged in our appellate decision. These certificates show the amounts paid to the Petitioner, 
but they do not show the "industry standards" or average salaries of actors in Kazakhstan. Therefore, 
the salary documents provide no basis for comparison between the Petitioner and others in her field to 
show that she earned a high salary or significantly high remuneration in relation to others in the field. 
The Petitioner asserts that the salary documents "are further corroborated by letters from industry 
professionals and employers, affirming her exceptional earning power in the field." The Petitioner 
does not specify which letters contain this information, nor does the Petitioner quote from those letters. 
The general assertion that the record contains unspecified supporting evidence is not sufficient to 
establish proper cause for reconsideration. A motion does not entail de novo review of the entire 
record, and therefore it is not incumbent upon us to search the record for materials that might match 
the general description the Petitioner offers on motion. As noted above, the Petitioner has made a 
number of claims about specific letters or other materials in the record which those documents do not 
corroborate or support. 
The Petitioner has not established that we erred in our prior determination regarding the criterion. 
In our appellate decision, we reserved discussion of two criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(vii) and 
(viii), relating, respectively, to display at artistic exhibitions or showcases and leading or critical roles 
for distinguished organizations or establishments. We reserved these issues because, given our other 
conclusions, there was no way for the Petitioner to satisfy at least three of the eligibility criteria. 
Therefore, detailed discussion of the remaining two criteria could not have changed the outcome of 
our appellate decision. 
On motion, the Petitioner contends that our "failure to address the Petitioner's eligibility under [the 
reserved criteria] is a clear error in adjudication," because "[t]he regulatory framework requires a 
comprehensive analysis of all criteria presented." 
6 
All claimed criteria received due consideration in the initial adjudication ofthe petition, and the denial 
decision addressed those criteria. The adjudication of an appeal is not the same as the adjudication of 
the underlying petition. In our decision, we cited applicable case law indicating that, when discussion 
of a particular issue is not necessary to determine the outcome of an appeal, we may reserve that issue. 
Specifically, we cited INS v. Bagamasbad, 429 U.S. 24, 25-26 (1976), in which the Supreme Court 
acknowledged: "As a general rule courts and agencies are not required to make findings on issues the 
decision of which is unnecessary to the results they reach." We also cited Matter ofD-L-S-, 28 I&N 
Dec. 568, 576-77 n.10 (BIA 2022), which cited INS v. Bagamasbad in support of the principle that, 
once we have made determinations that are sufficient to decide the outcome of an appeal, we need not 
address additional issues that would not change that outcome. 
On motion, the Petitioner does not address this cited case law, and the Petitioner does not establish 
that the outcome of the appeal would have been different if we had addressed the two criteria relating 
to display and leading or critical roles. As discussed above, we do not conclude that the Petitioner has 
overcome any of our prior determinations regarding the five criteria that we did address. 
The Petitioner has not established that our previous decision was based on an incorrect application of 
law or policy at the time we issued our decision. As such, the Petitioner has not shown proper cause 
for reconsideration. We will dismiss the motion to reconsider. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
B. Motion to Reopen 
A motion to reopen must state new facts and be supported by documentary evidence. 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.5(a)(2). We may grant motions that satisfy these requirements and demonstrate eligibility for 
the requested benefit. See Matter ofCoelho, 20 I&N Dec. 464, 473 (BIA 1992) (requiring that new 
evidence have the potential to change the outcome). 
On motion, the Petitioner submits web printouts concerning her claimed receipt of prizes and awards, 
and about newspapers that published articles about her. The Petitioner asserts that these new facts 
establish that she satisfied the criteria as 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i) and (iii). 
The Petitioner submits what she describes as "additional articles confirming the Petitioner's award 
[from a Turkish theater festival] is internationally recognized." The materials are printouts from the 
websites of the Turkish state of Konya, which hosted the festival, and of the youth theater that won 
the award. These materials do not establish wider recognition of the award beyond the entities that 
awarded and received it. 
A printout from the website of the Kazakh American Center is not new to the record, because the 
Petitioner had submitted similar materials in response to a request for evidence. 
The Petitioner also submitted web printouts from the publishers of the two Kazakh newspapers 
discussed farther above. A printout from the publisher of Alash Ainasy provides the newspaper's 
circulation figures, considered previously, but adds no relevant new information to the record. A 
translated printout of the "About the newspaper" page from Qazaq Adebieti' s website describes the 
7 
newspaper's history but provides no new information to establish that the newspaper qualifies as a 
professional or major trade publication or other major media. 
While most of the documents submitted on motion are new to the record, they introduce no new 
information to establish the Petitioner's eligibility for the highly restrictive immigrant classification 
that she seeks. Therefore, we will dismiss the motion to reopen. 8 C.F.R. § 103.5(a)(4). 
ORDER: The motion to reopen is dismissed. 
FURTHER ORDER: The motion to reconsider is dismissed.
