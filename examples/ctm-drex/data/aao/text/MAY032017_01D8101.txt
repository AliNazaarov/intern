PETITION: FORM I-129, PETITION FOR A NONIMMIGRANT WORKER 
The Petitioner, a retail nutrition business, seeks to classify the Beneficiary as a foreign national of 
extraordinary ability in the arts to perform services as a fashion and fitness model. See Immigration 
and Nationality Act (the Act) section 101(a)(15)(0)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(l5)(0)(i). This 0-1 
classification makes nonimmigrant visas available to foreign nationals who can demonstrate their 
extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and whose achievements 
have been recognized in the field through extensive documentation. 
' 
The Director of the Vermont Service Center denied the petition, concluding that the submitted 
evidence did not satisfy the applicable evidentiary requirements. 
Upon de novo review, we will dismiss the appeal. 
On February 13, 201 7, and March 21, 2017, we sent a notice of intent to dismiss the appeal based on 
derogatory information, and afforded the Petitioner an opportunity to provide rebuttal evidence. 1 
Specifically, we noted that the Petitioner claims to be a limited liability company organized under 
the laws of the State of Florida. Pursuant to 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(16)(i), we notified the Petitioner 
that, according to the records of the Florida Department of State, Division of Corporations, the 
Petitioner's corporate status is inactive with an "Administrative Dissolution for Annual Report" 
dated September 23, 2016.2

We also informed the Petitioner that, if it is currently dissolved, this fact is material to its eligibility 
for the requested visa.3 Specifically, a company that has been dissolved by the state cannot qualify 
as a U.S. employer for purposes of employing a nonimmigrant worker. See section 214(c)(l) of the 
Act; see also 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(2)(i). 
We provided the Petitioner 30 days in which to provide evidence to rebut the finding that it has been 
dissolved. To date, more than 30 days have passed since our March 21, 2017, notice, and the 
Petitioner has not responded with proof that it remains in operation as a viable business. As the 
Petitioner was dissolved, it cannot qualify as a U.S. employer for purposes of employing the 
Beneficiary. Accordingly, we will dismiss the appeal. 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
