Appeal of U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) Decision 
Form I-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, an executive in the medical nutnt10n field, requests classification under the 
employment-based, first-preference (EB-1) immigrant visa category as an alien with "extraordinary 
ability." See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S.C. 
§ 1153(b )(1 )(A). Successful petitioners for U.S. permanent residence in this category must 
demonstrate "sustained national or international acclaim" and extensively document recognition of 
their achievements in their fields. Section 203(b )( 1 )(A)(i) of the Act. 
U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) denied the petition. The Acting Associate 
Director of Service Center Operations concluded that the Petitioner met two of ten initial evidentiary 
requirements - one less than needed for a final merits determination. On appeal, the Petitioner 
contends that she satisfied an additional requirement by submitting evidence of high salary or 
significantly high remuneration for her services in relation to others in her field. See 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(ix). 
The Petitioner bears the burden of demonstrating eligibility for the requested benefit by a 
preponderance of the evidence. Matter of Chawathe, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). 
Exercising de novo appellate review, see Matter of Christa 's, Inc., 26 I&N Dec. 537,537 n.2 (AAO 
2015), we conclude that she has satisfied the required number of evidentiary criteria. But, because 
she has not established acclaim or recognition sufficient to place her among the small percentage at 
her field's top, we will dismiss the appeal. 
I. LAW 
To qualify for the requested immigrant visa category, a petitioner must demonstrate that they: 
• Have "extraordinary ability" in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics; 
• Seek to continue work in their field of expertise in the United States; and 
• Would substantially benefit the country as a permanent resident. 
Section 203(b)(l)(A)(i-iii) of the Act. 
The term "extraordinary ability" means expertise commensurate with "one of that small percentage 
who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(2). 
Unless a petitioner received "a major, international recognized award," they must satisfy at least three 
of ten initial evidentiary criteria. 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3). 1 If a petitioner meets either evidentiary 
standard and the requirements at sections 203(b )(1 )(A)(ii), (iii) of the Act, USCIS must then make a 
final merits determination. To merit final approval, the record - as a whole - must establish a 
petitioner's receipt of "sustained national or international acclaim" and recognized achievements 
placing them among the small percentage at their field's very top. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 
1115, 1119-20 (9th Cir. 2010) (describing a two-step, adjudicatory process); see generally 6 USCIS 
Policy Manual F.2(B), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual. 
TI. ANALYSIS 
A. The Petitioner and Her Field 
The record shows that universities in the Petitioner's native country of China awarded her a bachelor 
of science degree in microbial pharmaceutics and a master of business administration degree. She has 
worked in the Chinese pharmaceutical and medical nutrition industries for about 35 years. 
For the past 16 years, the Petitioner has served as a vice president of market access and director of 
government affairs at a medical nutrition company that makes specialized liquid nutrients delivered 
directly into patients' stomachs and small intestines. In these closely related roles, she has primary 
responsibility for market access work, which involves securing regulatory approval, payer coverage, 
and favorable insurance reimbursements for products. She also manages product pricing and 
maintaining products on reimbursement lists. She stated: 
I build the evidence and pathways for payers and hospitals to adopt new therapies, 
including health economics and outcomes research and real-world evidence to support 
coverage decisions. In practice, this has meant translating scientific nutrition and 
medicines into reimbursable, scalable clinical use. 
The Petitioner stated that, if allowed to immigrate to the United States, she plans to form a business 
here. Using $500,000 that she previously invested in an unsuccessful immigrant investor petition 
under section 203(b )(5) ofthe Act, she said she would establish a business that would provide "market­
access strategy and execution services that help U.S. biopharmaceutical, medical-nutrition, and 
medical-technology firms enter China and other high-growth emerging markets." She stated: "By 
pairing U.S. scientific leadership with practical market-access execution, [her proposed business] will 
contribute to U.S. exports and the broader life-sciences economy while operating to the highest 
standards of compliance and transparency."

B. The Evidentiary Criteria 
The record does not indicate - nor does the Petitioner claim - her receipt of a major internationally 
recognized award. She must therefore meet at least three of the ten initial evidentiary criteria at 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i-x). 
The record supports the Director's findings that the Petitioner successfully submitted evidence of: 
• Published materials about her and her work in her field; and 
• Her performance in a leading or critical role for organizations with distinguished reputations. 
See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii), (viii). 
We will now review the Petitioner's claim that she also submitted evidence of her commandment of a 
high salary or other significantly high remuneration for her services. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(ix). 
"[T]he regulations regarding this preference classification are extremely restrictive." Kazarian, 
596 F.3d at 1120 (citation omitted). The Petitioner's evidence must objectively meet the parameters 
of the applicable regulatory description. See generally 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(8). "[N]either 
users nor [the] AAO may unilaterally impose novel substantive or evidentiary requirements beyond 
those set forth at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5." Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1121 (citation omitted). 
The criterion that the Petitioner claims to have met requires "[ e ]vidence that the alien has commanded 
a high salary or other significantly high remuneration for services, in relation to others in the field." 
8 C.F.R. § 204.5(3)(ix). 
The Petitioner submitted documentary evidence from her current employer in China. The materials 
indicate that the employer paid her annual salary amounts of: 2,137,485.23 CNY (Chinese yuan) in 
2022; 2,331,574.90 CNY in 2023; and 2,484,467.29 CNY in 2024. She also provided copies of salary 
surveys for China and the United States. 
Under USCrS policy: 
Officers evaluate persons working outside of the United States based on the wage 
statistics or comparable evidence relevant to the applicable work location, rather than 
by simply converting the salary to U.S. dollars and then viewing whether that salary 
would be considered high in the United States. 
6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(8)(1), Criterion 9. Thus, we will disregard the salary surveys for the 
United States. 
The initially submitted salary survey for China, dated in 2025, indicates that the average "manager 
market access" with at least 18 years of experience earned 343,736 CNY a year, or about $55,500. 
See XE, "Exchange Rate and Currency Converter Tool," www.xe.com/en-nz/currencyconverter. As 
the record indicates that the Petitioner's 2024 salary is almost seven times higher that amount, she 
argues that she has submitted evidence of a high salary in relation to others in her field. 
3 
The Petitioner, however, has not demonstrated that the occupation described in the salary survey 
relates to her job. The survey' s second page contains a "job description" of a manager market access. 
The description states the job's duties as "[r]esearch conditions in local, regional, national, or online 
markets. Gather information to determine potential sales of a product or service, or plan a marketing 
or advertising campaign." Thus, the salary survey describes a market research analyst or marketing 
specialist position. See U.S. Bureau of Lab. Stats., "Occupational Employment & Wage Statistics 
Profiles," https://data.bls.gov/oesprofile (stating that market research analysts and marketing 
specialists "[ r ]esearch conditions in local, regional, national, or online markets. Gather information 
to determine potential sales of a product or service, or plan a marketing or advertising campaign"). 
The record does not indicate that the Petitioner's job involves marketing or sales, and she has not 
explained how the described job relates to hers. Thus, the salary survey does not sufficiently 
correspond to the Petitioner's job and therefore does not demonstrate her claimed high salary in her 
field. See Xia v. Miller, No. 3:20-CV-01468-JR, 2021 WL 5817324, at *7 (D. Or. Nov. 18, 2021) 
("Inherent in plaintiff's burden 'to provide appropriate evidence' of a higher salary is the need to 
explain how the information [s]he supplies provides a relevant, appropriate, and fair comparison."); 
see generally 6 USCJS Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), Criterion 9 (stating that, when comparing a 
petitioner's documented remuneration with compensation listed in a survey, the occupation's 
description may be a relevant consideration). 
In response to the Director's request for additional evidence (RFE), the Petitioner submitted additional 
salary surveys for China that better correspond to her job. For example, a 2023 survey states that 
"deputy general director[s] or above" in the "market access" sections of "government affairs" 
departments oflarge life sciences companies in China made from 1,000,000 CNY to 2,000,000 CNY 
a year. 2 The record shows that, in 2023, the Petitioner received an annual salary of2,331,574.90 CNY, 
above the compensation range listed in the survey. Her evidence therefore indicates that she received 
a high salary in relation to others in her field. Also, a 2025 salary survey reports that directors of 
government affairs in the Chinese pharmaceutical and biotechnology industries earned from 800,000 
CNY to 1,800,000 CNY a year. With this compensation range, too, the record shows that the 
Petitioner's salary exceeded the range. 
The Petitioner submitted evidence that she has commanded a high salary in relation to others in her 
field. We will therefore withdraw the Director's contrary finding. 
The Petitioner has met three evidentiary criteria. USCIS must therefore make a final merits 
determination on her petition. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3); Kazarian, 596 F.3d at 1119-20. As the 
Director did not make such a finding, we will make one now. 
C. Final Merits Determination 
To establish final eligibility, the Petitioner must demonstrate that she has received sustained national 
or international acclaim and recognition of her achievements in her field sufficient to show that she is 
one of that small percentage who has risen to the field's very top. See generally 6 USCIS Policy 
Manual F.2(B)(2). Congress intended classification as an alien with extraordinary ability to reflect a 
"career of acclaimed work in the field." See H.R. Rep. No. 101-723, at 59 (Sept. 19, 1990).

Under USCTS policy: 
[t]he petitioner must provide sufficient context ... to demonstrate that the evidence 
meets the relevant criteria and to establish the person's extraordinary ability in the 
totality of the circumstances. This means that the petitioner must explain the 
significance of the submitted evidence, and how it demonstrates that the person has 
achieved sustained national or international acclaim and recognition in their field of

l. Published Materials About the Petitioner 
The Petitioner submitted copies of five published articles about her and her work. Two of the articles 
merely quote or mention her. But three of them significantly focus on her and her job. In her RFE 
response, she stated that "the mainstream media coverage of [her] . . . demonstrates her sustained 
national acclaim in the industry." 
We agree that the published materials about the Petitioner garnered her some national acclaim and 
recognition. But, contrary to her statement, the record does not indicate that the articles appeared in 
"mainstream media." Rather, the record indicates that professional publications in the health field 
published the articles. Also, the articles were published between 2022 and 2025. They therefore do 
not reflect "sustained national ... acclaim," see section 203(b )(1 )(A)(i) of the Act ( emphasis added), 
or a "career of acclaimed work in the field." See H.R. Rep. No. 101-723, at 59 ( emphasis added). 
Further, the Petitioner has not established that three published articles about a person and their work 
reflect a "career of acclaimed work in the field." See Hamal v. US. Dep 't ofHomeland Sec., No. CV 
19-2534 (RC), 2021 WL 2338316, at *4 (D.D.C. June 8, 2021), aff'd sub nom. Hamal v. United States 
Dep 't ofHomeland Sec., No. 21-5160, 2023 WL 1156801 (D.C. Cir. Jan. 31, 2023) (affirming our 
finding that a petitioner did not show that "his press coverage is indicative of a level of success 
consistent with being" at the top ofhis field). Thus, the published materials about the Petitioner, alone, 
do not demonstrate her claimed extraordinary ability in her field. 
2. Performance in a Leading or Critical Role 
The Petitioner provided evidence that she has played leading and critical roles for her current 
employer, including advocating to get an enteral nutritional product on China's list of essential 
medicines for the first time and obtaining broader reimbursement of medical nutrition products. She 
also contends that, before joining her current employer, she held leading and critical roles for 
pharmaceutical companies. She stated: "These leadership roles at prominent institutions with 
distinguished reputations demonstrate [her] sustained national acclaim in the field." 
We agree that the Petitioner's leading and critical roles have garnered her some national acclaim and 
recognition. She demonstrated that she has represented her current employer before government 
officials, signed off on research and development projects for her employer, and attended fora and 
5 
conferences on the company's behalf But the Petitioner has not demonstrated that the acclaim and 
recognition she received places her among the small percentage at her field's very top. The record 
lacks independent evidence explaining her place within her field as a whole. See Joseph v. Dir. of 
Texas Serv. Ctr., USCIS, No. 24-40249, 2025 WL 458001, at *4 (5th Cir. Feb. 11, 2025) (affirming 
USCIS' finding that the petitioner did not demonstrate that "the impact of his work has risen to the 
level where it places him at the very top of the field;" see also 6 USCIS Policy Manual F.2(B)(2) ("In 
all cases, the petitioner must provide sufficient context ... to establish the person's extraordinary 
ability in the totality of the circumstances.") 
The Petitioner provided recommendation letters from others in her field, including a former hospital 
pharmaceutical director, a university director of clinical nutrition, and a former official of China's 
food and drug administration. The letters praise the Petitioner's expertise in policy research and 
development and her professionalism and integrity in advocating for reimbursement of medical 
nutrition. But the letters do not place her achievements in context within her field. The letters 
therefore do not demonstrate her claimed positioning among the small percentage at the field's top. 
See 6 USCJS Policy Manual F.2(B)(2) ("In all cases, the petitioner must provide sufficient context ... 
to establish the person's extraordinary ability in the totality of the circumstances.") 
Also, the recommendation letters indicate that their authors have worked professionally with the 
Petitioner. "It is generally expected that one whose accomplishments have garnered sustained national 
or international acclaim would have received recognition for their accomplishments well beyond the 
circle of their personal and professional acquaintances." Mishra v. Richardson, No. 
120CV991LMBTCB, 2021 WL 1566071, at *3 (E.D. Va. Apr. 20, 2021); 6 USCIS Policy Manual 
F.2(B)(3). 
3. Commandment of a High Salary 
The Petitioner's high salary reflects recognition of her achievements in her field. But we do not find 
her compensation, alone, sufficient to demonstrate her claimed extraordinary ability. See Hamal, 
2023 WL 1156801 at *2 ("[T]he Department well explained its conclusion that salary evidence alone 
was insufficient to establish Hamal as a singularly exceptional artist due to the other gaps in his 
evidence.") 
In sum, the record identifies the Petitioner as a talented and knowledgeable executive in the 
pharmaceutical and medical nutrition fields. She met the requested category's initial evidentiary 
requirements and demonstrated her receipt of some national acclaim and recognition in her field. But, 
even considering the evidence in the aggregate, the Petitioner has not established herself among the 
small percentage at her field's top. 
Congress intended to set "a very high standard" for aliens of extraordinary ability, reserving this 
immigrant visa category "for that small percentage of aliens who have risen to the very top of their 
field of endeavor." Proposed Rule for Employment-Based Immigrants, 56 Fed. Reg. 30703, 30704 
(July 5, 1991 ). Considering the evidence as a whole, the Petitioner has not established her claimed 
extraordinary ability in her field. See Amin v. Mayorkas, 24 F.4th 383, 386 ("[G]iven the lofty bar for 
extraordinary ability classifications, we cannot say that the agency acted arbitrarily when it determined 
that [a petitioner] was not 'extraordinary' but merely very good."); Matter ofPrice, 20 I&N Dec. 953, 
6 
954 (Assoc. Comm'r 1994) (holding that even athletes performing in their sports' premier leagues do 
not automatically meet the extraordinary ability standards). 
III. CONCLUSION 
The Petitioner satisfied the requested category's initial evidentiary criteria. But she has not 
demonstrated her claimed place among the small percentage at her field's very top. We will therefore 
affirm the petition's denial. 
ORDER: The appeal is dismissed.
