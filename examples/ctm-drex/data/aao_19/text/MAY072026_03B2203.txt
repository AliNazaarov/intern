Form 1-140, Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers (Extraordinary Ability) 
The Petitioner, a business development executive and entrepreneur, seeks classification as an alien of 
extraordinary ability. See Immigration and Nationality Act (the Act) section 203(b)(l)(A), 8 U.S .C. 
§ 1153(b)(l)(A). This first preference classification makes immigrant visas available to those who 
can demonstrate their extraordinary ability through sustained national or international acclaim and 
whose achievements have been recognized in their field through extensive documentation. 
SCOPS denied the petition, concluding that the record did not establish that the Petitioner had either 
received a major, internationally recognized award or satisfied at least three of ten initial evidentiary 
criteria for this classification, as required. The matter is now before us on appeal under 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.3. 
The Petitioner bears the burden ofproof to demonstrate eligibility by a preponderance ofthe evidence. 
Matter afChawathe, 25 l&N Dec. 369, 375-76 (AAO 2010). We review the questions in this matter 
de novo. Matter a/Christa's, Inc. , 26 I&N Dec. 537, 537 n.2 (AAO 2015). Upon de novo review, 
we will withdraw the decision and remand the matter for entry of a new decision consistent with the 
following analysis. 
I. LAW 
To qualify under this immigrant classification, a petitioner must demonstrate: 
• They have extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which 
has been demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim; 
• They seek to enter the country to continue working in the area of extraordinary ability; and 
• Their entry will offer substantial prospective benefits to the United States. 
Section 203(b)(1 )(A)(i)-(iii) of the Act. The term "extraordinary ability" refers only to those 
individuals in "that small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor." 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(2). 
The implementing regulation at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3) sets forth a multi-part analysis. First, a 
petitioner can demonstrate international recognition of an alien's achievements in the field through a 
one-time achievement in the form of a major, internationally recognized award. Alternatively, a 
petitioner can submit evidence that meets at least three of the ten criteria listed at 8 C.F.R. 
§ 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). Where a petitioner meets these initial evidence requirements, we then consider 
the totality ofthe material provided in a final merits determination and assess whether the record shows 
sustained national or international acclaim and demonstrates that the individual is among the small 
percentage at the very top of the field of endeavor. See Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115, 1121 (9th 
Cir. 2010) ( discussing a two-part review where the documentation is first counted and then, iffulfilling 
the required number of criteria, considered in the context of a final merits determination); see also 
Amin v. Mayorkas, 24 F.4th 383, 394 (5th Cir. 2022) (upholding USCIS' two-step review process as 
"consistent with the governing statute and regulation").

A. Evidentiary Criteria 
Because the Petitioner has not indicated or shown that he received a major, internationally recognized 
award, he must satisfy at least three ofthe alternate regulatory criteria at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(i)-(x). 
The Petitioner claimed to have satisfied five of these criteria, summarized below: 
• (iii), Published material about the alien in professional or major media; 
• (iv), Participation as a judge of the work of others; 
• (vi), Authorship of scholarly articles; 
• (viii), Leading or critical role for distinguished organizations or establishments; and 
• (ix), High remuneration for services. 
SCOPS concluded that the Petitioner met two of the five claimed criteria based on evidence that he had 
participated as a judge for business competitions and commanded a high salary or other significantly 
high remuneration for his services relative to others. See 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iv) and (ix). 
Upon review of the record, we conclude that the Petitioner has also submitted evidence that satisfies 
the criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)(iii), which requires "[p]ublished material about the alien in 
professional or major trade publications or other major media, relating to the alien's work in the field 
for which classification is sought." These materials must include "the title, date, and author of the 
material, and any necessary translation." Id. Professional or major trade publications may be 
identified by their intended audience, while other major media and major trade publications should 
have elevated circulation or readership relative to other media in the field. See generally 6 USCIS 
Policy Manual F.2(B)(l), www.uscis.gov/policy-manual.

B. Derogatory information not disclosed to Petitioner 
SCOPS' determination that the Petitioner did not establish his eligibility under the criterion at 8 C.F.R. 
204.5(h)(3)(viii) was based, in part, on information relating to his employment history that was 
obtained from nonimmigrant visa applications he had submitted to the U.S. Department of State. 
However, SCOPS did not inform the Petitioner that it would rely on this information from outside the 
record of proceeding in the adjudication of his petition. 
If a decision is unfavorable to a petitioner and is based on derogatory information considered by 
USCIS and of which the petitioner is unaware, the petitioner must be advised of this fact and offered 
an opportunity to rebut the information and present information in his or her own behalf before the 
decision is rendered. See 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b )(16)(i). A determination of eligibility shall be based 
only on information contained in the record of proceeding which is disclosed to the petitioner. See 8 
C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(l6)(ii). 
We are remanding this matter to SCOPS for entry of a new decision for the reasons discussed below. 
If the new decision will be based, in part, on any information from outside the record, SCOPS must 
issue a new request for evidence or notice of intent to deny in compliance with 8 C.F.R. 
§ 103.2(b)(l6)(i) prior to its issuance.

alien of extraordinary ability. The next step is to undertake a final merits determination to analyze

classification. Accordingly, we will remand the matter to SCOPS for a final merits determination.
